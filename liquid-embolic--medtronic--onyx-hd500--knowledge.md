# Onyx HD-500

**Manufacturer:** Medtronic (Micro Therapeutics, Inc.)
**Category:** Liquid embolic agent (high-density formulation)
**Delivery catheter:** DMSO-compatible microcatheter (Marathon, Rebar, or UltraFlow HPC)

> **Source limitation:** The PDF filed as `liquid-embolic--medtronic--onyx-hd500--ifu.pdf` in the device catalog contains the Cordis ENTERPRISE Vascular Reconstruction Device summary, not Onyx HD-500 instructions for use. This entry is written from the Onyx LES IFU (which describes the shared platform) and known HD-500 labeling characteristics. A corrected HD-500 IFU should be sourced for verification.

## What It Is

High-viscosity formulation of the Onyx liquid embolic platform, designed specifically for endovascular treatment of intracranial aneurysms rather than AVMs. Like standard Onyx, it is composed of EVOH copolymer dissolved in DMSO with suspended micronized tantalum powder. HD-500 has a substantially higher EVOH concentration (20%) compared to Onyx 18 (6%) or Onyx 34 (8%), producing a viscosity of approximately 500 cSt. This high viscosity causes the material to remain cohesive within an aneurysm sac rather than penetrating distally through small vasculature.

HD-500 was approved under a Humanitarian Device Exemption (HDE) for a narrower indication than Onyx LES. The mechanism is the same: DMSO dissipates on contact with blood, causing EVOH and tantalum to precipitate into a spongy, non-adhesive embolus. The higher viscosity prevents the material from migrating through the aneurysm dome or into downstream vessels.

## Sizing

| Formulation | EVOH Concentration | Nominal Viscosity | Primary Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onyx HD-500 | 20% EVOH | ~500 cSt | Intracranial aneurysm sac filling |
| Onyx 34 (for comparison) | 8% EVOH | 33 cSt | bAVM high-flow fistulous components |
| Onyx 18 (for comparison) | 6% EVOH | 18 cSt | bAVM nidal penetration |

**Decision rule:** HD-500 is not interchangeable with Onyx 18/34. Use HD-500 only for aneurysm sac filling. Use Onyx 18/34 only for bAVM embolization.

## Indications

- Endovascular embolization of intracranial aneurysms (HDE indication)
- Specifically intended for aneurysms that are not amenable to surgical clipping or standard endovascular coiling
- Approved under Humanitarian Device Exemption, meaning annual use is limited to fewer than 8,000 patients in the US

## Contraindications

- Optimal catheter placement is not achievable
- Provocative testing indicates intolerance to the occlusion procedure
- Vasospasm stops blood flow
- High-flow AV fistulae within the aneurysm (material may be washed out)

## Relevant Protocol

- Same DMSO handling and catheter compatibility requirements as Onyx LES
- Use only DMSO-compatible microcatheters (Marathon, Rebar, UltraFlow HPC) and MTI syringes
- Standard neurointerventional anticoagulation per institutional protocol
- Heparinized saline drip through guide catheter
- Mixing on Onyx mixer (setting 8, minimum 20 minutes) is required before use, same as Onyx 18/34

## Deployment/Use Step-by-Step

1. **Mix Onyx HD-500:** Place vial on Onyx mixer at setting 8 for at least 20 minutes. Continue mixing until ready to inject. Inadequate mixing causes tantalum settling and poor visualization.
2. **Confirm catheter position:** Position microcatheter tip within or at the neck of the aneurysm per institutional protocol. Confirm with contrast injection.
3. **Flush catheter:** Flush contrast from microcatheter with saline. Leave syringe connected.
4. **Fill catheter deadspace with DMSO:** Aspirate DMSO into the yellow MTI 1 mL syringe. Inject into microcatheter to fill the deadspace volume.
5. **Fill Onyx syringe:** Fill white MTI 1 mL syringe with HD-500 through 18-20 gauge needle. Remove DMSO syringe from hub, hold hub vertically, wash luer hub with remaining DMSO.
6. **Connect Onyx syringe:** Immediately connect to hub with no air. Point syringe vertically. Any contact with saline, blood, or contrast causes premature solidification.
7. **Begin injection:** Inject slowly under fluoroscopic guidance. Due to the high viscosity, injection rates will be slower than Onyx 18/34. Monitor fluoroscopically for any migration toward parent vessel.
8. **Fill aneurysm sac:** Continue controlled injection to achieve target fill of the aneurysm. The high viscosity keeps material cohesive within the sac.
9. **Terminate and remove catheter:** Wait a few seconds after injection, slightly aspirate syringe, gently pull catheter.

## Bailout / Troubleshooting

**Material migrating into parent vessel?**
- STOP injection immediately. The high viscosity of HD-500 makes this less likely than with Onyx 18/34, but it remains possible especially with wide-necked aneurysms. Balloon-assisted technique may be needed.

**Catheter stuck?**
- Same retrieval protocol as Onyx LES: carefully pull to assess resistance, remove slack, apply 3-4 cm of gentle traction, hold and release, repeat. Wrist snap technique over 10-15 cm if standard traction fails. Do not exceed 20 cm traction.

**Can HD-500 be used in combination with coils?**
- Yes, coil packing followed by HD-500 fill has been described. Coils provide scaffold; HD-500 fills interstitial spaces.

**What if injection is interrupted >2 minutes?**
- Do not resume. Solidification at catheter tip may have occurred. Attempting to clear risks catheter rupture.

## Complications to Know

Specific HD-500 trial data is limited due to HDE status (small patient population). Expected complications are similar to those seen with Onyx LES and endovascular aneurysm treatment generally:

- Thromboembolic events (stroke, TIA)
- Intracranial hemorrhage
- Catheter entrapment
- Vasospasm
- Parent vessel occlusion
- Material migration
- DMSO-related effects (garlic taste, histamine release, interstitial cystitis reported rarely)
- Death

## Effectiveness

Limited published data due to HDE pathway. The device is intended as a niche option for aneurysms not treatable by clipping or coiling. Published case series report variable occlusion rates depending on aneurysm morphology.

## MRI Safety

Not explicitly addressed in available labeling. Contains tantalum (same as Onyx LES), which creates CT and MRI artifact. Consult institutional MRI safety protocols before scanning.

## Also Known As

Onyx HD-500, Onyx HD, high-density Onyx, Onyx for aneurysms
