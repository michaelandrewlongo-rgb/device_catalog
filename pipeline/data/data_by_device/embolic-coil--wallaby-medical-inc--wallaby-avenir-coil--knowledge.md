# Wallaby Avenir Coil System

**Manufacturer:** Wallaby Medical, Inc.
**Category:** Embolic Coil

## What It Is

Platinum-tungsten detachable embolization coils for endovascular occlusion of intracranial aneurysms and neurovascular malformations. The system consists of an implant coil affixed to a delivery pusher for insertion through a microcatheter. Coils are available in complex (3D) and helical secondary shapes spanning framing, filling, and finishing roles. Sequential coil deployment creates blood stasis within the aneurysm sac and progressively thromboses the aneurysm until occlusion is achieved. Detachment is mechanical.

The Avenir system is manufactured by Wallaby Medical (Laguna Hills, CA) and cleared under 510(k) K173711, using the Axium Detachable Coil System (ev3/Medtronic, K162704) as the primary predicate.

## Indications

Intended for:
- Endovascular embolization of intracranial aneurysms
- Neurovascular abnormalities including arteriovenous malformations and arteriovenous fistulae
- Arterial and venous embolization in the peripheral vasculature

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **Material composition**: Wallaby Avenir uses a 92/8 platinum-tungsten alloy, while Medtronic's Axium family uses bare platinum (Axium) or platinum/tungsten (Axium Prime), and MicroVention's HydroCoil uses hydrogel-coated platinum-tungsten alloy for expansion.
- **Detachment mechanism**: Wallaby Avenir uses mechanical detachment, similar to Medtronic's Axium systems (via Instant Detacher) but different from Cerenovus systems (hydraulic detachment), MicroVention's HydroCoil (thermal via V-Grip), and Kaneka's i-ED COIL (electrolytic via EDG v4).
- **Coil length range**: Wallaby Avenir offers 1-60 cm coil lengths, providing a broader maximum length than Medtronic's Axium (1-50 cm) and MicroVention's HydroCoil (2-43 cm).
- **Delivery pusher working length**: Wallaby Avenir has a 180 cm pusher, shorter than MicroVention's HydroCoil (195 cm pusher); Medtronic's Axium pusher length is not specified.
- **Compatibility notes**: Unlike Medtronic's Axium systems which require a separately sold Instant Detacher, Wallaby Avenir's detachment mechanism is integrated into the system; compatibility with specific microcatheters is not specified for Wallaby Avenir while Medtronic specifies minimum ID requirements.
