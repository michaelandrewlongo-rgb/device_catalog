# Pipeline Flex Embolization Device

**Manufacturer:** Medtronic (ev3 Neurovascular)
**Category:** Flow diverter
**Delivery catheter:** Marksman (0.027" ID, minimum 135 cm length)

## What It Is

Braided, multi-alloy mesh cylinder (platinum/tungsten + cobalt-chromium-nickel) that sits across the aneurysm neck in the parent artery. Provides ~30% metal coverage of the arterial wall surface area. Diverts flow away from the aneurysm sac, promoting thrombosis and endothelialization over time.

The implant sits on a 304 stainless steel micro-guidewire inside an introducer sheath (~200 cm total length). The delivery wire has a fluorosafe marker no further than 125 cm from the distal end.

## Sizing

**Rule: Match the labeled diameter to the largest target vessel diameter.**

The device foreshortens 50-60% during deployment. Choose a length at least 6 mm longer than the aneurysm neck to ensure adequate landing zones on both sides.

If there's a mismatch between proximal and distal parent vessel diameter, **size to the larger (proximal) vessel** for proper wall apposition and anchoring.

## Indications

- Adults >= 22 years old
- Large or giant wide-necked intracranial aneurysms in the ICA (petrous to superior hypophyseal)
- Also indicated for small and medium wide-necked aneurysms (neck >= 4 mm OR dome-to-neck ratio < 2) OR saccular/fusiform aneurysms, arising from parent vessel 2.0-5.0 mm, in the ICA up to the terminus

## Contraindications

- Active bacterial infection
- Not on dual antiplatelet therapy (ASA + clopidogrel) -- absolute contraindication
- Have not received DAPT prior to procedure
- Pre-existing stent at target aneurysm location
- Parent vessel outside 2.0-5.0 mm range

## Anticoagulation Protocol

From the PREMIER trial:
- **Pre-procedure:** Aspirin 325 mg daily for minimum 7 days + Clopidogrel 75 mg daily for minimum 7 days prior
- **Intra-procedure:** Heparinized saline drip to continuously flush microcatheter
- **Post-procedure:** Aspirin 325 mg daily for minimum 6 months + Clopidogrel 75 mg daily for minimum 3 months (then 75 mg daily for 3 more months)

## Deployment Step-by-Step

1. **Position microcatheter:** Advance Marksman at least 20 mm past the distal edge of the aneurysm. Gently retract to reduce slack.
2. **Prep the device:** Remove packaging hoop, pull blue clip from introducer sheath. Insert introducer sheath into the RHV at the catheter hub. Flush with heparinized saline at 250 mmHg pressure. Confirm the sheath tip is seated in the hub.
3. **Lock and advance:** Secure introducer sheath to hub by locking down the RHV. Advance proximal end of delivery wire until it aligns with the proximal end of introducer sheath.
4. **Remove introducer sheath.** The fluorosafe marker is only compatible with microcatheters >= 135 cm.
5. **Advance to tip:** Push the delivery wire through the microcatheter until the delivery wire tip aligns with the microcatheter tip. Place the distal end of the PED at least 3 mm past the distal edge of the aneurysm.
6. **Deploy:** Unsheathe the device by a combination of advancing the microcatheter and pushing the delivery wire simultaneously. The device foreshortens 50-60% as it opens.
7. **Monitor the resheathing marker** during deployment. The device can be resheathed (pulled back into the catheter) until the resheathing marker has reached the distal marker of the microcatheter. Maximum 2 full resheathing cycles -- more than 2 may damage the braid ends.
8. **After full expansion:** Lock the delivery wire to the microcatheter by locking down and/or manipulating together as a system to facilitate expansion.
9. **Detach:** Once fully deployed, advance the microcatheter tip distal to the PED, making sure not to dislodge the braid. Retract the delivery wire into the microcatheter tip.
10. **Inspect under fluoro:** Confirm the device is fully apposed to vessel wall, not kinked. If incomplete apposition, consider balloon catheter, guidewire, or a second PED to open it.

## Bailout / Troubleshooting

**Excessive friction during delivery:**
- Stop. Remove the device AND microcatheter simultaneously. Do not force against resistance -- this damages the braid or vessel.

**Tortuous anatomy causing load buildup:**
- Unload the microcatheter by pulling back on the system (catheter + wire together)
- Continue unloading until the device passes through the tortuous area and delivery force decreases
- Re-advance the delivery wire while maintaining reduced load in the microcatheter

**Device expanded at wrong location:**
- If the resheathing marker has NOT reached the microcatheter distal marker, resheath the device by advancing the microcatheter while pulling the delivery wire
- If fully deployed: cannot be removed. Deploy a second PED if needed.

**Can I telescope a second PED inside the first?**
- Yes. After placing the first, advance the microcatheter over the delivery wire through the deployed PED. Position at the desired location and deploy normally. Select an appropriate new size for the second device.
- Caution: Multiple PEDs increase ischemic complication risk.

**Which diameter if proximal and distal vessel sizes differ?**
- Size to the larger (typically proximal) vessel for anchoring.

## Complications to Know

From PUFS-PAS (N=134, 5-year follow-up):
- Cerebral hemorrhage: 4.5%
- Cerebral ischemia: 2.2%
- Ischemic stroke: 1.5%
- All neurological events at 5 years: 20.1%

From PREMIER (N=141, 1-year follow-up):
- Major stroke at 1 year: 2.17% (mITT population)
- Composite disabling stroke or neurological death at 1 year: 1.4%
- Headache at 180 days: 29.1%
- Visual impairment at 180 days: 10.6%
- Vasospasm: 19.9%

**Key safety signal:** Use in patients >= 60 years may result in decreased effectiveness and additional safety risks. The C7 (communicating segment) ICA has specific anatomical concerns including PComm overlap, fetal origin PCA, and retrograde filling that warrant careful patient selection.

**Hypertension post-PED is associated with increased risk of ipsilateral stroke or neurovascular death.**

## Effectiveness

- Complete aneurysm occlusion at 180 days: 76.6% (PUFS-PAS)
- Complete aneurysm occlusion at 5 years: 93.8% (PUFS-PAS)
- Complete aneurysm occlusion at 1 year: 77.6% (PREMIER, ICA population)
- Mean 3.1 PEDs placed per subject (range 1-15)
- Most common sizes used: 3.75-4.25 mm diameter, 14-18 mm length

## MRI Safety

MR Conditional at 3T:
- Static field <= 3 Tesla
- Spatial gradient <= 720 Gauss/cm
- SAR <= 4.0 W/kg for 15 minutes
- Temperature rise < 0.6C in non-clinical testing
- MRI may degrade accuracy of MR angiogram for assessing luminal patency due to susceptibility artifact
