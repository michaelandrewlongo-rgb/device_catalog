# SOFIA Distal Access Catheter (MicroVention/Terumo)

**Manufacturer:** MicroVention, Inc. (Terumo)
**Category:** Distal access / aspiration catheter
**Materials:** No natural rubber latex, PVC, or DEHP
**Source:** https://www.terumoneuro.com/products/sofia

## What It Is

Non-tapered, single-lumen, flexible catheter with coil and braid reinforcement designed for intracranial distal access and aspiration thrombectomy. The distal segment is steam-shapeable to facilitate vessel selection. A hydrophilic coating covers the distal shaft for trackability. A radiopaque marker at the distal tip provides fluoroscopic visualization. The catheter serves dual purposes: (1) intermediate catheter / distal access platform for delivering microcatheters and devices, and (2) direct aspiration catheter for mechanical thrombectomy.

Packaged with an Introducer Sheath and a Shaping Mandrel. The Introducer Sheath is removed after femoral insertion and is not intended to remain in the body.

## Indications

- General intravascular use, including neuro and peripheral vasculature
- Facilitate introduction of diagnostic or therapeutic agents
- Removal/aspiration of emboli and thrombi from selected blood vessels in the arterial system, including peripheral and neuro vasculatures
- Not intended for use in coronary arteries

## Contraindications

No known contraindications per the IFU.

## Relevant Protocol

**Anticoagulation:** Administer appropriate anti-coagulation and anti-platelet therapy per standard medical practice.

**Catheter maintenance:** Maintain continuous perfusion of heparinized saline through the inner lumen at all times to prevent thrombus formation. If removed from the patient, hydrate the hydrophilic coating with heparinized saline and do not allow it to dry.

**Contrast restriction:** Do not use Ethiodol, Lipiodol, or other oil-based contrast media. Do not use organic solvents (will damage the catheter).

**Aspiration limits:** Maximum 3 aspiration attempts, each up to 2 minutes duration, per the IFU.

## Deployment/Use Step-by-Step

**Preparation:**

1. Carefully remove the SOFIA Catheter and Introducer Sheath from the package.
2. Inspect the catheter for any damage. Do not use if damage or irregularities are observed.
3. **Steam shaping (optional):** Bend the Shaping Mandrel to the desired shape (do not exceed 90 degrees). Insert the mandrel into the distal tip. Hold the distal segment with the mandrel under steam for 30 seconds. Immediately place in heparinized saline to set the shape. Inspect the distal shaft for damage. Remove the mandrel. Do not steam the same device more than once.
4. Flush the lumen with heparinized saline. Attach a rotating hemostatic valve (RHV) to the proximal hub. Set up a perfusion line through the sidearm of the RHV.
5. Hydrate the hydrophilic coating with heparinized saline. Keep the coating hydrated at all times.

**Delivery (non-intracranial):**

6. Prepare a 0.035" or 0.038" guidewire. Insert into the SOFIA catheter and advance until aligned at the distal end.
7. Using the Introducer Sheath, insert the catheter and guidewire through the femoral sheath hemostatic valve.
8. Remove the Introducer Sheath once the distal shaft is inside the patient body. The Introducer Sheath must not remain in the patient.
9. Under fluoroscopic guidance, advance or withdraw the SOFIA Catheter over the guidewire to the desired position. Select vessels by slowly torquing as needed.

**Delivery (intracranial):**

10. Prepare a microcatheter and compatible guidewire for navigation. Remove any previously inserted devices and insert the microcatheter with guidewire into the SOFIA catheter.
11. Under fluoroscopic guidance, advance or withdraw the SOFIA over the microcatheter and guidewire to the desired intracranial position. Select vessels by slowly torquing.
12. Slowly remove the guidewire or microcatheter if necessary. Maintain continuous heparinized saline perfusion.

**Aspiration with Syringe:**

13. Under fluoroscopy, confirm the distal tip is in a straight portion of the vessel (not at a bend).
14. Ensure the distal tip is engaged with the emboli or thrombi under fluoroscopy.
15. Remove the perfusion line from the RHV sidearm. Attach a stopcock with 30 cc or 60 cc locking syringe to the sidearm.
16. Tighten the RHV. With the stopcock closed, pull and lock the plunger.
17. Open the stopcock to begin aspiration.
18. Confirm aspiration of blood/emboli/thrombi through the system. If no aspiration or slow aspiration is observed, close the stopcock and investigate. If material is stuck, remove the entire device (catheter, microcatheter, guidewire) and clear the lumen.
19. With the emboli or thrombi fully engaged with the distal tip, pull the SOFIA Catheter out of the patient body.

**Aspiration with Pump:**

20. Under fluoroscopy, confirm the distal tip is in a straight portion of the vessel.
21. Confirm distal tip engagement with the emboli/thrombi under fluoroscopy.
22. Connect aspiration tubing to the pump. Confirm the gauge reads -20 inHg. Ensure the stopcock is closed.
23. Remove the perfusion line from the RHV sidearm and attach the aspiration tubing.
24. Tighten the RHV. Confirm engagement under fluoroscopy.
25. Open the aspiration tubing stopcock to begin aspiration.
26. If blood is still flowing after 10 seconds without clot retrieval, stop aspiration, reposition the distal tip to engage the thrombus, and resume.
27. If flow is restricted or absent, maintain aspiration, confirm engagement, and slowly pull back the SOFIA Catheter to withdraw out of the patient with the thrombus.
28. Once outside the body, flush the catheter to clear thromboembolic material.

## Bailout / Troubleshooting

**Aspiration is slow or absent despite tip engagement?**
- Close the stopcock. Reposition the distal tip under fluoroscopy. Re-engage the thrombus and resume aspiration. If material is stuck in the lumen, remove the entire device from the body and clear it ex vivo.

**Excessive resistance to advancement or withdrawal?**
- STOP. Do not advance or withdraw until the cause is determined. Tortuosity, calcification, or other devices may be causing the resistance. Forcing may damage the catheter or vessel.

**Catheter is kinked?**
- Withdraw the entire device (catheter, microcatheter, and guidewire). A severely kinked SOFIA may result in separation of the device. Do not continue using a kinked catheter.

**Can I clear a clogged lumen by infusing while the catheter is in the patient?**
- NO. Do not attempt to clear the inner lumen by infusion while the device is in the patient body. Remove the catheter first, then flush and clean ex vivo.

**Tip is against the vessel wall during aspiration?**
- Excessive aspiration against the vessel wall may cause vessel injury. Verify tip position under fluoroscopy: the tip should be in a straight portion of the vessel, not at a bend or against the wall.

**Can I reuse the device for a second pass?**
- Yes, up to 3 aspiration attempts total, each up to 2 minutes. After each pass, flush and clean the inner lumen by infusion outside the body. Inspect for damage. Reintroduce and navigate to target per delivery steps.

## Complications to Know

Per IFU, potential complications include:
- Vessel or aneurysm perforation
- Vasospasm
- Hematoma at access site
- Embolism (air, thrombus, device fragment)
- Ischemia
- Intracerebral/intracranial hemorrhage
- Pseudo aneurysm
- Seizure
- Stroke
- Infection
- Vessel dissection
- Thrombus formation
- Death

No device-specific clinical trial complication rates are provided in the IFU. Complication rates for aspiration thrombectomy using large-bore catheters are available from published stroke trials (ADAPT technique, ASTER, COMPASS).

## Effectiveness

No device-specific efficacy data in the IFU. Published literature supports the SOFIA catheter for direct aspiration thrombectomy in acute ischemic stroke and as a distal access platform for stent retriever deployment.

## MRI Safety

Not explicitly addressed in the IFU. The catheter is a temporary-use device removed after the procedure and is not an implant. No post-procedure MRI restrictions apply.
