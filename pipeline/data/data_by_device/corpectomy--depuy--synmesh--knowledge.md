# SYNMESH Vertebral Body Replacement (DePuy Synthes)

## What It Is

The SYNMESH is a cylindrical titanium mesh cage used for vertebral body replacement (corpectomy reconstruction). It is a hollow, open-ended titanium cylinder with a diamond-shaped mesh pattern that can be cut to length intraoperatively and packed with autograft bone to promote fusion. The mesh design allows bone ingrowth through the walls. SYNMESH is used after partial or complete vertebral body resection in the thoracic and lumbar spine, providing immediate structural support while the graft incorporates. It is supplemented with posterior or anterior fixation.

## Sizing/Specs

### Implant Sizes
SYNMESH cages are available in multiple outer diameters. The cylindrical cage is supplied in longer stock lengths and cut to the needed height intraoperatively using the SYNMESH Cutter.

**Available Outer Diameters:**
- Multiple diameter options to match vertebral body endplate size
- Exact diameter options are selected based on the vertebral body anatomy

### Implant Material
- Titanium mesh cylinder
- Diamond-shaped mesh pattern for bone ingrowth
- Open-ended design for graft packing from both ends

## Indications

- Vertebral body replacement after corpectomy
- Thoracolumbar burst fractures with vertebral body comminution
- Vertebral body tumors requiring resection
- Vertebral body infections (osteomyelitis) after debridement
- Failed previous vertebral body reconstruction
- Must be used with supplemental fixation (anterior plate or posterior instrumentation)

## Contraindications

- Active systemic or local infection at the surgical site (unless the corpectomy is performed for debridement of vertebral osteomyelitis, in which case the cage is placed after thorough debridement)
- Patient anatomy that cannot accommodate available cage diameters
- Severe osteoporosis where endplates cannot support a rigid cylindrical cage (consider expandable alternatives)
- Patients unable to comply with postoperative activity restrictions and bracing requirements
- Known titanium allergy or hypersensitivity
- Morbid obesity or conditions that preclude adequate surgical exposure
- Inadequate posterior or anterior fixation (the cage must not be used as a standalone device)

## Compatible With

- **Supplemental fixation (required):**
  - DePuy Synthes anterior plating systems (e.g., VECTRA, ATLANTIS)
  - DePuy Synthes posterior pedicle screw systems (e.g., EXPEDIUM, VIPER)
  - Other manufacturer anterior plates or posterior fixation systems (the cage itself is not system-specific)
- **Graft materials:**
  - Autograft bone (morselized cancellous bone from corpectomy site, iliac crest, or rib)
  - Allograft bone (cancellous chips or demineralized bone matrix as supplement or alternative)
  - Bone graft substitutes may be packed alongside autograft; refer to specific product labeling
- **Instruments:**
  - SYNMESH Implant Holders (396.388 small tips, 396.389 large tips)
  - SYNMESH Cutter (397.091)
  - Standard vertebral body spreaders/distractors
- **Imaging:** Intraoperative fluoroscopy (C-arm) for position verification; compatible with navigation if used with supplemental fixation that supports navigated placement

## Use Notes

- The cage is supplied in stock lengths and must be cut to the corpectomy defect height intraoperatively using the dedicated SYNMESH Cutter
- Cut 1-2mm longer than the measured defect to achieve press-fit under mild distraction
- Pack the cage densely with morselized autograft before insertion; loosely packed graft compromises fusion rates
- Preserve the cortical rim of the endplates to prevent subsidence; the mesh cage concentrates load on a smaller footprint than the full endplate
- Position centrally on the endplates; eccentric placement increases subsidence and migration risk
- Do not impact aggressively during insertion; the mesh can deform under excessive force
- Use controlled, steady pressure during seating
- Supplemental fixation is mandatory; the cage alone does not provide rotational or translational stability
- In osteoporotic patients, consider larger-diameter cages or expandable alternatives due to higher subsidence risk
- The cylindrical design is simple but does not conform to lordotic endplate anatomy; for cervical cases requiring lordosis, consider alternatives with built-in angulation

- **Bone graft:** Autograft (iliac crest, local bone from corpectomy, rib), allograft, or bone graft extenders (e.g., demineralized bone matrix). BMP use is off-label in this context

## Use Notes

- SYNMESH is a non-expandable cage. The surgeon must measure the defect under distraction and cut the cage to length before insertion. This contrasts with expandable cages that are inserted compressed and expanded in situ.
- The cylindrical mesh design distributes load across the endplate contact area but has a smaller footprint than rectangular VBR cages. Central positioning on the endplate is critical to minimize subsidence risk.
- Cut length should be 1-2 mm longer than the measured defect to achieve a press-fit under distraction. Under-sizing leads to cage migration.
- Dense graft packing is essential. The open mesh allows bone ingrowth through the cage walls, but the interior chamber is the primary fusion site.
- In osteoporotic bone, the mesh cage is prone to subsidence. Consider expandable cages with larger endplate footprints or augment with cement-enhanced screws posteriorly.
- The cage can be used in the thoracic and lumbar spine. It is not indicated for cervical corpectomy (where expandable cages or strut grafts are more commonly used due to the smaller vertebral body size).
- Supplemental fixation is mandatory. Without anterior plating or posterior instrumentation, the cage provides no rotational or translational stability.

## Relevant Setup

- **Supplemental fixation required.** SYNMESH is not a standalone device. Anterior plating or posterior pedicle screw fixation must be used.
- **Graft material:** Autograft bone (local bone from corpectomy, iliac crest, or rib) is packed into and around the cage.
- **Imaging:** Intraoperative fluoroscopy to confirm cage position and alignment.
- **Sizing:** Measure the corpectomy defect height and vertebral endplate diameter to select the appropriate cage diameter and cut length. The cage should be cut slightly longer (1-2mm) than the defect to allow press-fit seating under mild distraction.

## Use Step-by-Step

### Corpectomy and Defect Preparation
1. Complete the corpectomy, removing the diseased/fractured vertebral body while preserving the adjacent endplates above and below.
2. Prepare the endplates: remove cartilaginous endplate material to expose bleeding subchondral bone for graft incorporation, but preserve the cortical rim to prevent cage subsidence.
3. Measure the defect height (distance between prepared endplates under gentle distraction) and endplate anteroposterior/lateral dimensions.

### Cage Preparation
4. Select the appropriate diameter SYNMESH cage to match the endplate size.
5. Using the SYNMESH Cutter (397.091), cut the cage to the measured defect height. Cut slightly longer (1-2mm) than the measured defect to ensure press-fit.
6. Pack the cage with autograft bone (morselized cancellous bone from the corpectomy site, iliac crest, or rib). Pack firmly to maximize graft volume within the mesh.

### Cage Insertion
7. Distract the corpectomy defect using a vertebral body spreader or distractor to create space for cage insertion.
8. Grasp the packed cage with the SYNMESH Implant Holder (396.388 for small tips or 396.389 for large tips).
9. Insert the cage into the corpectomy defect, positioning it centrally on the endplates.
10. Slowly release distraction to allow the endplates to compress onto the cage. Confirm press-fit seating.
11. Pack additional bone graft around the cage as needed.

### Fixation
12. Apply supplemental fixation (anterior plate or posterior pedicle screw construct) to stabilize the reconstruction.
13. Confirm final position with intraoperative fluoroscopy in AP and lateral views. Verify cage is centered, appropriate height is restored, and there is no encroachment on the spinal canal.

## Pearls / Troubleshooting

- **Preserve the cortical rim of the endplates** to prevent cage subsidence. The mesh cage concentrates load on a smaller footprint than the full endplate; the cortical rim provides the strongest bone for support.
- **Cut slightly long (1-2mm).** Under-sizing leads to cage migration or settling. A mild press-fit under distraction is ideal.
- **Pack graft densely.** The mesh allows bone ingrowth, but the cage interior is the primary fusion chamber. Loosely packed graft compromises fusion rates.
- **Do not impact aggressively.** The mesh can deform if hammered too hard during insertion. Use controlled, steady pressure.
- **Position centrally on the endplates.** Eccentric placement increases the risk of subsidence and cage migration.
- **Use the correct Implant Holder tip size** (small vs. large) for the cage diameter selected. Mismatched tips can slip during insertion.
- **The SYNMESH Cutter is the only tool for cutting the cage.** Do not use wire cutters or other instruments, which can leave sharp edges or deform the mesh pattern.
- **Supplemental fixation is mandatory.** The cage alone does not provide rotational or translational stability.
- **Consider endplate quality.** In osteoporotic patients, the cage may subside even with a press-fit. Consider larger-diameter cages or expandable alternatives if endplate quality is poor.
