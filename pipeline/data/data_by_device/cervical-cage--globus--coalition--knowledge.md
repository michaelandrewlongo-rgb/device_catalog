# COALITION Anterior Cervical Cage (Globus Medical)

**Manufacturer:** Globus Medical
**Category:** Cervical Cage

## What It Is

The COALITION is a 3D-printed porous titanium interbody cage for anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF). The cage features a porous titanium structure engineered for bone through-growth and biological fixation. Its key differentiator is an integrated fixation option: screws can be placed through the cage body into the vertebral bodies above and below, potentially eliminating the need for a separate anterior cervical plate in single-level constructs.

Key design features:
- 3D-printed porous titanium architecture mimicking trabecular bone structure
- Integrated screw fixation ports for anterior-to-posterior screw placement through the cage
- Open graft window for bone packing
- Serrated/textured endplate contact surfaces for initial stability
- Implanted via standard open anterior cervical approach

The integrated fixation design provides a zero-profile or near-zero-profile construct when used without a supplemental plate, reducing anterior hardware bulk and associated dysphagia risk.

## Indications

- Anterior cervical interbody fusion (ACDF) at one or two contiguous levels from C2 to T1
- Degenerative disc disease confirmed by history and radiographic studies
- Cervical radiculopathy or myelopathy from disc herniation or spondylosis
- May be used with integrated fixation screws (without supplemental plate) in single-level cases per surgeon judgment
- Multi-level cases may require supplemental anterior plate fixation
- Must be used with autogenous and/or allogenic bone graft
- Patients must be skeletally mature

## Contraindications

- Active infection at the operative site
- Severe osteoporosis or inadequate bone stock (risk of screw pullout and subsidence)
- Known allergy to titanium or other implant materials
- Conditions precluding benefit of spinal implant surgery
- Previously fused levels at the target segment
- Skeletal immaturity
- Mental or neuromuscular disorders creating unacceptable risk of fixation failure or noncompliance

## Compatible With

- **Integrated fixation:** Dedicated screws placed through the cage body into adjacent vertebral bodies. This is the primary fixation mode for single-level cases.
- **Supplemental anterior plate (optional for single-level, may be needed for multi-level):** Any FDA-cleared anterior cervical plate system. Do not mix metals (use titanium plates with this titanium cage).
- **Bone graft:** Autogenous cancellous bone, allograft, or bone graft substitutes packed into the graft window
- **Cervical retractor systems:** Standard anterior cervical retractor setups
- **Imaging:** Intraoperative fluoroscopy (AP and lateral). Note: titanium construction produces more artifact on CT and MRI compared to PEEK cages, limiting radiographic fusion assessment.
- **Dedicated instrument set:** Globus Medical COALITION instrument tray including trials, inserter, drill guides, and screw driver

## Use Notes

- The integrated fixation option is the primary selling point. In single-level ACDF, screws through the cage can eliminate the need for a separate anterior plate, reducing surgical steps, operative time, and anterior hardware profile.
- For multi-level constructs, supplemental anterior plating may still be required for adequate stability. Surgeon judgment based on bone quality and construct demands.
- Porous titanium architecture is designed to allow bone through-growth into the cage structure, rather than relying solely on bone graft within the window. This may improve long-term biological fixation.
- Titanium construction is stiffer than PEEK. This provides robust initial stability but may increase stress shielding. Monitor for subsidence, particularly in osteoporotic patients.
- Titanium produces more artifact on CT and MRI compared to PEEK cages. Fusion assessment relies more on plain radiographs and clinical assessment than on cross-sectional imaging.
- Preserve subchondral endplate bone during disc space preparation to minimize subsidence risk.
- Pack graft material into the open window before insertion.
- The 3D-printed porous surface should not be scratched or damaged during handling. Surface defects compromise bone ingrowth potential.
- Counsel smokers on increased nonunion risk.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **vs. Zero-P (DePuy):** Both offer integrated fixation that can eliminate the anterior plate. Zero-P uses a PEEK spacer with a titanium plate/screw component; COALITION uses a fully titanium 3D-printed porous body with screws through the cage. Zero-P provides better imaging (PEEK radiolucency); COALITION offers bone through-growth via porous architecture. Both achieve zero-profile or near-zero-profile constructs.
- **vs. Tritanium C (Stryker):** Both are titanium cages with porous surfaces for osseointegration. The Tritanium C always requires a supplemental anterior plate in the US. COALITION's integrated fixation option can eliminate the plate in single-level cases. Both produce similar imaging artifact. COALITION has a workflow advantage from fewer implant components.
- **vs. CAPSTONE (Medtronic):** CAPSTONE is PEEK (radiolucent, artifact-free imaging); COALITION is porous titanium (bone through-growth, more artifact). CAPSTONE requires a supplemental plate; COALITION can function without one. CAPSTONE offers superior postoperative imaging for fusion assessment. COALITION offers potential for biological fixation through its porous structure.
- **Integrated fixation vs. plate-dependent:** Cages with integrated fixation (COALITION, Zero-P) simplify the surgical construct and reduce anterior hardware profile. Plate-dependent cages (Tritanium C, CAPSTONE) offer the flexibility of established anterior plate systems with variable-angle screws and longer constructs. The tradeoff is simplicity and low profile vs. construct versatility.
