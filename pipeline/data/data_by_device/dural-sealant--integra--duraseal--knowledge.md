# DuraSeal Dural Sealant System

**Manufacturer:** Integra LifeSciences (Confluent Surgical, Inc.)
**Category:** Synthetic absorbable dural sealant
**REF:** 20-2050 (polymer kit + delivery system)

## What It Is

Two-component synthetic absorbable hydrogel sealant applied as a spray over sutured dural closures to achieve watertight seal. The system consists of a PEG (polyethylene glycol) ester solution ("clear" precursor) and a trilysine amine solution ("blue" precursor). When co-sprayed through the applicator, the two precursors cross-link on contact to form a hydrogel at the tissue surface. The blue dye (FD&C Blue #1) aids in gauging thickness during application.

The hydrogel is absorbed in approximately 4-8 weeks, providing sufficient time for dural healing. The hydrogel may swell up to 50% of its initial size in any dimension after application.

## Sizing

Not a sized device. Volume applied is determined by the extent of the durotomy.

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| Powder Vial/Diluent Syringe Assembly | PEG powder + diluent for the blue precursor |
| Clear Precursor Syringe | PEG ester solution |
| Applicator | Dual-barrel spray applicator |
| Spray Tips (3) | Replaceable tips for applicator |
| Plunger Cap | Locks both syringe plungers together |

**Decision rule:** Apply a thin layer (1-2 mm). As thickness increases to 2 mm, the blue color deepens and fine epidural vasculature becomes less visible. Do not over-apply; thicker layers increase swelling-related compression risk.

## Indications

- Adjunct to sutured dural repair during cranial surgery to provide watertight closure
- Must be used with autologous duraplasty material only
- Patient must have a CSF leak after primary dural closure (spontaneous or upon Valsalva maneuver up to 20 cm H2O for 5-10 seconds)

**Eligible surgical approaches:** Frontal, Temporal, Parietal, Occipital, and/or Suboccipital

**Wound classification:** Class I/Clean (per CDC criteria)

**Minimum durotomy length:** 2 cm

**Minimum dural margin from bony defect edges:** 3 mm throughout

## Contraindications

- Do not apply to confined bony structures where nerves are present -- neural compression may result from hydrogel swelling (up to 50% in any dimension)
- Active infection at the surgical site

## Relevant Protocol

**Pre-application requirements:**
- Achieve adequate hemostasis before applying DuraSeal
- Ensure 2-3 mm clear margins around the durotomy edge, free of clots, fluids, hemostatic agents, and loose connective tissue
- Must be used within 1 hour of preparation
- Do not combine with other sealants or hemostatic agents

**Not studied in:**
- Patients with known allergy to FD&C Blue #1 dye
- Contaminated cranial procedures penetrating air sinus or mastoid air cells (other than superficial penetration)
- Patients with severely altered renal or hepatic function
- Patients with compromised immune system or autoimmune disease
- Translabyrinthine, transsphenoidal, transoral procedures
- Procedures involving petrous bone drilling
- Patients with traumatic head injuries
- Patients younger than 18 years
- Pregnant or breastfeeding females
- Hydrocephalus (elevated ICP > 22 cm H2O)

## Deployment/Use Step-by-Step

**Polymer Kit Mixing:**

1. Inspect the PEG powder vial to ensure the powder is free flowing (shake to loosen if needed). If powder remains clumped, discard the entire kit.
2. Open the sterile tray and introduce contents into the sterile field.
3. Pierce the vial seal by pushing the syringe/stopcock assembly into the vial cap. The seal is completely pierced when the vial luer neck recesses into the cap and a "click" is noted. Twisting is not required. Avoid pushing the syringe plunger.
4. Rotate the stopcock to the open position and inject syringe contents into the vial. If fluid leaks from the vial/syringe assembly, discard the entire polymer kit.
5. Gently shake the vial/syringe assembly until the powder is completely dissolved. The solution will turn blue.
6. Invert the vial/syringe assembly and draw the vial contents back into the syringe.
7. Unscrew the syringe from the vial/stopcock assembly and discard the vial/stopcock assembly.

**Applicator Preparation:**

8. Before attaching syringes to the applicator, ensure fluid levels in both syringes are equal. If not, expel fluid until equal.
9. Attach the blue and clear precursor syringes to the applicator.
10. While holding syringes by the plungers, carefully attach the plunger cap to both syringe plungers without dispensing precursors into the applicator. Do not touch the plunger cap before application to avoid inadvertent injection and tip plugging.
11. Attach a spray tip to the applicator.

**Hydrogel Application:**

12. Position the applicator 2-4 cm from the target site. Apply firm, even pressure to the center of the plunger cap. Use rapid initial spraying, followed by a slower controlled rate.
13. Continue applying until a thin (1-2 mm) coating is formed. Use the blue color to gauge thickness.
14. Remove any hydrogel beyond the dural margins with scissors or mechanical disruption. Irrigation immediately after solidification is permitted.

## Bailout / Troubleshooting

**Spray tip is plugged during delivery?**
- Remove the plugged spray tip, wipe the applicator tip clean, attach a new spray tip (3 included), and continue delivery.

**Applied too thick a layer?**
- Excess hydrogel beyond the dural margins can be removed with scissors or mechanical disruption after solidification. Irrigation is permitted immediately after solidification.

**Incidental application to muscle or skin planes?**
- Avoid. Application to tissue planes that will subsequently be approximated (muscle, skin) should be avoided because the hydrogel will prevent proper tissue-to-tissue healing.

**Fluid levels in syringes are unequal?**
- Expel fluids from syringes until levels are equal before attaching to the applicator. Unequal levels will result in improper cross-linking.

**How does DuraSeal appear on imaging?**
- CT: Dark (similar to CSF) due to high water content.
- T1-weighted MRI: Dark (similar to CSF).
- T2-weighted MRI: Helpful for differentiating DuraSeal from CSF.
- During absorption, vascular enhancement of residual margins is normally seen.
- May overlap imaging characteristics with persistent unilocular fluid collection or infected surgical bed. If CT/MR cannot exclude infection, consider indium-labeled white-blood cell nuclear medicine study.
- Average 74.5% reduction in extradural space at 3 months based on CT imaging.

## Complications to Know

From the pivotal clinical study (N=111, single-arm, multi-center):

| Complication | Rate |
|---|---|
| Cranial nerve deficit | 30.0% |
| Nausea and/or vomiting | 21.6% |
| Motor deficit | 15.3% |
| Visual disturbance | 19.8% |
| Speech difficulty | 10.9% |
| Headache (responding to standard therapy) | 8.1% |
| CSF leak (incisional) | 1.8% |
| CSF leak (pseudomeningocele) | 2.7% |
| Surgical wound infection (deep) | 7.2% |
| Surgical wound infection (superficial) | 0.9% |
| Hydrocephalus | 3.6% |
| Seizure | 2.7% |
| Cerebral edema | 3.6% |
| Meningitis (aseptic) | 4.5% |
| Meningitis (bacterial) | 1.8% |
| Death | 1.8% (2/111, attributed to prior conditions) |

**Primary endpoint:** 98.2% (109/111) intraoperative sealing success (no CSF leak after up to 2 DuraSeal applications during Valsalva up to 20 cm H2O for 5-10 seconds). All 111 patients showed no leakage during intra-operative assessment; 2 patients were tested at only 10 cm H2O and could not technically be classified as successes.

**Post-op CSF leak rate:** 4.5% (1.8% incisional, 2.7% pseudomeningocele)

**Infection:** 8.1% (9/111) total surgical wound infections, with 7.2% deep and all treated with surgical debridement. History of smoking and prolonged surgery were independent predictors for infection.

**Note:** These adverse event rates reflect the complexity of the surgeries performed (45.9% tumor, 10.8% aneurysm, 6.3% AVM, 18.9% nerve decompression) and are not attributable solely to DuraSeal.

## Effectiveness

- 100% (111/111) intraoperative sealing with no CSF leak observed
- 98.2% met primary endpoint criteria (Valsalva to 20 cm H2O)
- 94.6% required only one application; 5.4% required two applications
- All wounds well healed by 3-month post-operative visit
- No untoward effect on hepatic or renal function associated with product use and absorption
- 74.5% average reduction in extradural space at 3 months on CT

## MRI Safety

Not a metallic implant. The hydrogel is absorbable (4-8 weeks). No MRI restrictions.

**Imaging characteristics:**
- Dark on CT and T1-weighted MRI (similar to CSF)
- T2-weighted MRI is helpful for differentiation from CSF
- Vascular enhancement of residual margins is normal during absorption
