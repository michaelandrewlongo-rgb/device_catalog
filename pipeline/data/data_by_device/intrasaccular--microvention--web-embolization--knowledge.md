# WEB Aneurysm Embolization System (Sequent Medical / MicroVention)

**Manufacturer:** MicroVention, Inc. (Terumo) -- acquired from Sequent Medical
**Category:** Intrasaccular device
**Source:** https://www.terumoneuro.com/products/web-family

## What It Is

The WEB is an intrasaccular flow disruptor for wide-necked bifurcation aneurysms. It is a self-expanding nitinol and platinum core braided mesh device that is placed entirely within the aneurysm sac. It works by disrupting inflow at the aneurysm neck without requiring a stent in the parent vessel, meaning dual antiplatelet therapy is not mandatory (though practice varies). The device is electro-thermally detached using a hand-held, battery-powered WEB Detachment Controller (WDC). Available in two shapes: SL (Single Layer, barrel shape) and SLS (Single Layer Sphere), with dome diameters from 3 mm to 11 mm. The device foreshortens approximately 60% during deployment.

Key differentiator: intrasaccular treatment without parent vessel implant, potentially avoiding lifelong DAPT.

### Sizing Decision Rule

1. Perform 2D angiographic fluoroscopy in two orthogonal projections
2. Measure aneurysm necks, diameters, and heights in each projection
3. Select WEB SL or SLS shape based on average aneurysm diameter-to-average neck ratio:
   - **SL shape:** fits 1-to-1 through 1.5-to-1 ratio aneurysms
   - **SLS shape:** fits 1.5-to-1 through 2-to-1 ratio aneurysms
4. Choose the appropriate WEB device using the **average width and smallest height** measurements
5. **Correct WEB size is approximately 1 mm larger than the average aneurysm diameter AND approximately 1 mm smaller than the smallest aneurysm height**
6. Device should be deployed using device diameter **over-sizing** relative to the target lesion diameter

### Key Sizing Notes

- The WEB foreshortens during delivery (~60%). Example: an 11 mm x 9 mm device will measure ~20 mm in length within a 0.032"-0.038" delivery microcatheter
- When properly deployed, two radio-opaque markers should be separated and visible. The distance between proximal and distal markers should approximate the labeled WEB length
- WEB visibility varies with diameter; larger sizes more visible than smaller sizes

## Indications

- Adults aged 18-75 years
- Wide-necked, saccular intracranial aneurysms at:
  - Middle cerebral artery (MCA) bifurcation
  - Internal carotid artery (ICA) terminus
  - Anterior communicating artery (AComm) complex
  - Basilar artery apex
- Dome diameter 3 mm to 10 mm
- Either neck size >= 4 mm OR dome-to-neck ratio > 1 and < 2

**Expanded indications (from patient leaflet / international labeling):**
- Endovascular embolization of ruptured AND unruptured intracranial aneurysms
- Other neurovascular abnormalities such as arteriovenous fistulae (AVF)
- Vascular occlusion of blood vessels to permanently obstruct blood flow to an aneurysm or other vascular malformation
- Materials: nitinol wires with platinum core

**Key advantages over alternatives (per manufacturer):**
- Single-device treatment for wide-neck bifurcation aneurysms that often require multiple coils + stent
- No stent in parent artery (avoids mandatory long-term DAPT)
- Shorter procedure time vs coil embolization with stent assist
- Potentially reduced post-procedure medication burden

## Contraindications

- Known active bacterial infection that may interfere with or negatively affect the implantation procedure
- Known hypersensitivity to nickel

## Relevant Protocol

**Antiplatelet therapy:** The IFU does NOT mandate DAPT (this is a key advantage of intrasaccular devices). However, the safety/effectiveness has not been established for patients taking anticoagulants or with known blood dyscrasia, coagulopathy, or hemoglobinopathy. Clinical practice often involves some antiplatelet regimen at physician discretion.

**Detachment:** Electro-thermal detachment via the WEB Detachment Controller (WDC). The WDC is a hand-held, battery-powered device (4x A23 batteries, pre-loaded). The detachment control device is a single-use device. Ensure at least 2 WEB detachment control devices are available before initiating an embolization procedure.

**Activated Clotting Time (ACT):** Do NOT allow an inappropriately sized or non-optimally positioned device to reside in the aneurysm significantly beyond the ACT. Thrombus formation can prevent full deployment and recapture.

**Post-procedure imaging:** Use only DSA or CTA to assess intracranial aneurysm occlusion for follow-up. MRA may be degraded by susceptibility artifacts.

## Deployment/Use Step-by-Step

### Catheterization of the Lesion
1. Access the vessel with a guide catheter using standard interventional procedures. Guide catheter should have an inner diameter large enough to allow contrast injection while the microcatheter is in place.
2. Attach a rotating hemostatic valve (RHV) to the hub of the guide catheter. Attach a three-way stopcock to the side arm for continuous infusion of flush solution.
3. Select a microcatheter with the appropriate inner diameter (see microcatheter table above).
4. After the microcatheter is positioned inside the lesion, remove the guidewire.
5. Attach a second RHV to the hub of the microcatheter. Attach a one-way stopcock to the sidearm and connect the flush solution line.
6. Open the stopcock to allow flush through the microcatheter with sterile flush solution.

### Device Size Selection
7. The WEB implant should be deployed using device diameter over-sizing relative to the target lesion.
8. Understand the aneurysm shape, width, and height by performing 2D angiographic fluoroscopy in two orthogonal projections.
9. Measure the aneurysm necks, diameters, and heights in each projection.
10. Select SL or SLS shape based on the average diameter-to-neck ratio.
11. Choose the appropriate WEB device using the average width and smallest height measurements.
12. The correct WEB size is approximately 1 mm larger than average aneurysm diameter and approximately 1 mm smaller than the smallest aneurysm height.

### Preparation of the Device for Delivery
13. Remove the detachment control device from its protective packaging and place it within the sterile field. Do not use any power source other than the WEB detachment controller.
14. Remove the delivery device from the packaging hoop by pulling the proximal end until the introducer exits the hoop.
15. Slowly advance the embolization device out of the introducer sheath and inspect for any damage.
16. Firmly insert the proximal end of the delivery device into the distal end of the detachment control device. If the light flashes green and the device beeps, the device is good to use. If the light flashes green and beeps with a second controller, proceed. Otherwise, obtain a new WEB delivery system.
17. While holding the introducer sheath vertically, gently retract the embolization device back into the introducer sheath.

### Introduction and Deployment of the Device
18. Open the RHV on the microcatheter to accept the introducer sheath.
19. Insert the introducer sheath through the RHV. Seat the distal tip of the introducer sheath at the distal end of the microcatheter hub and close the RHV lightly. Do only insert until a slight resistance is felt. Do not over-insert.
20. Push the device into the lumen of the microcatheter. Use caution to avoid catching the device on the junction between the introducer sheath and hub of the microcatheter.
21. Push the device through the microcatheter until the proximal end of the delivery device meets the proximal end of the introducer sheath.
22. Loosen the RHV.
23. Retract the introducer sheath just out of the RHV.
24. Close the RHV around the delivery device.
25. Slide the introducer sheath completely off the delivery device using care not to kink or damage the delivery system.
26. Carefully advance the device until the distal end of the device reaches the last marker on the microcatheter.
27. Reposition the tip of the microcatheter so that it sits just at the neck of the aneurysm. Do NOT insert the microcatheter completely inside the aneurysm.
28. Under fluoroscopic guidance, slowly advance the embolization device out the tip of the microcatheter and continue to advance into the lesion until optimal deployment is achieved.
    - a. If the size is not appropriate, remove and replace with another device.
    - b. If undesirable movement is noted following placement and prior to detachment, remove the device. Movement may indicate the device could migrate once detached.
    - c. If the device does not fully open:
      - i. Retrieve the implant, reposition the microcatheter further proximally, and re-deploy to allow more room for expansion, OR
      - ii. Replace the implant with another of the same or alternate size.

**CRITICAL:** The embolization device should not be retracted and deployed more than twice. After two attempts, remove and replace with another device of the same or alternate size. Do NOT allow an inappropriately sized or non-optimally positioned device to reside in the aneurysm significantly beyond the ACT.

29. Angiographic assessment should always be performed prior to detachment to ensure the device is not markedly protruding into the parent vessel.
30. The WEB should be placed with the proximal surface (center of proximal marker) aligned with the aneurysm neck and the proximal marker extending beyond the neck.
31. If redeploying, retract device so that there is no contact with the aneurysm before attempting redeployment.
32. Tighten the RHV to prevent movement of the embolization device.

### Detachment of the Device
33. Verify that the distal portion of the delivery device is not under tension or compression prior to detachment (could cause the microcatheter tip to move, resulting in aneurysm or vessel rupture).
34. The detachment control device is pre-loaded with batteries and will activate when the delivery device is properly connected.
35. Verify the RHV is firmly locked around the delivery device.
36. Ensure the delivery device gold connectors are clean and free from blood or contrast. Wipe with sterile water and dry before connecting.
37. Insert the proximal end of the delivery device into the detachment control device. When properly connected, the light will flash green and an intermittent tone will be heard.
38. Verify the embolization device position before pressing the detachment button.
39. Push the detachment button. During firing, the light should be solid green and the beep should be continuous.
40. Verify detachment by first loosening the RHV valve, then pulling back slowly on the delivery device and verifying there is no embolization device movement. If it does not detach, push the detachment button again. If still not detached, obtain a new detachment control device and attempt up to two additional times. If it still does not detach, remove the delivery device.
41. Verify position of the embolization device angiographically through the guide catheter.
42. Prior to removing the microcatheter from the treatment site, place an appropriately sized guidewire completely through the microcatheter lumen to ensure no part of the embolization device remains within the microcatheter.

## Bailout / Troubleshooting

**Q: The WEB device does not fully open inside the aneurysm.**
A: Two options: (1) Retrieve the implant, reposition the microcatheter further proximally, and re-deploy to allow more room for expansion. (2) Replace the implant with another of the same or alternate size. Maximum 2 redeployment attempts.

**Q: The radio-opaque markers are clustered (shorter distance between markers than expected).**
A: Retract the WEB into the microcatheter and evaluate the microcatheter/aneurysm position with multiple fluoroscopic angles. The clustering suggests incomplete deployment or sizing mismatch.

**Q: The device does not detach after pressing the button.**
A: Push the detachment button again. If still not detached, obtain a new WEB detachment control device and repeat. Attempt up to two additional times. If still not detached, remove the delivery device entirely.

**Q: The embolization device needs to be retrieved from the vasculature after detachment.**
A: Use retrieval devices (e.g., alligator and snare) per their manufacturer's instructions.

**Q: I see movement of the device before detachment.**
A: Remove the device. Movement of the embolization device may indicate that the device could migrate once it is detached. Do not detach.

**Q: Steam-shaped microcatheter causing delivery problems.**
A: Steam shaping 0.021" and greater microcatheters may result in improper WEB delivery and deployment, depending on the degree of shaping and catheter deflection during WEB delivery.

## MRI Safety

**MR Conditional.** Safe under the following conditions:

- Static magnetic field of 3-Tesla or less
- Maximum spatial gradient field of 4,000 gauss/cm (40 T/m)
- Maximum MR system reported whole body averaged SAR of 2.0 W/kg for 15 minutes of scanning per pulse sequence in Normal Operating Mode

**Temperature rise:**
- Maximum temperature rise of +1.4 degrees C after 15 minutes of continuous scanning per pulse sequence

**Image artifact:**
- Extends approximately 5 mm from the implant when imaged with a gradient echo pulse sequence on a 3-Tesla MRI system

**Caution:** The WEB device may create local field inhomogeneity and susceptibility artifacts during MRA, potentially degrading diagnostic quality. Use DSA or CTA only for assessing intracranial aneurysm occlusion for patient follow-up.

**Materials:** Not made with natural rubber latex or PVC.

## Patient Counseling Points

**Pre-procedure:** Final preparations include additional tests, medications, and groin puncture site prep. Procedure performed under general anesthesia.

**During procedure (for family):** Small groin puncture to access femoral artery. Microcatheter advanced to aneurysm. WEB device deployed through microcatheter into the aneurysm. Device stops blood from entering the aneurysm, allowing it to occlude over time.

**Post-procedure:**
- Hospital stay: one to several days depending on condition
- Mild discomfort at groin puncture site expected
- Physician will monitor treated aneurysm over time with follow-up imaging
- Patient receives MRI card explaining device behavior under medical scans

**Seek immediate medical attention for:**
- Severe post-procedure headache
- Drowsiness
- Severe neck stiffness
- Any neurological impairment: difficulty speaking, difficulty moving, visual disturbances

**Device lifetime:** No expected limitation. No routine replacement needed.
