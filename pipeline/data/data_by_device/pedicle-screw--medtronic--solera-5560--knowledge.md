# Solera 5.5/6.0 Pedicle Screw System (Medtronic / CD HORIZON)

## What It Is

The CD HORIZON SOLERA 5.5/6.0 is a comprehensive posterior thoracolumbar pedicle screw system compatible with 5.5mm and 6.0mm rods. It uses a top-loading open tulip design with multi-axial, fixed-angle, sagittal adjusting, and reduction screw options. The system employs break-off set screws for final locking and is color-coded by screw diameter. It is part of the broader CD HORIZON spinal system family and is compatible with CROSSLINK plates, hooks, and lateral connectors. The Solera system is designed for posterior, non-cervical fixation as an adjunct to fusion in the thoracic, lumbar, and sacral spine.

## Indications

- Degenerative disc disease (discogenic origin with degeneration confirmed by history and radiographic studies)
- Spondylolisthesis
- Trauma (fracture or dislocation)
- Spinal stenosis
- Curvatures (scoliosis, kyphosis, lordosis)
- Tumor
- Pseudarthrosis
- Failed previous fusion
- Posterior, non-cervical fixation as an adjunct to fusion
- Thoracic, lumbar, and sacral spine

## Contraindications

- Active infection at the operative site
- Severe osteoporosis or osteopenia that would compromise implant fixation
- Patient conditions precluding benefit of spinal fixation (e.g., morbid obesity, pregnancy, mental illness, substance abuse affecting compliance)
- Inadequate tissue coverage of the implant
- Known allergy to titanium, cobalt-chromium, or stainless steel
- Any condition that would compromise the ability of the implant to provide stability
- Fever or elevated WBC count suggestive of active systemic infection
- Do not mix stainless steel and titanium in the same construct (accelerates corrosion)
- PEEK rods not indicated for degenerative disc disease in the LEGACY/3.5mm platform

## Use Notes

- Break-off set screws provide built-in torque confirmation: the cap shears at the designed torque. If it does not break off, re-seat the rod before retrying.
- Color-coded screws by diameter (pink=4.5, green=5.5, blue=6.5, gold=7.5, dark blue=8.5, purple=9.5) reduce back-table selection errors.
- Reduction Multi-Axial Screws (RMAS) have extended tulip heads for rod reduction in deformity cases without additional instruments.
- Sagittal Adjusting Screws allow fine sagittal plane correction after rod placement.
- Under-tap by 0.5-1.0mm relative to screw diameter for better purchase, especially in osteoporotic bone.
- Do not reverse-bend rods (creates stress risers and rod notching). Use small incremental bends in one direction.
- Straight rods 90mm and greater have hex ends for derotation maneuvers.
- Iliac screws available in closed multi-axial and fixed configurations for spinopelvic fixation.

## Relevant Setup

### Instruments Required
- **Pedicle access:** Awl, Curved Blunt Probe, Thoracic Pedicle Probe, Pedicle Feelers (malleable, medium, stiff), Modular Tap (3.0-10.5mm)
- **Screw insertion:** Screwdriver (Inner Shaft + Outer Shaft + Sleeve assembly), multiple handle options (T-Handle, Round Handle, Ratchet variants, Jacobs Chuck Handle)
- **Rod work:** Rod Template, Table-Top Rod Cutter and Stand, French Benders, Tube Benders (Left/Right), Rod Insertion Forceps, Rod Gripper, In Situ Rod Benders (Left/Right), Coronal Plane Benders (Left/Right), Ball Joint
- **Compression/distraction:** Compressor (Small/Large), Distractor (Small/Large)
- **Final tightening:** Torque Wrench (standard and short), Counter Torque

### Screw-Screwdriver Assembly
The Solera Screwdriver has three components:
1. **Inner Shaft** - tabs engage the screw saddle
2. **Outer Shaft** - threads lock the screwdriver to the screw head
3. **Sleeve** - slides over the assembly for alignment

## Use Step-by-Step

### Pedicle Preparation
1. Expose the posterior elements and identify pedicle entry points using anatomical landmarks.
2. Create a pilot hole using the Awl at the entry point.
3. Probe the pedicle tract using the Curved Blunt Probe or Thoracic Pedicle Probe to establish the screw path.
4. Check all four walls and the floor of the pedicle channel with the Pedicle Feeler to confirm no breach.
5. If needed, tap the pedicle channel using the Modular Tap to the desired diameter. Under-tapping by 0.5-1.0mm is typical.

### Screw Insertion
6. Select the appropriate screw size based on pedicle anatomy and pre-operative planning.
7. Assemble the Screwdriver: insert the Inner Shaft down the Outer Shaft, then slide the Sleeve over both.
8. Attach the quick-connect handle to the Inner Shaft.
9. Hold the screw by the threads and engage the tabs on the Inner Shaft into the saddle of the screw head.
10. Fully seat the Inner Shaft into the screw head, then turn the Outer Shaft clockwise using the "LOCK" button until threads are fully engaged. A ratchet sound confirms locking.
11. Insert the screw into the prepared pedicle channel to the desired depth.
12. To disengage the Screwdriver: press and release the "UNLOCK" button, rotate the Outer Shaft counterclockwise while firmly holding the handle, then release the quick-connect handle and remove the Sleeve and Inner Shaft.

### Rod Contouring and Placement
13. After all screws are placed, measure rod length using the Rod Template.
14. Select the appropriate pre-cut rod or cut a longer rod using the Table-Top Rod Cutter.
15. Contour the rod to the desired sagittal profile using French Benders or Tube Benders. Use small incremental bends for even stress distribution. Do not repeatedly contour or bend in the opposite direction.
16. Place the rod into the screw head grooves using the Rod Insertion Forceps or Rod Gripper.

### Rod Reduction (if needed)
17. For easy rod seating, use the Rod Persuader or Reduction instruments to bring the rod down to the screw head.
18. Insert the Break-off Set Screw into each screw head using the appropriate driver. Finger-tighten only at this stage.

### Compression/Distraction
19. Apply compression or distraction across segments as needed using the Compressor or Distractor instruments.
20. Perform any additional in situ bending for fine-tuning alignment.

### Final Tightening
21. After satisfactory alignment is confirmed, final-tighten each set screw using the Torque Wrench with Counter Torque until the break-off cap shears (indicates proper torque achieved).
22. Confirm all set screws are fully seated and locked.

### Cross Connector Placement (if applicable)
23. Measure for cross connector size using the Cross Connector Measuring Device or MAC Caliper.
24. Place the CROSSLINK plate using the Cross Connector Inserter.
25. Tighten the set screws with the 3.5mm Hex Driver (set screws) and 8mm Hex Driver (center nut) for final locking.

## Pearls / Troubleshooting

- **Screw color coding matters at the back table.** Learn the color scheme to avoid diameter mismatches: pink = 4.5, green = 5.5, blue = 6.5, gold = 7.5.
- **Ratchet sound on screwdriver = locked.** If no ratchet sound when turning the Outer Shaft clockwise, press the "LOCK" button to confirm engagement. An unlocked screwdriver can drop the screw.
- **Under-tap by 0.5-1.0mm** relative to screw diameter for better purchase, especially in osteoporotic bone.
- **Do not reverse-bend rods.** Bending a rod in one direction then the opposite creates stress risers and notching. Make small incremental adjustments in one direction.
- **Break-off set screws provide built-in torque confirmation.** The cap shears at the designed torque. If it does not break off, the screw is not fully seated; re-seat the rod first.
- **Straight rods 90mm and greater have hex ends** for derotation maneuvers.
- **Do not mix stainless steel and titanium** in the same construct, as this accelerates corrosion.
- **PEEK OPTIMA-LT1 implants** may be used with stainless steel, titanium, or cobalt-chromium-molybdenum alloy.
- **Reduction screws (RMAS)** have extended tulip heads to allow rod reduction without additional instruments; useful for deformity cases where the rod is far from the screw heads.
- For pediatric cases, PEEK rods, Shape Memory Alloy Staples, SPIRE Plates, and DYNALOK bolts are specifically excluded.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **vs DePuy Synthes EXPEDIUM VERSE:** VERSE offers integrated open + MIS in one platform; SOLERA requires the separate SEXTANT system for MIS. VERSE has broader pediatric indications (neuromuscular, congenital scoliosis). SOLERA has more granular screw sizing with color-coded diameter identification and break-off torque-confirming set screws.
- **vs Stryker Xia 3:** Xia 3 is deformity-focused with SUK DVR and uniplanar screws. SOLERA covers a broader range of pathology (degenerative, trauma, tumor, deformity). SOLERA integrates with StealthStation and POWEREASE; Xia 3 does not. Both accept 5.5/6.0mm rods.
- **vs Globus REVERE:** REVERE uses a non-threaded 90-degree locking cap (no cross-threading, faster closure). SOLERA uses break-off set screws (tactile torque confirmation). SOLERA has wider hook selection and CROSSLINK plate options. REVERE has the Addition revision extension system.
- **vs NuVasive Reline:** Both are open thoracolumbosacral systems. SOLERA has deeper system breadth (hooks, CROSSLINK plates, iliac screws, lateral connectors, SPIRE plate). Reline is known for ease of rod capture. SOLERA integrates with Medtronic's navigation and power instrument ecosystem.
- **vs Neo Medical Neo PSS:** Neo radically simplifies inventory (14 screws, single-use instruments). SOLERA is the opposite approach: maximum configurability with dozens of screw types, hooks, connectors, and rod materials. SOLERA is better suited for complex deformity and revision; Neo targets straightforward degenerative/trauma cases.
