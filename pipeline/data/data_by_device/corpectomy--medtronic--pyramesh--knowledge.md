# Pyramesh Titanium Cage (Medtronic)

**Manufacturer:** Medtronic
**Category:** Corpectomy / Vertebral Body Replacement

## What It Is

The Pyramesh is a titanium mesh cage used for vertebral body replacement (corpectomy reconstruction) in the thoracic and lumbar spine. It is one of the original titanium mesh corpectomy cages based on the Harms cage concept. The cage is a hollow, open-ended titanium cylinder (cylindrical version) or rectangular shell that is cut to the required length intraoperatively and packed with morselized autograft bone to promote fusion. After partial or complete vertebral body resection, the Pyramesh provides immediate anterior column structural support while bone graft incorporates through the mesh walls. It requires supplemental anterior plate or posterior pedicle screw fixation.

## Sizing/Specs

### Implant Configurations
- **Cylindrical version:** Available in multiple outer diameters to match vertebral body endplate size
- **Rectangular version:** Available in select configurations (refer to Medtronic technical documentation for exact dimensions)
- **Length:** Supplied in stock lengths; cut to the required corpectomy defect height intraoperatively using the dedicated cutting instrument

### Material
- Titanium mesh (open-ended design)
- Mesh pattern allows bone ingrowth through the cage walls

### Key Features
- Cut-to-length design for intraoperative customization
- Open ends for bone graft packing from both sides
- Non-expandable (press-fit seating under distraction)

### Instruments
- Dedicated cage holder for insertion
- Dedicated cage cutter for trimming to length (do not use generic wire cutters)
- Refer to Medtronic Pyramesh instrument guide for full catalog numbers

## Indications

- Vertebral body replacement after corpectomy in the thoracic and lumbar spine
- Thoracolumbar burst fractures with vertebral body comminution
- Vertebral body tumors requiring resection and anterior column reconstruction
- Vertebral osteomyelitis/infection after debridement
- Failed previous vertebral body reconstruction
- Kyphotic deformity requiring anterior column support after corpectomy
- Must be used with supplemental fixation (anterior plate or posterior instrumentation)

## Contraindications

- Active systemic or local infection at the surgical site (unless the corpectomy is performed for infection debridement, with risk-benefit consideration)
- Severe osteoporosis with insufficient endplate quality to support the cage (high subsidence risk with non-expandable mesh design)
- Allergy or sensitivity to titanium
- Patient conditions precluding general anesthesia or major spine surgery
- Inadequate supplemental fixation options (Pyramesh cannot be used as a standalone device)
- Pathology in the cervical spine (not indicated for cervical corpectomy)

## Compatible With

- **Supplemental fixation (required):** Medtronic anterior plating systems, posterior pedicle screw systems (e.g., CD Horizon, Solera, Longitude), or lateral plate fixation
- **Bone graft:** Autograft (iliac crest, local bone from corpectomy, rib), allograft, demineralized bone matrix. BMP use is off-label in this application
- **Imaging:** Standard intraoperative fluoroscopy (AP and lateral); compatible with navigation-guided placement if desired
- **Instruments:** Pyramesh-specific cage holder and cage cutter

## Use Notes

- The Pyramesh is a non-expandable cage. The surgeon measures the corpectomy defect under distraction and cuts the cage to the required height before insertion. Cut slightly longer (1-2 mm) than the measured defect for press-fit seating.
- One of the original titanium mesh VBR cages with a long clinical track record. The Harms cage concept (named after Jurgen Harms) established the paradigm of mesh cage + morselized bone graft for corpectomy reconstruction.
- Dense graft packing is critical. The cage interior is the primary fusion chamber; the mesh walls allow supplemental bone ingrowth.
- Cylindrical design has a smaller endplate footprint than rectangular cages, concentrating load on a smaller area. Preserve the cortical rim of the adjacent endplates to minimize subsidence.
- In osteoporotic patients, consider expandable cages with larger endplate footprints as alternatives. The Pyramesh press-fit design is best suited for patients with adequate bone quality.
- Cutting is irreversible. Measure carefully. An undersized cage will migrate or settle.
- Do not impact aggressively during insertion. Controlled, steady pressure prevents mesh deformation.
- Supplemental fixation is mandatory for rotational and translational stability.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

| Feature | Medtronic Pyramesh | DePuy SYNMESH | Stryker X-CORP | Expandable Cages (e.g., Globus FORTIFY, DePuy VLIFT) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expansion mechanism | None (cut to length) | None (cut to length) | None (fixed sizes) | In-situ expansion |
| Shape | Cylindrical or rectangular | Cylindrical | Rectangular | Varies |
| Heritage | Original Harms cage concept | Early generation mesh cage | Later generation | Newer generation |
| Endplate contact | Circular (cylindrical) or rectangular footprint | Circular footprint | Rectangular footprint | Typically larger footprint |
| Subsidence risk | Higher in osteoporotic bone | Higher in osteoporotic bone | Lower (wider footprint) | Lower (expandable endplates) |
| Intraop sizing | Cut to length (irreversible) | Cut to length (irreversible) | Fixed sizes selected preop | Adjustable in situ |
| Cost | Lower | Lower | Moderate | Higher |

- Pyramesh and SYNMESH are functionally similar: both are cut-to-length titanium mesh cages with long track records. Choice between them is often driven by institutional vendor contracts.
- Expandable cages have largely supplanted non-expandable mesh cages in many practices due to easier insertion and reduced subsidence risk, but mesh cages remain cost-effective and well-validated.
- The Pyramesh's long track record is its primary advantage: decades of published clinical data support its use in thoracolumbar corpectomy.
