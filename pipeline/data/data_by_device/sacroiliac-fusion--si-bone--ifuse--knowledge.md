# iFuse Implant System (SI-BONE)

**Manufacturer:** SI-BONE, Inc.
**Category:** sacroiliac-fusion

---

## What It Is

The iFuse Implant System is a set of triangular cross-section titanium implants designed for fusion and stabilization of the sacroiliac (SI) joint. The triangular geometry is the defining feature: it resists rotational forces that can cause loosening with cylindrical or threaded implant designs. Typically, three implants are placed across the SI joint in a triangular configuration via a lateral, minimally invasive percutaneous approach under fluoroscopic guidance. The implants span from the ilium into the sacral ala, achieving immediate mechanical stabilization while promoting long-term bony fusion.

Two implant variants exist:
- **iFuse (original):** Solid triangular titanium implant with a titanium plasma spray (TPS) coating on the bone-contacting surfaces for bone ongrowth.
- **iFuse-3D:** 3D-printed porous titanium implant with a fenestrated (windowed) design that allows bone through-growth in addition to ongrowth. The 3D-printed porous surface mimics cancellous bone architecture to enhance biological fixation.

The implants are inserted using a dedicated pin-based targeting and insertion system. No bone graft is required for the procedure, though the iFuse-3D variant is designed to incorporate with native bone through its porous structure. FDA 510(k) cleared.

## Sizing/Specs

### Implant Cross-Section
- Triangular profile (equilateral triangle)
- **4.0mm inscribed diameter** -- for sacral ala with limited bone stock or revision cases
- **7.0mm inscribed diameter** -- standard size for most primary cases

### Implant Lengths
- Available in multiple lengths from approximately 30mm to 70mm
- Length selected based on patient anatomy (ilium-to-sacrum distance) measured on preoperative CT
- Refer to surgical technique guide for exact length increments and catalog numbers

### Materials
- **Implant body:** Titanium alloy (Ti-6Al-4V ELI)
- **Surface (iFuse original):** Titanium plasma spray (TPS) coating for bone ongrowth
- **Surface (iFuse-3D):** 3D-printed porous titanium with fenestrations for bone through-growth; no separate coating needed

### Typical Configuration
- Three implants per SI joint, placed in a triangular pattern
- Most common: three 7.0mm diameter implants at standard lengths determined by anatomy
- Two-implant constructs may be used in select cases

### MRI Compatibility
- **MR Conditional** at 1.5T and 3T under the following conditions:
  - Static magnetic field of 1.5-Tesla and 3-Tesla only
  - Maximum spatial gradient magnetic field of 4,000 Gauss/cm (40-T/m)
  - Maximum MR system reported whole body averaged SAR of 2-W/kg for 15 minutes of scanning in Normal Operating Mode
  - Maximum temperature rise less than 5.4 C after 15 minutes of continuous scanning
- Image artifact extends approximately 20mm from the implant when imaged with a gradient echo pulse sequence at 3T
- CT is the preferred modality for assessing fusion and implant position

## Indications

Per IFU (500104 Rev. T, December 2022):
- Sacroiliac joint dysfunction that is a direct result of sacroiliac joint disruption and degenerative sacroiliitis (includes conditions whose symptoms began during pregnancy or in the peripartum period and have persisted postpartum for more than 6 months)
- Augmentation of immobilization and stabilization of the sacroiliac joint in skeletally mature patients undergoing sacropelvic fixation as part of a lumbar or thoracolumbar fusion
- Acute, non-acute, and non-traumatic fractures involving the sacroiliac joint

Clinical confirmation typically requires positive provocative SI joint tests on exam, supportive imaging (CT or MRI), and a diagnostic SI joint injection with at least 75% pain relief.

## Contraindications

Per IFU:
1. Deformities or anatomic variations that prevent or interfere with iFuse placement
2. Tumor of sacral or ilial bone
3. Active infection at treatment site
4. Unstable fracture of sacrum and/or ilium involving the sacroiliac joint
5. Allergy to metal components

Clinical considerations (not in IFU but relevant to patient selection):
- Severe osteoporosis that would compromise implant fixation
- Morbid obesity precluding safe lateral percutaneous access or adequate fluoroscopic visualization
- Central sensitization syndromes as the sole pain generator

## Compatible With

- **iFuse Bedrock Granite (SI-BONE):** Supplemental sacropelvic fixation implant for cases requiring additional iliac or sacral fixation (e.g., spinopelvic reconstruction, revision)
- **Fluoroscopy:** Standard C-arm fluoroscopy for intraoperative guidance; lateral, inlet, and outlet pelvic views used for targeting
- **Navigation:** Compatible with intraoperative CT-based navigation systems for implant trajectory planning (not required but increasingly used)
- **Preoperative CT:** Thin-cut CT of the pelvis (1mm slices) recommended for preoperative templating of implant length and trajectory
- **Dedicated instrumentation:** SI-BONE pin-based targeting system, tissue dilators, triangular broach, insertion tool, depth gauge
- **No bone graft required:** The implants achieve fixation and fusion without supplemental bone graft, though surgeons may optionally decorticate the joint surface arthroscopically or pack graft around implants

## Use Notes

- Patient selection is critical. The SI joint must be confirmed as the pain generator before surgery. Standard diagnostic workup includes at least three positive provocative SI joint physical examination maneuvers and a diagnostic intra-articular SI joint injection with at least 75% pain relief.
- The lateral percutaneous approach is the standard technique. The patient is positioned prone. A small (approximately 3cm) incision is made over the lateral buttock. Sequential tissue dilation is performed to the outer iliac table. Guide pins are placed across the SI joint under fluoroscopic guidance using lateral, AP, inlet, and outlet views.
- Three implants in a triangular configuration provide the most stable construct. The first implant is typically placed most inferiorly. All three are placed through the same lateral incision.
- The triangular broach must be used to prepare the channel before implant insertion. The broach creates a triangular channel matching the implant cross-section, preventing rotation during insertion and in the postoperative period.
- Implant length must be selected to engage the sacral ala without breaching the sacral neural foramen or anterior sacral cortex. Preoperative CT templating and intraoperative fluoroscopy in multiple planes are essential.
- The iFuse-3D variant is increasingly preferred over the original TPS-coated iFuse due to its porous through-growth design, which may produce faster and more robust biological fixation. Both remain commercially available.
- Typical operative time is 30-60 minutes per side. Blood loss is minimal (usually less than 50mL). Most patients are discharged the same day or after one overnight stay.
- Weight-bearing restrictions vary by surgeon protocol but commonly include toe-touch weight bearing on the operative side for 2-4 weeks, progressing to full weight bearing by 6-8 weeks.
- Bilateral SI joint fusion can be performed in a single session or staged. Single-session bilateral procedures increase total operative time but avoid a second anesthetic.
- Complications include implant malposition (most common technical error), nerve root irritation (S1 or S2 if implant trajectory is too medial or posterior), hematoma, wound infection, and persistent pain. Reoperation rates in published registries are approximately 3-6% at 2 years.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **vs. SI-LOK (Globus Medical):** SI-LOK uses cylindrical threaded titanium implants (similar to large screws) placed across the SI joint. The threaded cylindrical design relies on thread purchase for rotational stability. The iFuse triangular cross-section inherently resists rotation without threads. SI-LOK requires less specialized instrumentation. Both are placed via a lateral percutaneous approach.
- **vs. Rialto SI Fusion System (Medtronic):** Rialto uses cylindrical threaded implants with a hydroxyapatite and titanium plasma spray coating. It is placed through a similar lateral approach. Like SI-LOK, the cylindrical design depends on thread engagement for rotational stability. The iFuse triangular geometry is the primary differentiator: it provides a three-point cortical contact within the channel that resists micromotion.
- **vs. SImmetry (Zyga, now RTI Surgical):** SImmetry uses a posterior approach (through the sacral ala into the ilium), which is opposite to the lateral iFuse approach direction. SImmetry implants include a decorticating anchor and allograft to promote fusion. The posterior approach may allow direct joint visualization and decortication but involves more tissue disruption than the lateral percutaneous iFuse technique.
- **vs. LinQ (PainTEQ):** LinQ is a posterior allograft SI joint fusion device placed through the S2 foramen. It uses a bone-graft-only approach (no metal implant left in place across the joint). This is fundamentally different from the iFuse metal implant strategy. LinQ has a lower mechanical stability profile but avoids permanent metal hardware.
- **General differentiator:** The iFuse is the most studied SI joint fusion device in the literature, with multiple Level I and II studies (INSITE, LOIS, iMIA randomized controlled trials) demonstrating superiority over conservative management. Most competing devices have fewer published prospective trials.
