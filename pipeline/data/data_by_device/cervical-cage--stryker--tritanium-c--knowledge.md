# Tritanium C Anterior Cervical Cage

**Manufacturer:** Stryker Spine
**IFU Reference:** QIN4432TRICREV01, Version 1

---

## What It Is

A hollow, ring-shaped titanium alloy (Ti-6Al-4V) interbody fusion cage for anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF). The cage has an open window for bone graft containment and serrations on the superior and inferior surfaces to resist migration. It is offered in multiple lengths, heights, and lordotic angles.

The "Tritanium" name refers to Stryker's proprietary porous titanium surface technology, designed to promote osseointegration. Once fused, the implant integrates deeply into the endplates, making revision removal difficult by design.

Key points:
- Material: Ti-6Al-4V (ASTM F1472)
- Implanted via open anterior approach
- Requires supplemental fixation (anterior cervical plate or integrated screw system) in the United States; supplemental fixation required for multi-level use outside the US
- Packed with autogenous and/or allogenic cancellous or corticocancellous bone graft
- Single use, provided sterile (gamma irradiation, minimum 25 kGy)
- Not latex, not PVC

---

## Sizing

The IFU states the cage is offered in "a variety of lengths, heights, and lordotic angles" but does not include a specific sizing table with dimensions. Sizing is determined intraoperatively based on patient anatomy. Consult the Stryker Spine surgical technique brochure for the complete sizing matrix.

### Decision Rule

- **Height:** Match the distracted disc space height. Trial spacers determine the correct height. Undersizing risks subsidence; oversizing risks endplate fracture and overdistraction.
- **Length (AP dimension):** Must sit fully on the endplate without posterior overhang into the canal. Lateral fluoroscopy confirms position.
- **Lordotic angle:** Choose based on desired segmental lordosis. Higher lordotic angles restore cervical curvature in patients with kyphotic deformity.
- **Single vs. two-level:** FDA clearance covers one or two contiguous levels from C2-T1. Supplemental fixation is mandatory.

## Indications

### USA and Canada

- Cervical interbody fusion in skeletally mature patients with degenerative disc disease (DDD) at one level or two contiguous levels from C2-T1
- DDD is defined as neck pain of discogenic origin with degeneration confirmed by history and radiographic studies
- Patients must have completed at least 6 weeks of non-operative therapy
- Must be used with autogenous and/or allogenic bone graft (cancellous and/or corticocancellous)
- Must be used with supplemental spinal fixation cleared for cervical use (e.g., anterior cervical plate)
- Implanted via open anterior approach

### Outside USA and Canada

- Spondylolisthesis, degenerative spine disorders, discal and vertebral instability
- May be used in spine revision surgery
- Supplemental fixation required when used in multiple levels
- Packing bone graft material within the implant is recommended

## Contraindications

### Hard Stops

- Active infection at the operative site
- Use outside labeled indications

### Relative Contraindications (weigh risk/benefit)

- Severe osteoporosis, osteopenia, or osteomalacia involving the spine -- limits mechanical fixation and risks subsidence
- Primary or metastatic tumors involving the spine
- Abnormalities affecting bone remodeling (metabolic bone disease)
- Mental or neuromuscular disorders creating unacceptable risk of fixation failure or postop noncompliance
- Open wounds at the operative site
- Pregnancy
- Inadequate tissue coverage over the operative site
- Obesity -- excessive loads accelerate implant fatigue and increase subsidence risk
- Senility, mental illness, substance abuse -- noncompliance risk
- Known foreign body sensitivity to titanium (perform skin tolerance testing if suspected)
- Prior fusion at the level(s) to be treated
- Elevated ESR or WBC unexplained by other disease
- Any condition where implant use would interfere with anatomical structures or physiological performance

## Compatible With

- **Supplemental fixation (required in US):** Any FDA-cleared anterior cervical plate system (e.g., Stryker Reflex Hybrid, Vectra, Atlantis Vision Elite). Do not mix titanium cage with stainless steel plate components (galvanic corrosion risk).
- **Bone graft:** Autogenous and/or allogenic cancellous or corticocancellous bone graft packed into the cage window. Bone graft substitutes may be used per surgeon preference.
- **Cervical retractor systems:** Standard anterior cervical retractor setups (Caspar-type pins, self-retaining retractors)
- **Imaging:** Intraoperative fluoroscopy (AP and lateral). Postoperative CT for fusion assessment, though titanium artifact limits evaluation compared to PEEK cages. MRI compatibility has NOT been formally evaluated for this device.
- **Dedicated instrument set:** Stryker Spine Tritanium C instrument tray per the surgical technique brochure. Do not use instruments from other systems.

## Use Notes

- The Tritanium porous surface is designed for deep osseointegration. Once fused, revision removal is extremely difficult and risks vertebral body fracture. This is by design, not a defect.
- Requires supplemental anterior plate fixation in the US for all cases (single and multi-level). Outside the US, supplemental fixation is required for multi-level cases only.
- Cleared for one or two contiguous levels from C2 to T1.
- Patients must have completed at least 6 weeks of non-operative therapy before surgery.
- Preserve the subchondral endplate during preparation to minimize subsidence risk. The porous titanium surface relies on healthy endplate bone for integration.
- Do not notch, scratch, or damage the implant surface. Surface defects create stress risers that reduce fatigue life.
- Titanium alloy cages produce more artifact on CT and MRI compared to PEEK cages, making radiographic fusion assessment more difficult.
- Never reuse an implant, even if it appears undamaged. Single-use, sterile-packed.
- Counsel smokers on increased nonunion risk.
- Consider cervical collar use for 2-4 months postoperatively until imaging confirms fusion progression.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **vs. Zero-P (DePuy):** The Tritanium C is a standalone cage requiring a separate anterior plate. The Zero-P integrates fixation into the cage, eliminating plate overhang and potentially reducing dysphagia. Tritanium C uses porous titanium for osseointegration; Zero-P uses PEEK (radiolucent body with titanium fixation components). Tritanium C may offer superior bone integration long-term; Zero-P offers a simpler single-implant construct.
- **vs. CAPSTONE (Medtronic):** Both require supplemental anterior plating. Tritanium C is porous titanium (promotes bone through-growth, more artifact on imaging). CAPSTONE is PEEK (radiolucent, artifact-free imaging, relies on graft window for fusion). Tritanium C's osseointegration surface is its differentiator; CAPSTONE offers cleaner postoperative imaging for fusion assessment.
- **vs. COALITION (Globus):** Both use titanium construction. COALITION offers integrated fixation screws, potentially eliminating the anterior plate in single-level cases. Tritanium C always requires a plate in the US. Both feature porous/textured surfaces for bone integration. COALITION's integrated fixation is its key advantage over Tritanium C.
- **Material tradeoff:** Titanium cages (Tritanium C, COALITION) are stiffer than PEEK (Zero-P, CAPSTONE), which may increase stress shielding and subsidence risk in osteoporotic bone but provides better initial stability and bone integration potential.

## Relevant Protocol

### Pre-Operative

- Confirm skeletal maturity
- Confirm at least 6 weeks of failed non-operative therapy
- Counsel smokers: smoking is associated with increased nonunion rates
- Assess bone density -- osteoporosis is a relative contraindication
- Plan supplemental fixation (required in the US for all cases; required outside US for multi-level)
- Counsel patients on activity restrictions and realistic expectations for function

### Intra-Operative

- Use only Stryker-provided instruments per the surgical technique brochure
- Do not apply excessive impaction or cantilever force during insertion
- Discard any damaged or mishandled implants
- Never reuse an implant, even if it appears undamaged
- Do not mix metals (e.g., titanium cage with stainless steel plate) -- galvanic corrosion risk
- Do not notch or scratch the implant surface -- stress risers reduce fatigue life
- Protect components from contact with metal or abrasive objects

### Post-Operative

- External support (cervical collar/brace) may be recommended for 2-4 months or until imaging confirms fusion mass maturation
- Restrict activity during fusion consolidation to prevent excessive implant loading
- Scheduled follow-up imaging to monitor fusion progression
- Antibiotic prophylaxis may be advisable before dental procedures or endoscopy (transient bacteremia risk to implant site)

## Deployment / Use Step-by-Step

Note: Detailed surgical technique is in the Stryker Spine technique brochure (available from Stryker representative). The IFU provides general guidance only.

1. Position patient supine, standard anterior cervical approach (Smith-Robinson or similar).
2. Perform discectomy, prepare endplates. Preserve subchondral bone to resist subsidence.
3. Trial spacers: determine appropriate cage height, length, and lordotic angle.
4. Open sterile cage packaging. Verify package integrity and expiration date. Do not resterilize under any circumstance.
5. Pack the cage window with autogenous and/or allogenic cancellous or corticocancellous bone graft.
6. Insert cage using Stryker-provided instrumentation. Do not use excessive impaction force.
7. Confirm position with lateral fluoroscopy. Cage should sit fully on endplates without posterior overhang.
8. Apply supplemental fixation (anterior cervical plate and screws).
9. Confirm final construct position with fluoroscopy.
10. Close per standard technique.

## Bailout / Troubleshooting

**Q: Cage subsides into the vertebral body postoperatively.**
A: Subsidence risk increases with osteoporotic bone, endplate violation during preparation, and oversized cage height. If symptomatic, revision may require corpectomy cage or additional posterior fixation. Optimize bone health (vitamin D, calcium, consider anabolic agents).

**Q: Cage migrates anteriorly.**
A: Usually indicates inadequate supplemental fixation or undersized cage. If asymptomatic and stable, observe. If progressive or causing dysphagia/esophageal compression, revise fixation.

**Q: Pseudoarthrosis (nonunion) detected on follow-up imaging.**
A: IFU warns that if nonunion develops, the device will be subject to repeated stresses that can cause loosening, bending, or fatigue fracture. Revision with posterior fixation and bone graft augmentation may be needed. Counsel smoking cessation.

**Q: Need to remove the cage after successful fusion.**
A: The Tritanium surface integrates deeply into bone by design. Removal is technically difficult and risks vertebral body fracture. The IFU states the cage is not intended to be removed unless managing a complication. If removal is required, expect a challenging revision with possible corpectomy.

**Q: Suspected titanium allergy.**
A: Uncommon but possible. If hypersensitivity is suspected preoperatively, perform skin tolerance testing before implantation. Postoperatively, if inflammatory reaction is disproportionate to expected course, consider metal sensitivity workup (lymphocyte transformation test).

**Q: Intraoperative endplate fracture during impaction.**
A: Stop impaction. Assess fracture extent on fluoroscopy. If the endplate is comminuted, the cage may not have adequate support -- consider a longer construct, corpectomy cage, or additional posterior stabilization.

## Complications to Know

Per IFU, potential adverse effects include:

- **Pseudoarthrosis / nonunion** -- implant will eventually fatigue-fracture if fusion does not occur
- **Implant fracture, loosening, or migration** -- finite device life; cannot indefinitely bear loads of normal bone
- **Subsidence** -- especially with osteoporotic bone or endplate violation
- **Infection** -- superficial or deep; transient bacteremia from dental/endoscopic procedures can seed the implant
- **Neurological injury** -- peripheral neuropathy, nerve damage, paralysis, loss of bowel/bladder function, foot drop
- **Dural leak** -- requiring surgical repair
- **Heterotopic bone formation**
- **Loss of spinal curvature, height, or correction**
- **Pain, discomfort, or abnormal sensations** from device presence
- **Allergic reactions** to implant materials (uncommon)
- **Stress shielding** -- decreased bone density adjacent to implant
- **Vascular injury, thrombus, embolism**
- **Vertebral fracture** -- intraoperative or postoperative
- **Esophageal injury** (from approach)
- **Recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy** (from approach)
- **Death**

No specific complication rates are provided in the IFU. Published ACDF literature generally reports fusion rates of 90-97% for single-level procedures with cage and plate. Dysphagia rates range from 2-50% acutely (depending on definition) with most resolving by 12 weeks. Revision rates are typically 2-5% at 2 years.

## MRI Safety

**The Tritanium C Anterior Cervical Cage has NOT been evaluated for safety and compatibility in the MR environment. It has NOT been tested for heating or migration in the MR environment.**

This is stated explicitly in the IFU as a caution. Until MR conditional testing is completed and published, treat this implant as **MR Unsafe / MR status unknown**.

Practical implication: if a patient with a Tritanium C cage needs an MRI, the surgeon and radiologist must jointly assess the risk. Ti-6Al-4V is generally considered weakly paramagnetic and most titanium cervical implants have been shown to be MR conditional at 1.5T and 3T in practice, but the absence of formal testing for this specific device means there is no manufacturer backing for that assumption.
