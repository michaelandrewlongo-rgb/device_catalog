# Axium Detachable Coil / Axium Prime Detachable Coil

**Manufacturer:** Medtronic (ev3 Neurovascular)
**Category:** Embolic coil

## What It Is

The Axium family consists of detachable platinum embolization coils for endovascular treatment of intracranial aneurysms and neurovascular malformations. The family includes two product lines:

- **Axium Detachable Coil:** Bare platinum coils available in helical and 3D configurations, with optional bioabsorbable polymer (PGLA) or nylon filars in certain variants. Intended for intracranial aneurysms and neurovascular abnormalities.
- **Axium Prime Detachable Coil:** A softer, smaller-diameter variant (loop OD 1-6 mm in standard sizes) designed for finer packing and finishing applications. Also available in larger 3D configuration (FC-X-Y-3D) for broader use including peripheral embolization.

Both lines use the Instant Detacher (I.D.) -- a standalone handheld mechanical detachment unit that pulls a release element inside the pusher to separate the implant from the delivery pusher tip. The detachment controller is sold separately. The pusher includes a radiopaque positioning marker.

The 510(k) K233420 (cleared 2023) represents a labeling update to permit radial artery access as an alternative to femoral access, reflecting adoption of transradial approaches in neurointerventional practice.

## Indications

**Axium Detachable Coil:** Intended for endovascular embolization of intracranial aneurysms and other neurovascular abnormalities including arteriovenous malformations and arteriovenous fistulae.

**Axium Prime Detachable Coil (APB-X-Y-3D/HX-ES/SS models):** Intended for endovascular embolization of intracranial aneurysms and other neurovascular abnormalities including arteriovenous malformations and arteriovenous fistulae.

**Axium Prime Detachable Coil (FC-X-Y-3D model only):** As above, plus arterial and venous embolizations in the peripheral vasculature.

Access route: femoral or radial artery (updated labeling per K233420).

## Compatible With

- **Detachment:** Instant Detacher (I.D.) mechanical unit (sold separately; required for coil release)
- **Microcatheter (Bare coils):** Minimum ID 0.0165"
- **Microcatheter (Nylon and PGLA coils):** Minimum ID 0.0165"-0.020"
- **Microcatheter (Axium Prime):** Minimum ID 0.0165"-0.017" with two marker bands
- **Guide catheter:** 6-8F

## Use Notes

- Detachment is mechanical via the Instant Detacher -- no electrolytic current required.
- Axium Prime variants are designed for finer/softer packing roles; standard Axium variants span a broader size range suitable for framing large aneurysms.
- Radial access now supported per 2023 labeling update (K233420); prior labeling required femoral sheath only.
- Model number format: X = coil loop outer diameter in mm, Y = implant length in cm.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **Detachment mechanism**: Axium uses a standalone mechanical Instant Detacher (sold separately), while competitors use various methods: Cerenovus devices use hydraulic detachment, HydroCoil uses a battery-powered thermal detachment controller (V-Grip), and i-ED COIL uses an electrolytic detachment system (EDG v4).
- **Coil material and design**: Axium offers bare platinum coils with optional bioabsorbable polymer (PGLA) or nylon filars, whereas HydroCoil features hydrogel-coated platinum-tungsten coils designed to expand for higher packing density, and Cerenovus coils use bare platinum with polypropylene stretch-resistant suture.
- **Indications and access**: Axium's 2023 labeling update explicitly permits radial artery access as an alternative to femoral access for neurointerventional procedures; this specific access route update is not mentioned for other devices in the provided data.
- **Recall status**: Axium has no active recalls mentioned, unlike Cerenovus (1 active recall for detachment failure) and Stryker's InZone system (20 active recalls, including for expired products).
- **Device family role**: Axium Prime is specifically designed as a softer, smaller-diameter variant for finer packing and finishing, with a dedicated large 3D configuration for peripheral embolization; other devices like Cerenovus' systems offer complex framing, filling, and finishing coils without this explicit role differentiation.
