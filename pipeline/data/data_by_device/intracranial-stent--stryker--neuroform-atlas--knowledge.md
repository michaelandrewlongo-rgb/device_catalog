# Neuroform Atlas Stent System (Stryker Neurovascular)

## What It Is

The Neuroform Atlas is a self-expanding, open-cell, nitinol intracranial stent designed for stent-assisted coiling of wide-necked intracranial aneurysms. The open-cell design features three radiopaque marker bands on each end (proximal and distal) and four interconnects between central stent segments to provide coil mass support within the aneurysm and minimize stent deflection. It is deployed by retracting the stent delivery microcatheter (unsheathing technique) rather than pushing the stent out. The delivery wire comes in two configurations: with an 8.5 mm distal tip or without a distal tip. An accessory torque device is included for handling stabilization (not for torquing). Available in 3 diameters (3.0, 4.0, 4.5 mm) and 4 lengths (15, 21, 24, 30 mm).

Key differentiator: open-cell design provides better wall apposition in tortuous vessels and easier access through stent interstices for coiling compared to closed-cell designs.

## Sizing

### Stent-to-Vessel Diameter Selection

| Labeled Stent Diameter (mm) | Unconstrained Stent Diameter (mm) | Recommended Parent Vessel Diameter (mm) |
|---|---|---|
| 3.0 | 3.5 | >= 2.0 and < 3.0 |
| 4.0 | 4.5 | >= 3.0 and < 4.0 |
| 4.5 | 5.0 | >= 4.0 and <= 4.5 |

**Sizing decision rule:** Select stent diameter based on the **larger vessel diameter** (proximal or distal reference vessel). For vessels with different proximal/distal diameters, size for the larger vessel. Select a stent with working length (WL) at least **8 mm longer** than the aneurysm neck to maintain a minimum of **4 mm on each side** of the aneurysm neck along the parent vessel. The stent is not designed to treat aneurysms with a neck > 22 mm.

**Foreshortening:** Up to 6.3% after deployment.

**Coiling microcatheter OD:** Must not exceed the maximum OD of the stent delivery microcatheter. Standard interventional devices with distal tips > 1.8 F (0.60 mm / 0.024 in) may not be able to pass through the interstices of the stent.

## Indications

- Patients >= 18 years of age
- Anterior circulation of the neurovasculature
- Saccular, wide-necked intracranial aneurysms: neck width >= 4 mm OR dome-to-neck ratio < 2
- Parent vessel diameter >= 2.0 mm and <= 4.5 mm
- Used with neurovascular embolization coils (stent-assisted coiling)

## Contraindications

- Parent vessel size outside the indicated range
- Antiplatelet and/or anticoagulation therapy (e.g., aspirin and clopidogrel) is contraindicated
- Patient has not received anti-platelet agents prior to stent implantation
- Active bacterial infection
- Anatomy not appropriate for endovascular treatment due to:
  - Severe intracranial vessel tortuosity or stenosis
  - Intracranial vasospasm not responsive to medical therapy
- Pre-existing stent in the parent artery at the target intracranial aneurysm location

## Relevant Protocol

**Antiplatelet therapy:** Typical antiplatelet and anticoagulation regimen used for interventional intracranial procedures is recommended at the discretion of the treating physician. DAPT required prior to implantation. Do not use in patients in whom antiplatelet and/or anticoagulation therapy is contraindicated.

**Ruptured aneurysms:** Do NOT use device to treat patients with ruptured intracranial aneurysms within a minimum of 30 days from aneurysm rupture.

**Coiling:** Can be performed prior to or after stent deployment per physician preference (jailing technique or sequential). When multiple aneurysms are to be treated, start at the most distal aneurysm first.

**Minimum landing zone:** 4 mm of stent on each side of the aneurysm neck.

## Deployment/Use Step-by-Step

### Initial Access and Stent Selection
1. Gain vascular access per standard practice. Select recommended microcatheter (Excelsior SL-10, 0.0165 in / 0.42 mm ID or Excelsior XT-17, 0.017 in / 0.43 mm ID). Consider an intermediate catheter for additional stability.
2. Navigate the stent delivery microcatheter over an access-length guidewire at least 1.2 cm distal to the aneurysm neck. The microcatheter tip must be placed sufficiently distal to allow for slack removal after stent advancement while maintaining adequate stent length (~4 mm) distal to the aneurysm neck.
3. Remove the guidewire.
4. Select a Neuroform Atlas Stent based on the largest reference vessel diameter and sizing tables. Select a stent with WL at least 8 mm longer than the aneurysm neck.

### Delivery System Preparation and Stent Transfer
5. Inspect the stent delivery system packaging for damage.
6. Peel open the pouch aseptically and remove the sterile dispenser hoop.
7. Place the dispenser hoop into the sterile field.
8. Using two hands (one on each side of the wire retention clip), release the stent delivery wire from the clip on the dispenser hoop.
9. Remove the device by grabbing the stent delivery wire and the proximal end of the introducer sheath; hold them together and slowly remove the entire wire and sheath. Hold together during removal to prevent premature deployment.
10. Inspect the stent delivery system. Confirm the tip of the delivery wire is entirely within the introducer sheath, the wire is not kinked, and the sheath tip is not damaged.
11. Partially insert the distal end of the introducer sheath into the RHV connected to the microcatheter. Tighten the RHV to secure.
12. Open the y-connector valve of the RHV and verify that flush solution exits the proximal end of the introducer sheath.
13. Loosen the RHV, advance the introducer sheath until its tip is fully inserted into the microcatheter hub. Tighten the RHV firmly. Confirm no air bubbles are in the system.
14. Advance the stent delivery wire to transfer the stent from the introducer sheath into the microcatheter. Ensure the introducer sheath does not move during advancement.
15. Continue advancing until the distal edge of the fluoro saver mark enters the introducer sheath. The fluoro saver mark is 135 cm from the delivery wire distal tip. At this point, the stent is approximately 90 cm inside the microcatheter.
16. Loosen the RHV, remove the introducer sheath from the proximal end of the delivery wire while holding the wire fixed. Set the sheath aside.
17. If desired, place the torque device on the proximal end of wire (at least 5 cm from proximal end of fluoro saver marker). The torque device is for handling/stabilization only -- do not use to torque the wire.
18. Slowly advance the delivery wire and stent until the distal edge of the fluoro saver mark reaches the stent delivery microcatheter's RHV.

### Stent Positioning and Deployment
19. Under fluoroscopy, advance the stent delivery wire until the stent's distal radiopaque markers are 1-2 mm proximal to the distal tip marker of the stent delivery microcatheter.
20. Withdraw the microcatheter slightly to remove any slack and position the stent radiopaque markers across the target aneurysm. Maintain approximately 4 mm of stent on each side of the aneurysm neck.
21. If positioning is satisfactory, carefully retract the stent delivery microcatheter in a continuous movement while maintaining the position of the stent delivery wire. The stent's distal radiopaque markers will expand as the stent exits the microcatheter. Do NOT use the delivery wire to push the stent out.
22. Confirm deployed stent position using fluoroscopy.
23. If the stent did not adequately cover the aneurysm, withdraw the stent delivery wire from the microcatheter. Place additional Neuroform Atlas Stents as needed (telescoping).
24. Once the aneurysm is adequately covered, remove the stent delivery wire and microcatheter. Establish hemostasis.
25. Perform coiling procedure per appropriate coiling device DFU.
26. Discard used devices.

## Bailout / Troubleshooting

**Q: What is the optimal position of the stent with respect to the aneurysm?**
A: Position the stent so each end is secured in relatively straight areas of the parent vessel. Each end should be anchored in at least 4 mm of normal vessel. For supraclinoid ICA aneurysms, it may be better to deploy the distal end in the M1 (MCA first segment) than in a short segment between the aneurysm and the ICA bifurcation. Use a view that best shows the parent vessel distal to the aneurysm for accurate distal end deployment.

**Q: Which stent size for a vessel with different proximal and distal diameters (e.g., 4.0 mm ICA to 2.5 mm MCA)?**
A: Choose the stent sized for the larger vessel. In this example, choose the 4.0 or 4.5 mm stent. It can be deployed safely in the smaller MCA and will be well anchored in the ICA terminus.

**Q: I accidentally started deploying the stent in the wrong location. What should I do?**
A: Do NOT try to reposition the stent. The safest course is to continue deploying the stent where it is, then deploy a second stent at the desired location. Safely deploying a stent, even in an undesired location, will minimize vascular injury. Animal studies show stent endothelialization in < 30 days.

**Q: The stent deployed with one end adjacent to the aneurysm rather than in the normal parent vessel.**
A: Remove the spent stent delivery wire while maintaining the microcatheter position. Deploy a second Neuroform Atlas Stent starting from inside the first stent to the normal portion of the parent vessel (telescoping fashion). The second stent should be the same diameter or larger.

**Q: Excessive resistance is encountered during stent manipulation.**
A: Do not apply undue force. Withdraw the microcatheter, stent, and stent delivery wire as a unit and repeat the procedure with new devices.

**Q: The stent delivery microcatheter and delivery wire -- can they be used to recapture the stent?**
A: No. The stent delivery microcatheter and the Neuroform Atlas Stent delivery wire should NOT be used to recapture the stent. Once deployment begins, it cannot be reversed.

## Effectiveness

### ATLAS Trial (N=182 mITT, anterior circulation, 12-month follow-up)

**Primary effectiveness endpoint:** Raymond Class 1 (complete aneurysm occlusion) at 12 months in the absence of retreatment or significant parent artery stenosis (>50%).

- mITT population (regression imputation): **84.7%** (95% CI: 78.6%-90.9%), p < 0.001
- Completed endpoint cohort: 84.5% (131/155, 95% CI: 77.8%-89.8%)
- Worst case analysis (all missing = failure): 72.0% (131/182, 95% CI: 64.9%-78.4%)

**Secondary effectiveness endpoints (12 months, N=153 with DSA):**

| Endpoint | Result |
|---|---|
| Raymond Class 1 | 88.2% (135/153) |
| Raymond Class 2 | 7.8% (12/153) |
| Raymond Class 3 | 3.9% (6/153) |
| Raymond Class 1 + 2 combined | 96.1% (147/153) |
| Recanalization | 5.8% (9/154) |
| Parent artery stenosis > 50% | 1.3% (2/153) |
| Stent migration | 0.0% (0/153) |
| Retreatment | 3.8% (7/182) |

**Baseline aneurysm characteristics (N=182):**

- Mean dome-to-neck ratio: 1.2 +/- 0.3
- Previously ruptured: 12.1% (22/182)

## MRI Safety

**MR Conditional** (alone, overlapped with a second stent, and adjacent to a Stryker Neurovascular coil mass). Safe immediately after placement under the following conditions:

- Static magnetic field of 1.5 and 3.0 Tesla
- Maximum spatial gradient field up to 2500 gauss/cm (25 Tesla/m)
- Maximum MR system reported whole body averaged SAR of 2 W/kg (Normal Operating Mode) and head averaged SAR of 3.2 W/kg

**Temperature rise:**
- Maximum 4 degrees C after 15 minutes of continuous scanning

**Migration:** The Neuroform Atlas Stent should not migrate in this MRI environment.

**Image artifact:**
- Extends approximately 2 mm from the stent when imaged with a spin echo pulse sequence on a 3-Tesla MRI system
- Artifact may obscure the device lumen. May need to optimize MR imaging parameters.
