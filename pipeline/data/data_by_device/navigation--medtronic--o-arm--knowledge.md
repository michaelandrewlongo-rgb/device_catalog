# O-arm Surgical Imaging System (Medtronic)

**Manufacturer:** Medtronic (originally Breakaway Imaging, acquired by Medtronic)
**Category:** Navigation / Intraoperative Imaging (Capital Equipment)

## What It Is

The O-arm is a mobile intraoperative cone-beam CT imaging system designed primarily for spine surgery. It is a gantry-based system that opens to position around the patient on the operating table, then closes to perform a 360-degree rotation scan, producing 3D CT-like volumetric images (cone-beam CT). The O-arm integrates with the Medtronic StealthStation navigation system, providing the 3D dataset used for real-time instrument tracking during navigated spine procedures. It is capital equipment, not an implant. The current generation is the O-arm O2, which offers improved image quality and lower-dose scan protocols compared to the original O-arm.

## Sizing/Specs

### System Specifications
- **Imaging modality:** Cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT)
- **Detector:** Flat-panel detector (amorphous silicon)
- **Gantry design:** Open gantry that splits to position around the patient, then closes for scanning
- **Scan modes:** 3D volumetric (standard dose, low dose, high definition), 2D fluoroscopy (AP, lateral, oblique)
- **3D field of view:** Approximately 20 cm x 15 cm (covers roughly 6-8 vertebral levels per acquisition)
- **Scan time:** Approximately 13 seconds for a full 3D acquisition (O-arm O2)
- **Spatial resolution:** Sub-millimeter (refer to Medtronic technical documentation for exact specifications per scan mode)
- **Weight:** Approximately 770 kg (1,700 lbs) for the O-arm O2 gantry
- **Power:** Standard hospital electrical supply

### O-arm O2 Improvements (Current Generation)
- Enhanced image quality with improved flat-panel detector
- Low-dose scan protocols (reduced radiation compared to original O-arm)
- Faster image reconstruction
- Improved 2D fluoroscopy mode
- Smaller footprint and improved mobility

### Radiation Dose
- Single 3D scan delivers radiation roughly equivalent to a limited CT scan of the spine
- Low-dose protocols available (reduced mAs) with acceptable image quality for navigation registration
- Follow ALARA principles; minimize re-spins

## Indications

- Intraoperative 3D imaging for navigation-guided pedicle screw placement (thoracic, lumbar, cervical)
- Pre-instrumentation scan for StealthStation navigation registration (automatic registration with reference frame in field of view)
- Post-instrumentation verification scan to confirm screw position before wound closure
- Intraoperative imaging for interbody fusion, deformity correction, tumor resection, and trauma fixation
- 2D fluoroscopy for standard intraoperative imaging (can replace a separate C-arm)
- Any spine procedure where intraoperative 3D cross-sectional imaging is desired

## Contraindications

- Patient body habitus exceeding the gantry aperture (the open gantry must close around the patient and table)
- Procedures where the additional radiation exposure is not justified by clinical benefit
- OR environments without adequate space for the O-arm gantry and StealthStation console
- Not indicated for diagnostic imaging outside the operative setting (it is a surgical imaging device, not a diagnostic CT scanner)
- Standard contraindications for ionizing radiation apply (pregnancy, unless benefit outweighs risk)

## Compatible With

- **Navigation platform:** Medtronic StealthStation (primary integration partner); the O-arm transfers 3D datasets directly to the StealthStation for automatic registration and navigation
- **Robotic platform:** Medtronic Mazor X Stealth Edition (O-arm provides the imaging for Mazor X preoperative planning and intraoperative registration)
- **Operating tables:** Compatible with standard radiolucent spine surgery tables; the gantry must be able to position around the table and patient
- **Implant systems:** Implant-agnostic (provides imaging only; compatible with any spine implant system used with StealthStation navigation)
- **Draping:** Dedicated sterile drapes for the gantry are available to maintain the sterile field during scanning

## Use Notes

- **Typical workflow:** (1) Position patient and attach reference frame to spinous process. (2) Roll the O-arm into position, close the gantry around the patient. (3) Acquire a pre-instrumentation 3D scan. The dataset transfers automatically to the StealthStation, and registration is immediate because the reference frame was in the field of view during the scan. (4) Navigate and place instrumentation. (5) Optionally acquire a post-instrumentation verification scan to confirm screw position before closure.
- The O-arm replaces the need for a separate C-arm in most cases. It provides both 2D fluoroscopy and 3D volumetric imaging in a single device.
- Automatic registration eliminates manual point-matching or surface-tracing, saving time and reducing a source of error.
- Image quality is not equivalent to a diagnostic CT scanner. Cone-beam CT has more scatter artifact and lower soft-tissue contrast than multi-detector CT. It is adequate for bony anatomy and navigation registration but not for soft-tissue diagnosis.
- Metal artifact from prior hardware degrades 3D image quality. In revision cases, expect some artifact around existing implants.
- The gantry must be draped or positioned to avoid contaminating the sterile field. Coordinate with the scrub team on draping protocol.
- Each 3D scan delivers a meaningful radiation dose. Limit to 1-2 scans per case when possible (one for registration, one for verification).
- The O-arm requires dedicated OR space and staff training. It is a significant capital investment.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

| Feature | Medtronic O-arm O2 | Ziehm Vision FD 3D | Siemens Cios Spin | Standard 2D C-arm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3D imaging | Yes (cone-beam CT, 360-degree) | Yes (cone-beam CT, 190-degree) | Yes (cone-beam CT, 190-degree) | No (2D fluoroscopy only) |
| Full 360-degree rotation | Yes | No (limited arc) | No (limited arc) | No |
| Image quality (3D) | Higher (full rotation, flat-panel) | Moderate (partial rotation) | Moderate (partial rotation) | N/A |
| Navigation integration | Direct integration with StealthStation | Compatible with multiple nav platforms | Compatible with multiple nav platforms | Manual registration required |
| Robotic integration | Yes (Mazor X Stealth Edition) | No direct robotic integration | No direct robotic integration | No |
| 2D fluoroscopy | Yes (built-in) | Yes (primary function) | Yes (primary function) | Yes (primary function) |
| Gantry design | Opens/closes around patient | Standard C-arm | Standard C-arm | Standard C-arm |
| Size/weight | Large, heavy (~770 kg) | Moderate | Moderate | Smaller, lighter |
| Cost | High (dedicated capital equipment) | Moderate | Moderate | Lower |

- The O-arm's primary advantage is its 360-degree rotation, which produces higher-quality 3D images than partial-arc C-arm systems. This matters most for navigation accuracy and post-instrumentation verification.
- The deep integration with StealthStation and Mazor X is a lock-in factor: if the facility uses Medtronic navigation and/or robotics, the O-arm is the natural imaging choice.
- Ziehm and Siemens 3D C-arms are more versatile (usable for non-spine cases, more portable) and less expensive, but produce lower-quality 3D images due to limited arc rotation.
- A standard 2D C-arm provides no 3D capability. Navigation with a 2D C-arm requires manual registration to preoperative CT, which is slower and less accurate.
