# CAPSTONE PEEK Spinal System (Medtronic)

**Manufacturer:** Medtronic
**Category:** Cervical Cage

## What It Is

The CAPSTONE is a PEEK-OPTIMA polymer interbody spacer for anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF). The cage features an open central graft window for bone packing and serrated endplate surfaces to resist migration. It is designed as a standalone spacer that requires supplemental anterior cervical plate fixation.

Two configurations are available:
- **CAPSTONE PEEK:** Standard radiolucent PEEK-OPTIMA construction. Provides artifact-free imaging on CT and MRI for fusion assessment.
- **CAPSTONE with Titanium Endplates:** PEEK body with titanium alloy endplate inserts. The titanium surfaces enhance radiographic visibility on plain films and may promote osseointegration at the bone-implant interface.

The cage is implanted via a standard open anterior cervical approach and packed with autogenous or allogenic bone graft before insertion.

## Sizing/Specs

### Dimensions
- **Heights:** 5mm, 6mm, 7mm, 8mm, 9mm, 10mm, 11mm (refer to surgical technique guide for complete sizing matrix)
- **Lordotic angles:** 0° (parallel) and 6° lordotic
- **Footprints:** Multiple anteroposterior and mediolateral dimensions available to match vertebral body anatomy

### Materials
- **Cage body:** PEEK-OPTIMA polymer (radiolucent)
- **Endplate option:** Titanium alloy (Ti-6Al-4V) endplate inserts
- **Radiopaque markers:** Embedded markers for radiographic identification

### Design Features
- Open central graft window for autograft or allograft bone packing
- Serrated superior and inferior endplate contact surfaces for initial stability
- Bullnose anterior profile to facilitate insertion
- Provided sterile, single use

## Indications

- Anterior cervical interbody fusion (ACDF) at one or two contiguous levels from C3 to T1
- Degenerative disc disease confirmed by history and radiographic studies
- Cervical radiculopathy or myelopathy from disc herniation or spondylosis
- Must be used with supplemental anterior cervical plate fixation
- Must be used with autogenous and/or allogenic bone graft
- Patients must be skeletally mature

## Contraindications

- Active infection at the operative site
- Severe osteoporosis or inadequate bone stock (risk of subsidence and poor fixation)
- Known allergy to PEEK or titanium materials
- Conditions precluding benefit of spinal implant surgery
- Previously fused levels at the target segment
- Skeletal immaturity
- Mental or neuromuscular disorders creating unacceptable risk of fixation failure or noncompliance

## Compatible With

- **Supplemental fixation (required):** Anterior cervical plate system cleared for cervical use (e.g., Medtronic Atlantis Vision Elite, or other compatible plate). The CAPSTONE does not provide standalone fixation.
- **Bone graft:** Autogenous cancellous bone, allograft, or bone graft substitutes packed into the open graft window
- **Cervical retractor systems:** Standard anterior cervical retractor setups (Caspar-type distraction pins, self-retaining retractors)
- **Imaging:** Intraoperative fluoroscopy (AP and lateral). The PEEK body is radiolucent, allowing artifact-free CT and MRI assessment of the fusion zone. Titanium endplate variant produces minimal artifact.
- **Dedicated instrument set:** Medtronic CAPSTONE instrument tray including trials, inserter, and impactor

## Use Notes

- PEEK construction provides the primary imaging advantage: artifact-free CT and MRI allow direct visualization of bone graft incorporation through the cage window. This is a significant benefit over titanium cages for postoperative fusion assessment.
- The titanium endplate variant sacrifices some imaging clarity for potential osseointegration benefit at the bone-implant interface. Choose based on clinical priority (imaging vs. integration).
- Requires supplemental anterior cervical plate in all cases. This adds surgical steps and anterior profile compared to zero-profile or integrated-fixation alternatives.
- Height selection is critical. Undersizing risks subsidence and loss of lordosis; oversizing risks endplate fracture and overdistraction.
- The 6° lordotic option helps restore segmental lordosis in patients with kyphotic deformity. The 0° option is appropriate when existing lordosis is adequate.
- Pack graft firmly into the open window before insertion. Graft containment depends on the cage geometry and endplate contact.
- Preserve subchondral endplate bone during preparation to resist subsidence. PEEK is less stiff than titanium, which may reduce stress shielding but does not eliminate subsidence risk.
- Counsel smokers regarding increased nonunion risk.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **vs. Tritanium C (Stryker):** The Tritanium C is a porous 3D-printed titanium cage. CAPSTONE is PEEK with optional titanium endplates. CAPSTONE provides far superior postoperative imaging (artifact-free CT/MRI). Tritanium C offers osseointegration via porous titanium surface. Both require supplemental anterior plating.
- **vs. Zero-P (DePuy):** The Zero-P integrates fixation into the cage, eliminating the need for an anterior plate and reducing dysphagia risk. CAPSTONE requires a separate plate. CAPSTONE offers artifact-free imaging; Zero-P has titanium plate/screw components that partially obscure the fusion zone on plain films.
- **vs. COALITION (Globus):** The COALITION is 3D-printed titanium with optional integrated fixation, potentially eliminating the plate. CAPSTONE is PEEK requiring a plate. COALITION offers bone through-growth via porous titanium; CAPSTONE offers cleaner imaging. COALITION's integrated fixation is a workflow advantage over the CAPSTONE + plate construct.
- **Material tradeoff (PEEK vs. titanium):** PEEK cages (CAPSTONE, Zero-P spacer) have a modulus of elasticity closer to bone, theoretically reducing stress shielding. Titanium cages (Tritanium C, COALITION) are stiffer but offer direct bone integration via porous surfaces. PEEK provides artifact-free imaging; titanium obscures the fusion zone.
