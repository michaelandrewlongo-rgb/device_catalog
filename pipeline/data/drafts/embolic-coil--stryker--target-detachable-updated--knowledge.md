# Target Detachable Coils -- Stryker Neurovascular

**Manufacturer:** Stryker Neurovascular
**Category:** Embolic coil

FDA 510(k) K153658 | Cleared May 19, 2016 | Class II (primary clearance)
FDA 510(k) K222533 | Cleared December 14, 2022 | Class II (Target Tetra)
Predicate: K123377

## What It Is

Platinum-tungsten detachable embolization coils for endovascular occlusion of intracranial aneurysms and vascular malformations. All coils incorporate an internal multi-strand stretch-resistant member. Detachment is electrolytic via the InZone Detachment System (sold separately).

The family spans four shape categories -- 360, Helical, 3D, and Tetra -- each optimized for a different packing role:

- **Target 360:** Framing coil. Smaller distal loop (75% of main loop diameter) facilitates initial aneurysm sac placement. Available in Nano, Ultra, Soft, and Standard stiffness grades, plus XL 360 variants (Soft, Standard).
- **Target Helical:** Filling/finishing coil. Classic helical secondary shape for dense packing. Available in Nano and Ultra stiffness grades, plus XL Helical variant.
- **Target 3D:** Three-dimensional random secondary shape for mid-packing.
- **Target Tetra:** Introduced 2022. Combines a new geometric shape with enhanced softness, designed to provide both framing geometry and soft filling behavior in a single coil. Intended to simplify coil selection during packing.

## Sizing/Specs

- **Coil material:** Platinum-tungsten alloy coil on stainless steel delivery wire
- **Stretch resistance:** Multi-strand stretch-resistant member through center of coil
- **Specific diameter and length ranges:** Enumerated in wall chart AP004356 v3.0 and family DFU; refer to product label/IFU for current sizing matrix
- **Stiffness grades:** Nano (softest), Ultra, Soft, Standard (firmest) -- not all grades available across all shapes

## Indications

Target Detachable Coils are intended to endovascularly obstruct or occlude blood flow in vascular abnormalities of the neurovascular and peripheral vessels. Indicated for:

- Endovascular embolization of intracranial aneurysms
- Other neurovascular abnormalities such as arteriovenous malformations and arteriovenous fistulae
- Arterial and venous embolizations in the peripheral vasculature

## Contraindications

Not specifically enumerated in available 510(k) summaries. Refer to current IFU for full list. General contraindications for embolic coils apply: anatomy incompatible with endovascular access, known allergy to device materials.

## Compatible With

- **Detachment system:** Stryker Neurovascular InZone Detachment System (required; sold separately)
- **Delivery:** Via standard microcatheter access (specific catheter inner diameter compatibility per IFU and sizing chart)

## Deployment/Use

- Coil is advanced through microcatheter to target aneurysm or target vessel
- InZone system applies electrolytic current to detach coil from delivery wire
- K153658 iteration features dimensional changes resulting in shorter detachment time vs. predicate
- Standard coiling technique: frame with 360 coil, pack with Helical or 3D coils, finish with soft/nano grades or Tetra
- Target Tetra is positioned as a versatile mid-to-finish coil that reduces the need to switch between shape types during packing

## MRI Safety

- MRI testing performed per ASTM F2052 (displacement force), F2213 (torque), F2182 (RF heating), F2119 (artifact)
- Passed testing for both neurovascular and peripheral use configurations
- Conditional/unconditional designation: refer to current IFU -- do not rely on this summary for pre-procedure MRI screening decisions

## Complications to Know

Not specifically enumerated in 510(k) summaries. Standard embolic coil risks apply: coil migration, vessel perforation, thromboembolic events, parent artery occlusion, coil stretching or fracture, incomplete occlusion, aneurysm recurrence.
