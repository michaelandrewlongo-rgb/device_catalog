# FRED Flow Diverter (MicroVention/Terumo)

**Manufacturer:** MicroVention, Inc. (Terumo)
**Category:** Flow diverter
**Source:** https://www.terumoneuro.com/products/fred-family

## What It Is

The FRED (Flow Re-Direction Endoluminal Device) is a self-expanding nickel titanium (nitinol) flow diverter with an integrated dual-layer coverage design. The inner high-density mesh focuses flow diversion at the aneurysm neck while the outer stent provides scaffolding and vessel wall apposition. It has 4 distal and 4 proximal radiopaque markers plus interweaved helical marker strands for fluoroscopic visibility. Available in 7 diameters (2.5-5.5 mm) with working lengths from 7-39 mm. The FRED-21 system (2.5 and 3.0 mm) uses the Headway 21 microcatheter; the FRED-27 system (3.5-5.5 mm) uses the Headway 27 microcatheter.

Key differentiator vs. Pipeline: dual-layer braid design, resheathing capability (up to 3 times), and Headway microcatheter compatibility.

**Sizing decision rule:** Size the FRED to the parent vessel diameter (2.0-5.0 mm range). The implant foreshortens up to 60% as it expands to vessel diameter. Account for foreshortening when selecting length. Examine pre-treatment angiograms for correct and accurate vessel measurements. Ensure adequate implant length on each side of the aneurysm neck for appropriate coverage.

## Indications

- Adults >= 22 years old
- Wide-necked intracranial aneurysms: neck width >= 4 mm OR dome-to-neck ratio < 2
- Saccular or fusiform aneurysms
- Parent vessel diameter 2.0-5.0 mm
- Internal carotid artery from petrous segment to terminus
- Endovascular treatment of unruptured aneurysms (safety/effectiveness NOT established for ruptured aneurysms)

## Contraindications

- Anticoagulant, anti-platelet, or thrombolytic therapy is contraindicated in the patient
- Known hypersensitivity to nickel-titanium or metal jewelry
- Anatomy does not permit passage or deployment of the FRED System
- Active bacterial infection
- Pre-existing stent in place at the target aneurysm
- Parent vessel size outside the indicated range (2.0-5.0 mm)
- Patient has NOT received dual anti-platelet agents prior to the procedure

## Relevant Protocol

**Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT):** Required prior to the procedure. Patients who have not received DAPT are contraindicated.

**Anticoagulation during procedure:** Standard heparinized flush solution required. Maintain continuous heparinized saline flush through the microcatheter.

**C7 ICA segment caution:** Decreased complete occlusion rates observed in communicating segment (C7) of ICA -- 52.6% (10/19) at 1 year in the FRED IDE study. Carefully consider:
1. Observed PComm of fetal origin (small/hypoplastic/absent P1 with PCA fed by ICA)
2. PComm branch arising from the aneurysm neck
3. PComm branch arising from the dome of the aneurysm

**Post-procedure monitoring:** Follow-up imaging at 30 days, 180 days, and 12 months post-procedure.

## Deployment/Use Step-by-Step

1. Gain vascular access per standard angiographic practice. Perform diagnostic angiogram to confirm vessel diameter and document target aneurysm.
2. Place appropriate guide or intermediate catheter per standard practice.
3. Coaxially navigate the Headway 27 (FRED-27) or Headway 21 (FRED-21) microcatheter over a guidewire distal to the aneurysm neck or target location. Remove the guidewire.
4. Maintain flush through the microcatheter per standard endovascular practice.
5. Select an appropriate-sized FRED System based on parent vessel/aneurysm neck size.
6. Note foreshortening: FRED foreshortens up to 60% as it expands. Account for this when sizing.
7. Inspect the package for damage. Peel open the pouch aseptically and place the dispenser coil into the sterile field.
8. Unclip the molded cap from the dispenser coil. Pull on the proximal end of the delivery wire until the introducer exits the dispenser coil. Hold the delivery wire and introducer together while removing the entire device.
9. In a bowl of saline, partially deploy the FRED implant up to 5 mm or 50% (whichever comes first) -- do not detach the implant from the distal introducer tip.
10. Check for: distal marker uniformity, even displacement with no entanglement, smooth tracking through introducer.
11. WARNING: DO NOT FULLY DEPLOY the FRED System at this point.
12. Hydrate the FRED implant in the bowl of saline. Carefully pull back on the delivery wire to fully retrieve the implant and wire tip within the introducer.
13. If any defect is observed, DO NOT CONTINUE. Return the unit.
14. Confirm the device is entirely within the introducer, the delivery wire tip is not kinked, and the introducer tip is not damaged.
15. Partially insert the distal end of the introducer into the RHV connected to the compatible Headway microcatheter. Tighten the RHV. Flush the RHV with sterile saline, verifying fluid exits the proximal end of the introducer (hydrating the introducer).
16. WARNING: Purge the system carefully to avoid air introduction.
17. Untighten the RHV and advance the introducer until fully engaged with the microcatheter hub. Re-tighten.
18. Advance the delivery wire to transfer the FRED System from the introducer into the microcatheter.
19. WARNING: Do not torque the delivery wire while advancing or retracting.
20. Continue advancing the delivery wire into the microcatheter until the proximal tip of the delivery wire enters the introducer. Loosen the RHV, remove the introducer, set aside.
21. Fluoroscopy may be used up to this point at physician's discretion.
22. WARNING: Do not apply undue force. If resistance is encountered, withdraw the unit and select a new FRED System.
23. Carefully advance until the device exit marker approaches the RHV. Initiate fluoroscopic guidance.
24. Position the FRED System by aligning the distal radiopaque end markers past the aneurysm neck, allowing for adequate distal and proximal landing zones.
25. Use a slow, proper push/pull technique: delivery wire push force + opposing microcatheter withdrawal force to remove excess catheter slack. Maintain the microcatheter tip in the center of the parent vessel.
26. CAUTION: Rapid microcatheter withdrawal is NOT recommended -- may cause device elongation or improper deployment.
27. If positioning is unsatisfactory, the implant may be recaptured if not fully deployed (up to the point where the distal-most wire marker is aligned approximately 50% of length proximal to the microcatheter distal marker band).
28. CAUTION: If resistance during recapture, withdraw microcatheter slightly to unsheath, then attempt again. Maximum 3 redeployments.
29. If positioning is satisfactory, advance the delivery wire while retracting the microcatheter to deploy across the neck. Ensure proximal radiopaque end markers are proximal to the aneurysm neck.
30. The FRED will expand and may foreshorten up to 60%. Visually verify opening of proximal end.
31. Inspect the deployed FRED under fluoroscopy for complete opening and vessel wall apposition. If not fully open/apposed or kinked, consider using a micro guidewire and/or occlusion balloon catheter.
32. Withdraw and discard all accessory devices. CAUTION: watch distal and proximal markers when passing through the implanted device to avoid displacement.

## Bailout / Troubleshooting

**Q: The FRED is not fully open or apposed to the vessel wall after deployment. What do I do?**
A: Consider using a suitable micro guidewire and/or occlusion balloon catheter to fully open the implant. Confirm under fluoroscopy.

**Q: I need to recapture/reposition the FRED. Is this possible?**
A: Yes, if it is not fully deployed. The implant may be recaptured until the distal-most wire marker (collocated distal to the implant proximal markers) is aligned approximately 50% of constrained length proximal to the microcatheter distal marker band. Maximum 3 redeployments. If resistance is felt, withdraw the microcatheter slightly to unsheath, then attempt again. Do NOT continue to recapture if resistance persists.

**Q: I encounter unusual resistance during delivery or access.**
A: Remove the introducer/guide catheter/microcatheter and FRED System as a single unit. Applying excessive force can result in device loss or damage. Verify the microcatheter is not kinked or in extremely tortuous anatomy. Confirm the microcatheter does not ovalize. Confirm adequate heparinized flush solution.

**Q: Can I reposition the FRED after deployment/detachment?**
A: No. Do not attempt to reposition the FRED implant after deployment/detachment. Do not reposition in the parent vessel without fully retrieving the device. The FRED must be fully retrieved into the microcatheter and re-deployed or removed completely.

**Q: Can the delivery wire be used as a guidewire?**
A: No. The FRED System delivery wire should not be used as a guidewire. Do not torque the FRED System. A torque device should not be used.

## Complications to Know

### FRED IDE Pivotal Study (N=145 mITT, 12-month follow-up)

**Primary Safety Endpoint (death or major stroke within 30 days or neurologic death or major ipsilateral stroke within 12 months):**
- mITT population: 6.2% (9/145), posterior mean 6.8% (95% CI: 3.3%-11.3%)
- ICA population: 5.9% (8/135), posterior mean 6.6% (95% CI: 3.1%-11.3%)

**Additional warnings:**

- Benefits may not outweigh risks for small and medium stable asymptomatic extradural aneurysms (including cavernous ICA)

## Effectiveness

### FRED IDE Pivotal Study (ICA Population, N=135)

**Primary effectiveness endpoint:** Raymond-Roy I IA occlusion with <= 50% parent artery stenosis and no re-treatment at 12 months.

- Primary effectiveness success rate: 56.7% (posterior mean 56.6%, 95% CI: 48.2%-64.7%)
- Posterior probability of success rate > 46%: 0.993
- Worst-case analysis (without imputation): 54.8% (74/135)

**Number of devices per subject:**
- 1 device: 93.1% (135/145)
- 2 devices: 6.4% (9/145)
- 3 devices: 0.7% (1/145)

## MRI Safety

**MR Conditional.** Safe under the following conditions:

- Static magnetic field of 1.5-Tesla and 3-Tesla only
- Maximum spatial gradient magnetic field of 2,500 gauss/cm (25 T/m)
- Maximum MR system reported whole body averaged SAR of 2 W/kg for 15 minutes of scanning per pulse sequence in Normal Operating Mode

**Temperature rise:**
- Maximum 2.8 degrees C at 1.5T or 3.6 degrees C at 3T after 15 minutes of continuous scanning per pulse sequence

**Image artifact:**
- Extends approximately 4 mm from the device when imaged with a gradient echo pulse sequence on a 3T MR system
- May create local field inhomogeneity and susceptibility artifacts during MRA, potentially degrading diagnostic quality

**Recommendation:** Register MR conditions with MedicAlert Foundation or equivalent. Complete and provide the FRED patient implant card.
