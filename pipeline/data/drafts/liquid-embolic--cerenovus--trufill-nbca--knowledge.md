# TRUFILL n-BCA Liquid Embolic System

**Manufacturer:** Cerenovus (Johnson & Johnson MedTech)
**Category:** Liquid embolic agent
**Delivery catheter:** Prowler EX Microcatheter (standard; does not require detachable-tip or dual-lumen balloon microcatheters)

## What It Is

Adhesive liquid embolic agent composed of n-butyl cyanoacrylate (n-BCA) mixed with Ethiodized Oil and tantalum powder for embolization of cerebral arteriovenous malformations. Unlike non-adhesive EVOH-based systems, n-BCA polymerizes on contact with ionic fluids (blood), forming an instant adhesive cast. The operator controls polymerization speed by adjusting the ratio of n-BCA to Ethiodized Oil: higher oil concentration slows polymerization and allows deeper penetration; lower oil concentration accelerates polymerization for higher-flow or fistulous components. Tantalum powder concentration is also operator-adjustable and determines fluoroscopic radiopacity.

The system is available as a kit (n-BCA + Ethiodized Oil + tantalum powder + self-piercing cap) or a procedural set that adds the mixing and delivery accessories.

## Use Notes

**Polymerization customization:** Unlike Onyx (fixed-formulation vials), n-BCA concentration and tantalum loading are mixed by the operator immediately before use, allowing case-by-case adjustment of working time and radiopacity.

**Catheter retention:** Catheter retention rate was 1.6% in the pivotal RCT vs. 9.3% for Onyx LES. The adhesive nature of n-BCA requires prompt catheter withdrawal immediately after injection; delayed withdrawal increases adhesion risk.

**Procedure efficiency:** In the pivotal RCT, TRUFILL demonstrated significantly shorter fluoroscopy time (37 vs. 57 min, p<0.0001) and total procedural time (2.1 vs. 2.6 hrs, p=0.001) compared to Onyx LES.

**Cost:** Lower total procedural cost than Onyx LES because standard (non-detachable-tip, non-balloon) microcatheters are used.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

Primary competitor: Onyx LES (Medtronic, EVOH-based, non-adhesive).

| Parameter | TRUFILL n-BCA | Onyx LES |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Adhesive; polymerizes on ionic contact | Non-adhesive; precipitates as DMSO dissipates |
| Formulation | Operator-mixed (variable oil ratio, variable tantalum) | Fixed-concentration vials (Onyx 18 or Onyx 34) |
| Solvent requirement | None (no DMSO priming required) | DMSO deadspace fill required before each injection |
| Catheter type required | Standard microcatheter (e.g., Prowler EX) | DMSO-compatible microcatheter (Marathon, Rebar, UltraFlow HPC) |
| Detachable-tip catheter needed | No | Commonly used to aid catheter retrieval |
| Catheter retention rate (RCT) | 1.6% | 9.3% |
| Fluoroscopy time (RCT) | 37 min | 57 min (p<0.0001) |
| Procedural time (RCT) | 2.1 hrs | 2.6 hrs (p=0.001) |
| Primary endpoint >= 50% reduction (RCT) | 84.3% (43/51) | 97.6% (41/42) |
| Embolization controllability | Faster, less controllable distal spread | Slower, more controlled with reflux tolerance |
| Garlic odor/taste | No | Yes (DMSO-related; expected) |

**RCT context:** The pivotal non-inferiority RCT (N=100 ITT) compared TRUFILL n-BCA (N=63 enrolled) vs. Onyx LES (N=54 enrolled) for presurgical bAVM embolization. Onyx met the primary endpoint at a higher rate (97.6% vs. 84.3%), but the study was powered to demonstrate non-inferiority of Onyx relative to TRUFILL (n-BCA was the control arm). TRUFILL showed numerically lower complication rates, though differences were not statistically significant. No statistically significant difference in surgical blood loss or resection time between groups.
