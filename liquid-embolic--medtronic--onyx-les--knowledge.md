# Onyx Liquid Embolic System (LES)

**Manufacturer:** Medtronic (Micro Therapeutics, Inc.)
**Category:** Liquid embolic agent
**Delivery catheter:** DMSO-compatible microcatheter (Marathon, Rebar, or UltraFlow HPC)

## What It Is

Non-adhesive liquid embolic agent composed of EVOH (ethylene vinyl alcohol) copolymer dissolved in DMSO (dimethyl sulfoxide), with suspended micronized tantalum powder for fluoroscopic visualization. When injected through a microcatheter into a bAVM, the DMSO dissipates into the blood, causing the EVOH copolymer and tantalum to precipitate in situ into a spongy, coherent embolus. The material forms a skin on the outside first and solidifies inward, allowing continued distal penetration during injection. Final solidification occurs within 5 minutes.

Available in two formulations based on EVOH concentration, which determines viscosity and penetration depth. The system includes a 1.5 mL vial of Onyx, a 1.5 mL vial of DMSO, and three 1 mL Onyx delivery syringes.

## Sizing

**Rule: Choose formulation based on nidus anatomy and feeder characteristics.**

| Formulation | EVOH Concentration | Nominal Viscosity (at 40C) | Best Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onyx 18 | 6% EVOH | 18 cSt | Feeding pedicle injections close to nidus; deeper nidal penetration |
| Onyx 34 | 8% EVOH | 33 cSt | Higher-flow fistulous components; larger vessels |

Onyx 18 travels more distally and penetrates deeper into the nidus due to its lower viscosity. Onyx 34 is recommended for embolizing higher flow and larger fistulous components.

**Vial volume:** 1.5 mL Onyx per vial. Multiple vials may be needed per session.

**Adjunctive coil use:** Consider coil placement prior to Onyx injection for feeding pedicles with AV fistulae having flow rates exceeding 200 mL/min and vessel diameters of 3 mm or greater.

## Indications

- Presurgical embolization of brain arteriovenous malformations (bAVMs)
- bAVM in the cerebral cortex, cerebellum, or dura mater
- Spetzler-Martin grade I-IV
- Patient is a candidate for surgical resection post-embolization

## Contraindications

- Optimal catheter placement is not achievable
- Provocative testing indicates intolerance to the occlusion procedure
- Vasospasm stops blood flow

## Relevant Protocol

**Anticoagulation/Sedation:**
- Verify adequate sedation is maintained throughout the entire embolization procedure. Patient movement during injection may result in embolization of an unintended vessel.
- Standard neurointerventional heparinization per institutional protocol.

**Injection rate:** 0.16 mL/min recommended (0.25 mL/90 sec). Do not exceed 0.3 mL/min. Use thumb pressure only. Palm-of-hand pressure risks catheter rupture from overpressurization.

**DMSO compatibility:** Use only MTI 1 mL syringes for DMSO and Onyx. Other syringes may not be DMSO-compatible and can cause catheter degradation and thromboembolic events.

**Mixing:** Shake Onyx on mixer at setting 8 for a minimum of 20 minutes. Continue mixing until ready to inject.

## Deployment/Use Step-by-Step

1. **Mix Onyx:** Place vial on Onyx mixer (Scientific Industries Genie 2, setting 8) for at least 20 minutes. Continue mixing until ready to inject per step 5. Failure to mix adequately results in tantalum settling and poor fluoroscopic visualization.
2. **Confirm catheter position:** Verify microcatheter tip is distal to any arterial vessels that supply normal brain tissue or cranial nerves. Confirm placement with contrast injection per institutional procedure.
3. **Flush catheter:** Flush contrast from microcatheter with 10 mL of saline. Leave the syringe connected.
4. **Fill catheter deadspace with DMSO:** Aspirate approximately 0.8 mL of DMSO into the yellow MTI 1 mL DMSO syringe. Inject DMSO into the delivery microcatheter in sufficient volume to fill the catheter deadspace. Refer to delivery catheter labeling for deadspace volume.
5. **Fill Onyx syringe:** Ensure Onyx has been mixed per step 1. Fill white MTI 1 mL syringe with Onyx through an 18 or 20 gauge needle. As soon as the DMSO has been injected into the catheter deadspace, remove the DMSO syringe, hold catheter hub vertically, and overfill and wash the luer hub with the balance of DMSO.
6. **Connect Onyx syringe:** Immediately connect Onyx syringe to the hub. Ensure no air in the hub during connection. Point the syringe vertically to create an interface between DMSO and Onyx. Premature solidification occurs if catheter luer contacts saline, blood, or contrast.
7. **Begin injection:** Hold syringe vertically. Inject Onyx to displace DMSO at 0.16 mL/min (do not exceed 0.3 mL/min). Use thumb pressure only. Maintain adequate fluoroscopic visualization at all times. If visualization is lost at any time, HALT delivery immediately.
8. **Continue injection:** Once Onyx passes through the catheter hub, hold syringe comfortably and continue at 0.16 mL/min. Monitor volume injected versus vascular space being filled. Do not allow more than 1 cm of Onyx reflux back over catheter tip.
9. **Terminate and remove catheter:** Upon completion, wait a few seconds, slightly aspirate the syringe, and gently pull the catheter to separate it from the Onyx cast.

## Bailout / Troubleshooting

**What if Onyx is not visualized exiting the catheter tip?**
- STOP injection immediately. Catheter may be occluded and over-pressurization/rupture can occur with as little as 0.05 mL of injection without visible flow.

**What if increased resistance to injection is felt?**
- STOP injection. Determine cause (e.g., Onyx occlusion in catheter lumen). Do NOT attempt to clear or overcome resistance by increasing pressure. Replace the catheter. Excessive pressure risks catheter rupture and embolization of unintended areas.

**What if injection is interrupted for more than 2 minutes?**
- Do NOT resume injection. Solidification of Onyx at the catheter tip may have occurred. Attempting to clear a solidified tip may cause catheter rupture.

**Catheter is stuck / difficult to remove -- what are the causes?**
- Distal bAVM angioarchitecture (very distal, afferent, lengthened, tortuous pedicles)
- Vasospasm
- Excessive Onyx reflux over the catheter tip

**Standard retrieval technique for a stuck catheter:**
- Carefully pull to assess resistance.
- If resistance is felt, remove any "slack" in the catheter.
- Gently apply traction (3-4 cm of stretch).
- Hold traction for a few seconds, then release. Assess vasculature for hemorrhage risk.
- Repeat intermittently until catheter is retrieved.

**Alternate technique (wrist snap):**
- Remove all slack; create slight tension in the system.
- Firmly hold the catheter and pull using a quick wrist snap motion (left to right) over 10-15 cm to break free from the Onyx cast.
- Do not apply more than 20 cm of traction to minimize risk of catheter separation.

**Catheter is truly entrapped and cannot be retrieved?**
- In difficult clinical situations, it may be safer to leave the microcatheter in the vascular system rather than risk rupturing the malformation.
- Stretch the catheter and cut the shaft near the entry point of vascular access, allowing the entry point to remain in the artery.
- If the catheter breaks during removal, distal migration or coiling of the catheter may occur. Consider same-day surgical resection to minimize thrombosis risk.

**Garlic taste or odor from DMSO?**
- Expected. Warn the patient preoperatively. The taste may last several hours; odor on breath and skin may be present. This is not a complication.

## Complications to Know

From the pivotal RCT comparing Onyx LES (N=54) vs. TRUFILL n-BCA (N=63):

| Complication | Onyx Rate |
|---|---|
| Death | 5.6% (3/54) |
| Worsening neurologic status | 64.8% (persistent 29.6%, resolved 35.2%) |
| Headache/nausea/vomiting | 83.3% |
| Intracranial hemorrhage | 24.1% |
| Stroke | 7.4% |
| Seizures | 1.9% |
| Vasospasm | 1.9% |
| Delivery catheter removal difficulty | 9.3% (6 events in 5 patients) |
| Poor penetration/visualization | 9.3% |
| Failed access | 14.8% |
| Infection | 25.9% |
| Fever | 13.0% |

Note: All catheters in the Onyx group were able to be removed. Two Onyx deaths occurred during the clinical study (intra/peri-operative bleeding with hematoma and MCA infarction). A third death occurred post-study follow-up (surgery-related hemorrhage). The role of Onyx in patient deaths is unknown.

## Effectiveness

From the pivotal RCT (ITT analysis, N=100):
- **Primary endpoint (>=50% bAVM volume reduction):** Onyx 97.6% (41/42) vs. TRUFILL 84.3% (43/51)
- Demonstrates non-inferiority of Onyx compared to TRUFILL (n-BCA) for presurgical bAVM embolization
- No statistically significant difference in surgical blood loss or resection time between groups
- Average 1.8 embolization procedures per patient (range 1-7)
- Surgical blood loss: mean 1127 +/- 1401 mL (median 550 mL)
- Surgical resection time: mean 399 +/- 179 min (median 366 min)

## MRI Safety

Not explicitly addressed in the IFU. The tantalum component creates significant artifact on CT and MRI. Onyx is a permanent implant. Consult institutional MRI safety protocols before scanning patients with Onyx embolization.
