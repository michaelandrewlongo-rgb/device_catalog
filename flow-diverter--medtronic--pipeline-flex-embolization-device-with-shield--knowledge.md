# Pipeline Flex Embolization Device with Shield Technology

**Manufacturer:** Medtronic
**Category:** Flow diverter

## What It Is

Third-generation flow diverter embolization device for placement in parent vessel across the neck of intracranial aneurysms. Diverts flow away from aneurysm neck and reconstructs parent artery. Shield Technology is a surface modification using phosphorylcholine to reduce thrombogenicity. Reference device in flow diversion with 10 years of experience and highest number of clinical studies.

## Indications

Treatment of intracranial aneurysms (IA) by endovascular placement across the aneurysm neck in the parent vessel.

## Compatible With

Phenom 17/21/27 microcatheters (delivery catheter)

## Use Notes

Shield Technology (phosphorylcholine surface modification) shown to reduce thrombogenicity in vitro. Pipeline embolization devices are the reference in flow diversion. 1-year follow-up data available from PFLEX study.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **Surface Technology**: Pipeline Flex with Shield Technology features phosphorylcholine surface modification to reduce thrombogenicity, while Pipeline Flex (without Shield), FRED, and Surpass Streamline do not specify surface modifications.
- **Design Architecture**: FRED has a dual-layer braid design (inner high-density mesh for flow diversion, outer stent for scaffolding), whereas Pipeline Flex (with/without Shield) and Surpass Streamline are single-layer braided devices.
- **Indication Age Range**: Pipeline Flex (without Shield) and FRED are indicated for adults ≥22 years old, while Surpass Streamline is indicated for patients ≥18 years old; Pipeline Flex with Shield Technology does not specify an age range.
- **Resheathing Capability**: FRED specifies resheathing capability as a key differentiator, while Pipeline Flex (with/without Shield) and Surpass Streamline do not mention resheathing features.
- **Clinical Data Reference**: Pipeline Flex with Shield Technology is described as the reference device in flow diversion with 10 years of experience and the highest number of clinical studies, including 1-year follow-up data from the PFLEX study; other devices do not specify comparable clinical data references.
