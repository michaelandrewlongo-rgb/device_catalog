# Elevate Expandable Interbody Device -- Medtronic

## What It Is

An expandable PEEK and titanium alloy interbody cage for lumbar fusion via TLIF, PLIF, or MAST MIDLF approaches. The device has a bullet-tip design that self-distracts during insertion and a unique expansion mechanism that provides continuous bone graft-to-endplate apposition as it expands -- meaning the graft material stays pressed against the endplates throughout the expansion process rather than creating a gap. Two implant variants exist: Standard (posterior expansion with up to 8 degrees lordosis when fully expanded) and Extra-Lordotic (fixed posterior height with various degrees of lordosis up to 13 degrees when fully expanded). The implant has teeth on the surface to resist expulsion, tantalum markers for radiographic visualization, and an open volume design for bone graft packing. Width is 10mm across all sizes. Expansion is driven by a Torque Handle with a built-in 2.5 Nm torque limit to protect the endplates.

## Indications

- Degenerative disc disease (DDD) at one or two contiguous levels from L2 to S1
- Up to Grade 1 spondylolisthesis or retrolisthesis at involved levels
- DDD confirmed by history, radiology, and discogenic back pain with degeneration
- Skeletally mature patients who have failed at least 6 months of conservative treatment
- Open or MIS posterior (TLIF) or posterior bilateral (PLIF) approach
- MAST MIDLF approach (cortical screw construct along spinous process)

**Must be used with:** autograft and/or allograft bone graft (cancellous and/or corticocancellous), supplemental posterior fixation

**Not for cervical spine use.**

## Contraindications

- Active infection at the operative site (local or systemic)
- Severe osteoporosis or osteopenia that would preclude adequate endplate support
- Known allergy or sensitivity to titanium or PEEK
- Inadequate bone stock, bone quality, or anatomy to support the implant
- Conditions that preclude the potential benefit of spinal fusion
- Spondylolisthesis grade 2 or higher (device cleared for up to Grade 1 only)
- Prior fusion at the treated level (relative contraindication)
- Morbid obesity or conditions preventing adequate surgical exposure
- Patient non-compliance with postoperative restrictions
- Use in the cervical spine (explicitly contraindicated)

## Compatible With

- **Pedicle screw systems:** Medtronic CD HORIZON (SOLERA, LONGITUDE), VERTEX SELECT, or any posterior fixation system with standard 5.5mm rods. Supplemental posterior fixation is mandatory.
- **Cortical screw systems:** Compatible with MAST MIDLF approach using cortical screw trajectory along the spinous process base
- **Retractor systems:** Medtronic MAST Quadrant retractor system for MIS approach, standard open retractors for open TLIF/PLIF
- **Bone graft:** Autograft and/or allograft (cancellous and corticocancellous). Open volume design allows graft-to-endplate apposition during expansion. INFUSE (rhBMP-2) use is off-label but reported in literature.
- **Navigation:** Tantalum markers visible on AP and lateral fluoroscopy. Compatible with CT-based or fluoroscopy-based navigation systems.
- **Dedicated instrumentation:** Distractor/Trial (Standard and Extra-Lordotic, Small and Large), Inserter Assembly, Torque Handle (2.5 Nm limit), Leverage Tool, Extractor Forceps, Slap Hammer

## Use Notes

- Two implant variants exist: Standard (posterior expansion, up to 8 degrees lordosis) and Extra-Lordotic (fixed posterior height, up to 13 degrees lordosis). Select based on preoperative sagittal alignment goals.
- The Torque Handle is limited to 2.5 Nm. In patients with suspected poor bone quality, use less torque to avoid endplate fracture and subsidence.
- PEEK component orientation is critical: PEEK portion with tantalum markers faces superiorly. This provides the radiographic visualization needed on postoperative imaging.
- Always verify the back screw is fully retracted and the PEEK nose is fully recessed before insertion. Protruding components will damage tissue.
- For PLIF, two implants are placed bilaterally through the interlaminar window. Same expansion and insertion technique applies.
- The Distractor/Trial provides real-time intradiscal height readings (9-14mm markings visible in the trial window) and length markings visible under fluoroscopy (23, 28, 32mm). This reduces guesswork during sizing.
- Pack cancellous graft anteriorly into the disc space before implant insertion to maximize fusion surface area. Also fill the implant graft chamber.
- Seat the implant leading tip on the anterior apophyseal ring for structural support and to reduce subsidence risk.
- Account for kyphotic prone positioning when selecting implant size and lordosis to avoid inadvertent hyperlordosis.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **vs Globus RISE TLIF:** Elevate uses a PEEK/titanium hybrid construction; RISE is all-titanium. Elevate is 10mm width only; RISE offers 8, 10, and 12mm widths. Elevate has a bullet-tip design that self-distracts during insertion; RISE requires pre-distraction. Elevate offers an Extra-Lordotic variant (up to 13 degrees); RISE achieves up to 15 degrees through different lordotic angle options.
- **vs DePuy CONCORDE LIFT:** Both are expandable TLIF cages with PEEK components. CONCORDE LIFT uses PEEK-OPTIMA body with titanium endplates; Elevate uses PEEK top with titanium alloy bottom. CONCORDE LIFT is designed to reduce the need for trialing since the cage expands to fill the disc space. Elevate's Distractor/Trial provides real-time height readings.
- **vs static TLIF cages (e.g., Stryker Tritanium TL):** Expandable design eliminates the need to distract to full height before insertion, reducing nerve root retraction. Static 3D-printed titanium cages offer superior osseointegration surface but require more retraction and trialing. Expandable cages add cost and mechanical complexity.
- **vs lateral cages (e.g., NuVasive CoRoent XL):** Elevate is a posterior-approach cage (TLIF/PLIF). Lateral cages are inserted via transpsoas approach with larger footprints. Lateral approach avoids posterior musculature dissection but carries psoas/lumbar plexus injury risk. Different patient populations and indications.
- **Unique feature:** Continuous bone graft-to-endplate apposition during expansion. The graft material stays pressed against endplates throughout the expansion process, unlike some expandable designs where graft can shift.

## Use Step-by-Step (TLIF Approach)

1. **Position the patient.** Prone in kyphosis on a well-padded frame or table that allows a free abdomen. Kyphotic positioning is important: account for it when selecting implant size and lordosis to avoid inadvertent hyperlordosis. Imaging guidance is only possible in the lateral view.

2. **Incision and exposure.** Mark the affected segment under imaging. Make a 4cm skin incision lateral to midline at the level of the affected segment. Expose the interlaminar window and medial facet joint.

3. **Decompress and perform facetectomy.** Perform a partial medial facetectomy (bony resection cranial and lateral in the area of the facet joints). Spinous process resection is usually not required. Maintain the superior lamina to preserve interspinous stability.

4. **Discectomy.** Open the annulus, resect the nucleus and inner annulus as completely as possible. Remove endplate cartilage. Preserve the bony endplates intact -- injuring them leads to subsidence.

5. **Select the Distractor/Trial.** Choose Standard or Extra-Lordotic, Small or Large, based on preoperative planning for height and lordosis. Use caution during retraction of the dura mater and offbranching nerve roots.

6. **Distract and size.** Insert the Distractor/Trial into the disc space (mallet may be used). Position it on the apophyseal ring. Attach the Distractor Driver (T-handle). Rotate to expand the tip. Read the intradiscal height in the trial window (markings from 9-14mm visible). The head of the Distractor/Trial has length markings (23mm, 28mm, 32mm) visible under fluoroscopy to determine implant length.

7. **Pack graft anteriorly.** Place cancellous and/or corticocancellous bone graft (autograft or allograft) into the disc space anteriorly before implantation. Also fill the implant graft chamber.

8. **Assemble the Inserter.** Thread the Inner Locking Tube into the Inserter to create the Inserter Assembly. Attach the implant to the Inserter by turning the Inner Locking Tube clockwise. Orient the implant with the PEEK portion (with x-ray markers) facing superiorly.

9. **Verify implant configuration before insertion.** Confirm the screw on the back of the implant is fully retracted and the PEEK nose is fully recessed within the titanium alloy bottom. This is critical -- if the PEEK nose or screws protrude, insertion will damage tissue.

10. **Insert the implant.** Carefully advance into the disc space (mallet may be used). Position the leading tip on the anterior apophyseal ring.

11. **Expand the implant.** Insert the Inserter Drive Shaft with attached Torque Handle into the Inserter Assembly. Turn the Torque Handle clockwise to expand. The Torque Handle has a maximum torque of 2.5 Nm to protect the endplates. Verify position with imaging. Do NOT strike the Torque Handle with a mallet.

12. **Remove instrumentation.** Remove the Torque Handle and Inserter Drive Shaft first. Then disengage the Inner Locking Tube from the implant (the Leverage Tool can assist). Remove the Inner Locking Tube and Inserter from the disc space.

13. **Confirm final position.** Use lateral fluoroscopy with tantalum markers to verify implant position and expansion.

14. **Apply supplemental posterior fixation.** Pedicle screws and rods as appropriate.

## Pearls / Troubleshooting

- **Standard vs. Extra-Lordotic decision:** Standard implants give approximately 1mm of posterior expansion and up to 8 degrees of lordosis. Extra-Lordotic implants have a fixed posterior height and achieve lordosis only through anterior expansion (up to 13 degrees with the 23mm length). Use Extra-Lordotic when you need more segmental lordosis without posterior height gain.
- **PEEK nose orientation matters.** The PEEK portion with tantalum markers goes superior. This gives you radiographic visualization on AP and lateral views.
- **Pre-insertion checklist:** Always verify that the back screw is fully retracted and the PEEK nose is recessed before inserting. Protruding components will damage tissue and prevent proper insertion.
- **Do not exceed 2.5 Nm torque.** The Torque Handle is torque-limited for a reason. If poor bone quality is suspected, use even less torque to avoid endplate fracture and subsidence.
- **Never strike the Torque Handle with a mallet.** This can damage the implant and the expansion mechanism.
- **Place graft anteriorly first.** The open volume design allows graft-to-endplate contact during expansion, but pre-packing the disc space anteriorly maximizes fusion surface area.
- **Seat on the apophyseal ring.** The leading tip of the implant should rest on the anterior apophyseal ring for structural support and to reduce subsidence risk.
- **For repositioning or removal:** Collapse the implant by turning the Torque Handle counterclockwise. If the implant will not collapse, use the Extractor Forceps. Attach the Slap Hammer to the Inserter Assembly for extraction force. Consider increased risk to neural elements during explantation.
- **PLIF approach:** The same implant is used bilaterally. The technique is identical except the approach is through the interlaminar window (midline or lateral to midline incision). Two implants are placed side by side.
- **The Distractor/Trial window shows height in real time** as you expand it, visible at 9-14mm markings. This eliminates guesswork during sizing.
