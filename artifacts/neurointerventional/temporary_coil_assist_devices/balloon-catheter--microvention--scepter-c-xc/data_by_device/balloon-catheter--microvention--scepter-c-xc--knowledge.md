# Scepter C / Scepter XC Occlusion Balloon Catheter

**Manufacturer:** MicroVention (Terumo)
**IFU Reference:** IFU100302 Rev. X1, Revised 2022-09

---

## What It Is

Dual-lumen, single-use occlusion balloon microcatheters for temporary vascular occlusion in the peripheral and neurovasculature. The Scepter C and Scepter XC are the larger-balloon siblings of the Scepter Mini, designed for balloon-assisted coiling/embolization and temporary parent vessel occlusion in medium-sized intracranial and peripheral vessels.

Key features:
- 0.014" guidewire-compatible (vs. 0.008" for the Mini) -- accommodates standard neurovascular microwires
- Multiple balloon sizes: Scepter C in 4x10, 4x15, and 4x20 mm; Scepter XC in 4x11 mm
- Compliant balloons that can be inflated from approximately 1.8 mm to 4.9 mm diameter depending on size and volume
- External hydrophilic coating, dual radiopaque markers, distal air-purge hole
- Includes a shaping mandrel (not a stylet) for steam-shaping the distal tip
- Air-purge hole can optionally be sealed via steam technique for use during embolic delivery

The Scepter C offers three balloon lengths (10, 15, 20 mm) for different neck sizes. The Scepter XC (4x11 mm) has a wider compliance range, reaching up to 5.9 mm diameter at maximum volume.

### Decision Rule -- Choosing Between C and XC, and Balloon Length

- **Scepter C 4x10:** Short necks or focal occlusion. Reaches up to 4.9 mm diameter. Best for narrow-neck aneurysms or short-segment parent vessel occlusion.
- **Scepter C 4x15:** Medium-length necks. Reaches up to 5.0 mm. Standard workhorse for most balloon-assisted coiling.
- **Scepter C 4x20:** Wide-neck aneurysms or longer occlusion segments. Reaches up to 4.9 mm but covers 20 mm of vessel length. Trade-off: wider coverage but slightly less compliant at a given volume.
- **Scepter XC 4x11:** When you need a wider occlusion diameter (up to 5.9 mm) in a short segment. The XC has greater compliance -- same balloon length as the 4x10 but inflates larger. Use for wider parent vessels or wide-neck aneurysms where the C would not achieve adequate occlusion.

If the target vessel is under approximately 2.7 mm and you need a 0.008" system, use the Scepter Mini instead.

## Indications

- Temporary occlusion in the peripheral and neurovasculature where selective flow arrest or control is desired
- Balloon-assisted embolization of intracranial aneurysms (balloon-assisted coiling)
- Infusion of diagnostic agents (contrast media) in peripheral vasculature
- Infusion of diagnostic and therapeutic agents (embolization materials approved for neurovascular use) through the guidewire lumen

---

## Contraindications

- **Not for embolectomy or angioplasty**
- **Not for coronary vessels**
- **Not for pediatric or neonatal use**

## Relevant Protocol

### Agent Compatibility
- **Compatible:** DMSO, Onyx Liquid Embolic System, contrast media
- **NOT compatible:** NBCA (n-butyl cyanoacrylate), solutions containing ethyl esters of iodized fatty acids of poppy seed oil
- For other liquid embolics, check their own IFU

### Inflation Medium
- Inflate with **contrast/saline solution** (50:50 Omnipaque 300 recommended per Table 1)
- **Never inflate with air or any gas** while in the body
- Use a 1 cc high-resolution syringe for precise volume control during the procedure

### Pressure Limits
- Maximum guidewire lumen pressure: 700 PSI (4826 kPa, 47.6 atm)
- Do not attach high-pressure injection devices to balloon inflation port

## Deployment / Use Step-by-Step

### Preparation (Primary Method)

1. Choose appropriate balloon catheter for vessel size.
2. Gently pull hub from dispenser tube to loosen strain relief. Flush heparinized saline through dispenser tube via flush port. Allow 30 seconds hydration.
3. Flush guidewire lumen with heparinized saline. Introduce hydrated guidewire.
4. Pull catheter from dispenser tube. Inspect for damage. Do not let it dry.
5. Prepare contrast/saline solution per Table 1 (50:50 Omnipaque 300).
6. Fill 10 cc syringe with contrast/saline. Attach directly to inflation port without injecting. No bubbles.
7. Hold balloon proximal to inflation plug, point upright.
8. Apply steady syringe pressure. If balloon starts air-filled, maintain constant pressure. Do not fully inflate with air -- keep inflation below distal balloon marker band.
9. Do NOT tilt until contrast reaches distal purge hole and completely fills balloon.
10. Inspect for damage and bubbles. If air bubbles persist, use aspiration technique (Step A below) or place tip in saline, deflate.
11. Remove 10 cc syringe. Attach stopcock to 1 cc syringe filled with contrast/saline.
12. Prime stopcock, attach to inflation port.

### A. Aspiration Technique (if bubbles persist)

1. Attach 3-way stopcock to 20 cc syringe filled with 3 cc contrast/saline.
2. Connect to balloon inflation port.
3. Syringe pointed down, stopcock open, pull vacuum.
4. Remove balloon from saline bowl, hold distal tip in air.
5. Hold vacuum until inflation port clears.
6. Close stopcock to inflation lumen, point syringe up, purge air.
7. Reopen stopcock, syringe pointed down, pull vacuum again.
8. Slowly release vacuum, let system equalize.
9. Replace with 1 cc syringe filled with contrast/saline.
10. Advance guidewire or insert straight shaping mandrel to straighten balloon. Hold tip upright, slowly inflate to nominal diameter. If clear, proceed.

### B. Tip Shaping Technique (optional, after balloon is purged)

1. Bend shaping mandrel to desired shape.
2. With balloon deflated, insert mandrel into distal guidewire lumen.
3. Hold compliance card bottom with "balloon side" facing you.
4. Rest balloon proximal to inflation port on center of compliance card.
5. Place distal tip marker band at bottom of steam groove on compliance card. Do not expose more than 3 mm of distal tip to steam -- balloon damage will occur.
6. Hold in laminar steam flow for approximately 20 seconds. Compliance card protects balloon from heat.
7. Remove from steam. Quench in heparinized saline.
8. Remove shaping mandrel. **Mandrel is not intended for use inside the body.**

### C. Air-Purge Hole Sealing Technique (optional, for embolic delivery)

1. Insert shaping mandrel into distal guidewire lumen (balloon deflated).
2. Position distal tip marker band at bottom of compliance card steam groove.
3. Hold in laminar steam for approximately 20 seconds.
4. Remove, quench in saline. Remove mandrel.
5. Verify seal by inflating and checking for contrast leakage from purge hole. If still leaking, repeat.

### D. Final Inspection

1. Re-inflate to nominal volume. Inspect for irregularities. Discard if any found.
2. If purge hole was sealed, check for contrast leakage.
3. Deflate with distal tip submerged in saline. Let pressure equalize. Ready for use.

### Intravascular Use

4. Attach RHV to guidewire lumen. Connect continuous saline flush to RHV sidearm.
5. Attach RHV to guiding catheter hub. Connect saline flush.
6. Open RHV, introduce balloon catheter/guidewire via introducer sheath. Advance to guide tip. Retract and peel off introducer.
7. Navigate to target under fluoroscopy. Tighten RHV gently.
8. Attach 2-way stopcock to 1 cc syringe with contrast. Prime. Inflate to target volume per compliance table.
9. Lock stopcock if desired.
10. If delivering agents: remove guidewire, deliver through guidewire lumen per agent IFU.
11. To remove: deflate under fluoroscopy, confirm complete deflation (see deflation time table), slowly withdraw.

## Bailout / Troubleshooting

**Q: Balloon will not inflate or deflation is delayed.**
A: Check for over-tightened RHV. Verify contrast viscosity matches Table 1. Ensure stopcock is correctly positioned. If using high-viscosity contrast, expect slower inflation/deflation -- consider diluting.

**Q: Air bubbles persist after preparation.**
A: Use the aspiration technique (Step A). If bubbles still persist after two attempts, discard the catheter. Air in the balloon degrades fluoroscopic visualization and risks air embolism.

**Q: Cannot achieve adequate occlusion despite full inflation.**
A: Confirm you are using the correct balloon size. If the vessel is larger than the maximum balloon diameter (4.9 mm for C 4x10, 5.0 mm for C 4x15, 5.9 mm for XC 4x11), you need a larger device. Verify positioning -- the balloon may have migrated. Check for aneurysm geometry that allows flow around the balloon.

**Q: Contrast leaking from purge hole after sealing attempt.**
A: Repeat the air-purge hole sealing technique (Step C). If it still leaks after two attempts, use the catheter without sealing -- you can still perform balloon-assisted coiling but will have some contrast leak during inflation.

**Q: Resistance to catheter advancement.**
A: Stop. Do not force. Assess under fluoroscopy. Consider steam-shaping the tip (Step B) for tortuous anatomy. Reposition guide catheter or use a different access route.

**Q: Blood enters balloon after deflation.**
A: This occurs from continuing negative aspiration after the balloon is fully deflated. It reduces fluoroscopic visibility. If this happens, re-inflate and re-deflate. Avoid excessive aspiration.

**Q: Balloon tip damaged during back-loading over guidewire.**
A: Inspect the distal tip before use any time you back-load. If damage is seen, discard.

**Q: Shaping mandrel left in catheter during navigation.**
A: The shaping mandrel is NOT intended for intravascular use. It must be removed before the catheter enters the RHV or body.

## Complications to Know

Per IFU, potential complications include:
- Vessel or aneurysm perforation
- Vasospasm
- Hematoma at site of entry
- Embolism
- Ischemia
- Intracerebral/intracranial hemorrhage
- Pseudoaneurysm
- Seizure
- Stroke
- Infection
- Vessel dissection
- Thrombus formation
- Death

Radiation-related risks from fluoroscopy: alopecia, skin burns, cataracts, delayed neoplasia.

Note: The IFU does not provide specific complication rates. Published balloon-assisted coiling literature generally reports thromboembolic complication rates of 2-5% and balloon-related vessel rupture rates of <2%. These figures come from the broader literature, not MicroVention-specific registries.

---

## MRI Safety

**No MRI safety information is provided in the Scepter C/XC IFU.**

This is a single-use device removed at the end of the procedure. It is not an implant, so MRI conditional labeling does not apply in the typical use scenario.
