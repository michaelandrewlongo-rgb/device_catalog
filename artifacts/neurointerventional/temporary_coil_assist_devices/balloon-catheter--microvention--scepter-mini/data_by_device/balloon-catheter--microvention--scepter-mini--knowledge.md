# Scepter Mini Occlusion Balloon Catheter

**Manufacturer:** MicroVention (Terumo)
**IFU Reference:** IFU100301 Rev. X1, Revised 2022-09

---

## What It Is

A dual-lumen, single-use occlusion balloon microcatheter designed for temporary vascular occlusion in the peripheral and neurovasculature. The smaller sibling of the Scepter C/XC line, it is built for distal access in small-caliber vessels. It has a dedicated inflation lumen (balloon) and a guidewire lumen (for wire navigation and agent delivery). The hydrophilic coating covers the distal 100 cm. Two radiopaque marker bands delineate balloon position under fluoroscopy. A distal air-purge hole allows air evacuation from the inflation lumen during prep.

Key differentiator from the Scepter C/XC: accepts only a 0.008" guidewire (vs. 0.014"), enabling navigation into smaller, more distal vessels. The balloon is smaller (single size: 2.2 x 9 mm nominal) and uses a catheter stylet rather than a shaping mandrel.

### Decision Rule

Use the Scepter Mini when the target vessel or aneurysm neck is too small or distal for the Scepter C/XC (which requires a 0.014" wire). If the target vessel diameter is under approximately 2.7 mm and you need a 0.008" wire-compatible system, this is the correct catheter. For vessels 3-5 mm, use Scepter C or XC instead.

---

## Indications

- Temporary occlusion in the peripheral and neurovasculature where selective flow arrest or control is desired
- Balloon-assisted embolization of intracranial aneurysms (balloon-assisted coiling)
- Infusion of diagnostic agents (contrast media) in peripheral vasculature
- Infusion of diagnostic and therapeutic agents (embolization materials approved for neurovascular use) through the guidewire lumen

---

## Contraindications

- **Not for embolectomy or angioplasty** -- this is an occlusion balloon, not a treatment balloon
- **Not for coronary vessels**
- **Not for pediatric or neonatal use**

## Relevant Protocol

### Agent Compatibility
- **Compatible:** DMSO, Onyx Liquid Embolic System, contrast media
- **NOT compatible:** n-BCA (n-butyl cyanoacrylate), solutions containing ethyl esters of iodized fatty acids of poppy seed oil (e.g., Lipiodol)
- For other liquid embolics, check their own IFU for compatibility

### Inflation Medium
- Inflate with **contrast solution only** (100% Omnipaque 300 recommended per Table 1)
- **Never inflate with air or any gas** while in the body
- Use a high-resolution syringe (e.g., 1 cc) for precise volume control

### Pressure Limits
- Maximum guidewire lumen pressure: 700 PSI (4826 kPa, 47.6 atm) -- exceeding this may cause leakage or rupture
- Do not attach high-pressure injection devices to the balloon inflation port

### Deflation Time
- Omnipaque 300 (100:0, 6.3 cps at 37C): 15 seconds or less for the 2.2 x 9 mm balloon

### Flow Rates (Table 4)

| Medium | 100 psi | 300 psi |
|---|---|---|
| Saline | 0.7 cc/sec | 1.1 cc/sec |
| 50/50 Contrast (300 mgI/ml) | 0.3 cc/sec | 0.5 cc/sec |
| 100% Contrast (300 mgI/ml) | 0.1 cc/sec | 0.4 cc/sec |
| 100% Contrast (350 mgI/ml) | 0.1 cc/sec | 0.2 cc/sec |

## Deployment / Use Step-by-Step

### Preparation

1. Choose a balloon catheter appropriate for vessel size.
2. With catheter still in dispenser tube, flush heparinized saline through dispenser tube via flush port to hydrate the hydrophilic segment. Allow at least 30 seconds of hydration.
3. Pull catheter from dispenser tube. If resistance, re-flush and re-hydrate. Inspect for damage. Do not let the catheter dry.
4. Remove the stylet wire from the guidewire lumen. **Do not use the stylet inside a guide catheter -- it is for extraction from the dispenser hoop only.**
5. Flush guidewire lumen with heparinized saline. Introduce a hydrated guidewire.
6. Prepare 100% contrast solution.
7. Fill a 1 cc syringe with contrast. Attach directly to inflation port without injecting. Ensure no bubbles.
8. Hold balloon proximal to inflation plug, point balloon tip upright.
9. Hold syringe upright, apply steady pressure. If balloon starts air-filled, maintain constant pressure.
10. Do NOT tilt until contrast reaches distal purge hole and completely fills balloon.
11. Inspect balloon for damage and bubbles. Place tip in saline bowl, deflate.
12. Remove 1 cc syringe. Attach stopcock to high-resolution syringe filled with contrast. Prime the stopcock, attach to inflation port.

### Final Inspection

13. Re-inflate to nominal volume. Inspect for irregularities. If any seen, discard.
14. Check distal tip for contrast leakage from air purge hole. If leaking, discard.
15. Deflate with distal tip submerged in saline. Let pressure equalize. Catheter is ready.

### Intravascular Use

16. Attach RHV to guidewire lumen. Connect continuous saline flush to RHV sidearm.
17. Attach RHV to guiding catheter hub. Connect saline flush to that RHV sidearm.
18. Open RHV, introduce balloon catheter/guidewire into guiding catheter using the introducer sheath. Advance to guiding catheter tip. Retract and peel off introducer. Advance catheter through RHV.
19. Navigate catheter and guidewire to target under fluoroscopy. Tighten RHV gently (do not over-tighten -- it will delay inflation/deflation).
20. Attach 2-way stopcock to high-resolution syringe with contrast. Prime. Inflate balloon to desired volume per compliance table.
21. Lock stopcock if desired.
22. If delivering agents: remove guidewire, prepare per agent IFU, deliver through guidewire lumen.
23. To remove: deflate under fluoroscopy, confirm complete deflation (allow up to 15 seconds), slowly withdraw catheter and guidewire.

## Bailout / Troubleshooting

**Q: Balloon will not inflate or deflates too slowly.**
A: Check for over-tightened RHV -- loosen slightly. Verify contrast viscosity matches Table 1 expectations. Ensure stopcock is in correct position. Check for kinks in inflation lumen.

**Q: Air bubbles persist in balloon after preparation.**
A: Repeat the prep procedure. Keep distal tip submerged in saline during deflation to prevent air re-entry. If bubbles cannot be cleared, discard the catheter.

**Q: Contrast leaking from distal air-purge hole after prep.**
A: Discard the unit. The purge hole seal is compromised.

**Q: Resistance to catheter advancement.**
A: Stop. Do not force. Assess under fluoroscopy. May indicate vessel tortuosity, calcification, vasospasm, or an existing device in the path. Reposition guide catheter or use a different access route.

**Q: Balloon will not fully deflate.**
A: Allow adequate time (up to 15 seconds with Omnipaque 300). Apply gentle negative aspiration. Confirm under fluoroscopy before attempting withdrawal. Pulling a partially inflated balloon risks vessel dissection.

**Q: Hub assembly damage from excessive syringe torque.**
A: Switch to a new catheter. Excessive rotational force on the syringe connection can crack the hub.

## Complications to Know

Per IFU, potential complications include:
- Vessel or aneurysm perforation
- Vasospasm
- Hematoma at site of entry
- Embolism
- Ischemia
- Intracerebral/intracranial hemorrhage
- Pseudoaneurysm
- Seizure
- Stroke
- Infection
- Vessel dissection
- Thrombus formation
- Death

Radiation-related risks (from fluoroscopy): alopecia, skin burns (reddening to ulcers), cataracts, delayed neoplasia. Risk increases with procedure time and number of procedures.

Note: The IFU does not provide specific complication rates. Published balloon-assisted coiling literature generally reports procedural complication rates in the range of 2-5% for thromboembolic events and <2% for balloon-related vessel rupture, but these figures are from the broader literature, not from MicroVention-specific trials.

---

## MRI Safety

**No MRI safety information is provided in the Scepter Mini IFU.**

This is a single-use device removed at the end of the procedure. It is not an implant, so MRI conditional labeling does not apply in the typical use scenario.
