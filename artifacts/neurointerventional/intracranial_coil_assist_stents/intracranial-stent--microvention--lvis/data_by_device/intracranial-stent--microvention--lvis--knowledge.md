# LVIS / LVIS Jr. Intraluminal Support Device (MicroVention/Terumo)

**Manufacturer:** MicroVention, Inc. (Terumo)
**Category:** Intracranial stent
**Source:** https://www.terumoneuro.com/products/lvis-family

## What It Is

The LVIS (Low-Profile Visualized Intraluminal Support) and LVIS Jr. are self-expanding nickel titanium (nitinol) intracranial stents for stent-assisted coiling of wide-necked aneurysms. They use a single wire braid, compliant, closed-cell design that can be deployed and retrieved by a single operator. Key features include helical radiopaque marker strands woven into the braid for enhanced fluoroscopic visibility (the "visualized" feature), flared ends (2 mm each side beyond working length), and the ability to resheath/recapture before full deployment. The LVIS uses the Headway 21 microcatheter (0.021" ID); the LVIS Jr. uses the Headway 17 microcatheter (0.017" ID) or Scepter C / Scepter XC Occlusion Balloon.

Key differentiator vs. Neuroform Atlas: closed-cell design provides better neck coverage (lower free area percentage) and resheathing/recapture capability before detachment. LVIS Jr. has the smallest delivery profile of any intracranial stent (0.017" microcatheter compatible).

### Free Area (Metal Coverage) -- LVIS

Typical free area ranges from 71% to 82% depending on product code and vessel OD. Lower free area = more metal coverage at the neck.

### Free Area -- LVIS Jr.

Typical free area ranges from 76% to 85% depending on product code and vessel OD.

### Sizing Decision Rule

- LVIS: for parent vessels 2.5-5.5 mm OD
- LVIS Jr.: for parent vessels 2.0-3.5 mm OD
- Select device size based on pre-treatment angiograms with correct and accurate vessel measurements from multiple views
- Total Length includes 2 mm flared ends on each side beyond the working length
- The device significantly foreshortens as vessel diameter increases relative to labeled OD -- consult the sizing tables carefully

## Indications

- Patients >= 18 years of age
- Wide-necked (neck width >= 4 mm OR dome-to-neck ratio < 2) saccular intracranial aneurysms
- Parent vessel diameter >= 2.0 mm and <= 4.5 mm
- Used with neurovascular embolization coils (stent-assisted coiling)

## Contraindications

- Anticoagulant, anti-platelet, or thrombolytic drugs are contraindicated in the patient
- Known hypersensitivity to metal such as nickel-titanium and metal jewelry
- Anatomy does not permit passage or deployment of the LVIS/LVIS Jr. device
- Active bacterial infection
- Pre-existing stent in place at the target aneurysm

## Relevant Protocol

**Dual antiplatelet therapy:** Required prior to procedure. Contraindicated if patient cannot receive anticoagulant/anti-platelet/thrombolytic drugs.

**Ruptured aneurysms:** Do NOT use for acutely ruptured intracranial aneurysms within a minimum of 30 days from rupture.

**Posterior circulation:** Safety and effectiveness has NOT been well established in the posterior circulation.

**Large/giant aneurysms:** Safety and effectiveness has NOT been established for large and giant wide-neck intracranial aneurysms.

**Small aneurysms (<= 5 mm):** Benefits may not outweigh the risks of treatment in patients with wide-neck aneurysms <= 5 mm in size, or reduced life expectancy, in the absence of additional risk factors.

## Deployment/Use Step-by-Step

1. Gain vascular access according to standard practice.
2. Place guide catheter in the appropriate target vessel.
3. Navigate the corresponding microcatheter (0.021" Headway 21 for LVIS / 0.017" Headway 17 or Scepter C / Scepter XC for LVIS Jr.) over a guidewire at least 15 mm distal to the aneurysm neck or target location.
4. Remove the guidewire.
5. Maintain flush through the microcatheter per standard endovascular practice.
6. Select an appropriate sized LVIS/LVIS Jr. device (refer to sizing tables).
7. Carefully inspect the LVIS/LVIS Jr. device package for damage to the sterile barrier.
8. Peel open the pouch using aseptic technique.
9. Carefully place the dispenser coil into the sterile field.
10. a. Unclip the molded cap attached to the delivery wire from the dispenser coil. Pull on proximal end of the delivery wire until the introducer exits the dispenser coil. Hold the delivery wire and introducer together while continuing to remove the entire device. Do not partially deploy LVIS/LVIS Jr. device from the introducer.
    b. After removal from the dispenser coil, carefully push on the delivery wire in a bowl of saline, partially deploy the LVIS/LVIS Jr. implant up to 5 mm or 50% (whichever occurs first), being careful not to detach the implant from the distal introducer tip. Check for:
    - Implant distal marker uniformity
    - Implant distal end shows even displacement with no entanglement
    - Implant tracks smoothly through introducer
    WARNING: DO NOT FULLY DEPLOY. If deployed, DO NOT attempt to reload the device. Use a new device.
    c. Hydrate the implant in saline, gently manipulate to minimize visible air bubbles. Pull back on delivery wire to fully retrieve the implant and wire tip within the introducer.
    WARNING: DO NOT CONTINUE if any defect is observed; return to MicroVention, Inc.
11. Confirm that the tip of the delivery wire is entirely within the introducer.
12. Confirm the delivery wire is not kinked and the introducer tip is not damaged.
    WARNING: Do not shape the tip of the delivery wire.
13. Partially insert the distal end of the introducer into the RHV connected to the microcatheter. Tighten the RHV locking ring. Flush the y-connector of the RHV with sterile saline and verify fluid exits the proximal end of the introducer.
    WARNING: Purge carefully to avoid air introduction.
14. Untighten the RHV locking ring and advance the introducer until fully engaged with the microcatheter hub, then tighten.
    WARNING: Confirm that there are no air bubbles trapped anywhere in the system.
    CAUTION: Confirm no gap between the introducer and microcatheter hub.
15. Advance the delivery wire to transfer the LVIS/LVIS Jr. device from within the introducer into the microcatheter.
    WARNING: Do not torque the delivery wire while advancing or retracting. A torque device should not be used.
16. Continue advancing the delivery wire into the microcatheter until the proximal tip of the delivery wire enters the introducer. Loosen the RHV locking ring, remove the introducer, and set it aside.
    Note: Fluoroscopy may be used up to this point at the physician's discretion.
    WARNING: Do not apply undue force. If resistance is encountered, withdraw the unit and select a new LVIS/LVIS Jr. device.
17. Track the LVIS/LVIS Jr. device through the microcatheter to the tip. Carefully advance until the device exit marker on the proximal end of the delivery wire approaches the RHV on the hub of the microcatheter. At this time, fluoroscopic guidance must be initiated.
18. Position the LVIS/LVIS Jr. device for deployment, ensuring a sufficient length of stent will deploy on either side of the aneurysm neck, by aligning the distal radiopaque end markers sufficiently past the aneurysm neck.

## Bailout / Troubleshooting

**Q: Can the LVIS/LVIS Jr. be recaptured if positioning is unsatisfactory?**
A: Yes, BEFORE full deployment and detachment, the LVIS/LVIS Jr. can be retrieved into the microcatheter and re-deployed at the desired target location, or removed completely from the patient. However, do NOT reposition the LVIS/LVIS Jr. in the parent vessel without fully retrieving the device first. Do NOT attempt to reposition after detachment.

**Q: The device was fully deployed prematurely. Can I reload it?**
A: No. If the device is deployed, DO NOT attempt to reload. Use a new device.

**Q: I encounter unusual resistance during delivery or removal.**
A: Remove the introducer/microcatheter and LVIS/LVIS Jr. device as a single unit. Applying excessive force can result in loss or damage to the device and delivery components.

**Q: Can the delivery wire be shaped or torqued?**
A: No. Do not shape the tip of the delivery wire. Do not torque the delivery wire while advancing or retracting. A torque device should not be used.

**Q: What if I need to cross the deployed LVIS/LVIS Jr. with other devices?**
A: Exercise caution when crossing the deployed/detached device with adjunctive devices such as guidewires, catheters, microcatheters, or balloon catheters to avoid disrupting the device geometry and placement.

## Complications to Know

### LVIS/LVIS Jr. Pivotal Study (N=153 ITT, 12-month follow-up)

**Primary Safety Endpoint (composite rate of major stroke or death within 30 days OR major ipsilateral stroke or neurologic death within 12 months):**
- Pre-specified: 5.2% (8/153), posterior mean 5.5%

## Effectiveness

### LVIS/LVIS Jr. Pivotal Study (N=153 ITT, 12-month follow-up)

**Primary effectiveness endpoint (pre-specified):** Complete (100%) intracranial aneurysm angiographic occlusion at 12 months without retreatment and no significant (>= 50%) stenosis.
- **70.6% (108/153)**, posterior mean 70.5% (95% CI: 63.0%-77.4%)

**Modified primary effectiveness endpoint:** 90-100% aneurysm occlusion (Raymond-Roy I and stable RR II) without clinically significant in-stent stenosis or target aneurysm retreatment.
- **81.0% (124/153)**, posterior mean 80.8% (95% CI: 74.3%-86.6%)

**Evaluable-only analysis (patients with 12-month follow-up, N=139):**
- Pre-specified endpoint: 77.7% (108/139), posterior mean 77.5% (95% CI: 70.3%-84.0%)
- Modified endpoint: 89.2% (124/139), posterior mean 82.5% (95% CI: 75.3%-88.6%) -- using DSA only at 12 months: 88.6% (117/132), posterior mean 88.3% (95% CI: 75.3%-88.6%)

## MRI Safety

**MR Conditional.** Safe under the following conditions:

- Static magnetic field of 1.5 Tesla and 3 Tesla only
- Maximum spatial gradient magnetic field of 2,500 Gauss/cm
- Maximum MR system reported whole body averaged SAR of 2 W/kg (Normal Operating Mode)

**Temperature rise:**
- Maximum 2.8 degrees C at 1.5T or 3.6 degrees C at 3T after 15 minutes of continuous scanning

**Image artifact:**
- Extends approximately 4 mm from the LVIS/LVIS Jr. device when imaged with a gradient echo pulse sequence on a 3-Tesla MRI system

**Materials:** Does not contain latex or PVC.
