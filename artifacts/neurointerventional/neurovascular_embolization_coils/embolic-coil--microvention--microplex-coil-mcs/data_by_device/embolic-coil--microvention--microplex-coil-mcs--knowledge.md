# MICROPLEX COIL SYSTEM (MCS)

**Manufacturer:** MicroVention (Terumo)

## What It Is

Device Description: The MCS devices consist of an implantable coil made of platinum alloy
(MCS) or a platinum allot with an inner hydrogel core (HES). The coil is
attached to a V-Trak delivery pusher via a polymer filament. The proximal
end of the pusher is inserted into a hand held battery powered V-Grip
Detachment Controller (sold separately). The implant segment detaches
upon activation of the Detachment Controller.

## Indications

Indications for Use: The MicroPlex Coil System is intended for the endovascular embolization of
intracranial aneurysms and other neurovascular abnormalities such as
arteriovenous malformations and arteriovenous fistulae. The MCS is also
intended for vascular occlusion of blood vessels within the neurovascular
system to permanently obstruct blood flow to an aneurysm or other
vascular malformation and for arterial and venous embolizations in the
peripheral vasculature.

## Compatible With

Compatible with 10-system microcatheters (minimum ID of 0.0165").

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **Coil composition**: MCS HyperSoft 3D uses bare platinum alloy coils, while HydroCoil Embolic System (HES) uses hydrogel-coated platinum coils designed for higher packing density.
- **Coil wire diameter range**: MCS HyperSoft 3D offers 0.00125-0.0015 inch primary wire OD, whereas HydroCoil Embolic System offers 0.00150-0.00225 inch and Axium systems have not specified.
- **Detachment mechanism**: MCS uses V-Grip battery-powered thermal detachment (0.75 seconds), while CODMAN systems use hydraulic detachment and i-ED COIL uses electrical detachment; Axium systems use mechanical detachment.
- **Recall status**: MCS has 16 active recalls for missing implant coils, CODMAN systems have 1 recall for detachment failure, and other devices have no specified recalls.
- **Stretch-resistant material options**: MCS HyperSoft 3D offers polyethylene terephthalate or polyolefin elastomer, while HydroCoil Embolic System has not specified and other devices use various materials (polypropylene for CODMAN, not specified for others).
