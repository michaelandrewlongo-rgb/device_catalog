# Vectra Anterior Cervical Plate System (DePuy Synthes)

## What It Is

The Vectra is an anterior cervical plate system for ACDF and corpectomy reconstruction. It uses cancellous bone screws (4.0mm and 4.5mm) in both fixed-angle and variable-angle configurations. The plate features a built-in locking mechanism where the screw heads lock into the plate to prevent backout. The system offers four screw insertion options (variable-angle cancellous, variable-angle self-tapping, fixed-angle cancellous, and fixed-angle self-tapping) plus a dedicated screw extraction system for revision. The Vectra plate is designed for anterior cervical interbody fusion.

## Sizing/Specs

### Screws

**Variable-Angle Cancellous Screws:**
- 4.0mm diameter: self-tapping cancellous bone screws
- 4.5mm diameter: emergency/rescue screws (used when 4.0mm strips bone)
- Both available in variable-angle and fixed-angle configurations

**Fixed-Angle Self-Tapping Screws:**
- Available in 4.0mm and 4.5mm diameters
- Self-tapping design eliminates need for pre-tapping

**Screw Lengths:** Multiple lengths available (standard cervical range)

### Plate Design
- Low-profile anterior cervical plate
- Pre-contoured lordotic shape
- Screw hole geometry supports both fixed and variable-angle screw insertion
- Integrated screw-locking mechanism within the plate

## Indications

- Anterior cervical interbody fusion (ACDF)
- Anterior cervical corpectomy and fusion (ACCF)
- Degenerative disc disease
- Trauma (fracture)
- Tumor
- Deformity (kyphosis, lordosis, scoliosis)
- Failed previous fusion
- Cervical spine fixation

## Contraindications

- Active infection at the operative site
- Severe osteoporosis, osteopenia, or metabolic bone disease limiting screw fixation
- Known allergy to titanium or other implant materials
- Conditions precluding benefit of spinal implant surgery
- Inadequate bone stock for screw purchase
- Mental or neuromuscular disorders creating unacceptable risk of fixation failure or noncompliance
- Skeletal immaturity

## Compatible With

- **Cervical interbody cages:** Any ACDF cage (PEEK or titanium) cleared for anterior cervical use. The Vectra plate provides supplemental fixation over the cage construct.
- **Corpectomy cages:** Expandable or static corpectomy devices for ACCF reconstruction
- **Bone graft:** Autograft, allograft, or bone graft substitutes within the interbody space
- **Cervical retractor systems:** Standard anterior cervical retractor setups (Caspar-type distraction pins, self-retaining retractors)
- **Imaging:** Intraoperative fluoroscopy (AP and lateral) for screw trajectory and plate positioning
- **Dedicated instrument set:** DePuy Synthes Vectra instrument tray including drill guides, screwdrivers, taps, and extraction tools

## Use Notes

- Four screw insertion options (variable-angle cancellous, variable-angle self-tapping, fixed-angle cancellous, fixed-angle self-tapping) allow flexibility based on bone quality and anatomy.
- Variable-angle screws permit trajectory adjustment to avoid endplate violation or adjacent disc penetration in challenging anatomy. Fixed-angle screws provide a standardized trajectory via the drill guide.
- The 4.5mm screw serves as a rescue/emergency screw when the 4.0mm has stripped the bone. Always have 4.5mm screws available.
- Dense bone should be pre-tapped before screw insertion to avoid screw fracture.
- The plate should not overhang adjacent disc spaces. Overhang is associated with adjacent-level ossification and increased dysphagia risk.
- The plate is pre-contoured with lordotic shape. Additional manual contouring is typically not required for standard anatomy.
- For long constructs or poor bone quality, consider supplemental posterior fixation.
- The extraction screwdriver (352.311) is specifically for screw removal. Using it for insertion risks driver and implant breakage.
- Standard cervical disc anatomy is slightly inclined anterocaudal to posterocranial. Screw trajectories must account for this to keep screws within the vertebral body.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **vs. Atlantis Vision Elite (Medtronic):** Both are anterior cervical plate systems with fixed and variable-angle screw options. The Atlantis Vision Elite uses Nitinol locking caps that rotate over screw heads; the Vectra uses an integrated plate-based screw-locking mechanism. The Atlantis supports up to 5-level constructs; Vectra plate lengths are available for multi-level use as well. The Vectra offers both cancellous and self-tapping screw options; Atlantis offers self-drilling and self-tapping options.
- **vs. Reflex Hybrid (Stryker):** The Reflex Hybrid is a semi-constrained plate with variable-angle screws and a rotational locking mechanism. The Vectra offers both fully fixed and variable-angle screw holes. The Reflex Hybrid emphasizes low-profile design; the Vectra emphasizes screw versatility (4 insertion options).
- **vs. Zero-profile devices (Zero-P, COALITION):** Zero-profile cages with integrated fixation eliminate the anterior plate entirely. The Vectra is a traditional anterior plate requiring a separate cage. Zero-profile devices reduce dysphagia risk from plate overhang. Traditional plates like the Vectra offer broader construct flexibility (multi-level, corpectomy, variable screw angles) and are the established standard with extensive long-term outcome data.

## Relevant Setup

- **Four screw insertion options:** Choose based on bone quality and surgeon preference:
  - Option A: Variable-angle cancellous screw (drill + tap + screw)
  - Option B: Variable-angle cancellous screw (awl + screw, no drilling)
  - Option C: Fixed-angle self-tapping screw (drill + screw, no tapping)
  - Option D: Fixed-angle self-tapping screw (awl + screw)
- **Pre-operative planning:** Determine number of levels and select plate length.
- **Disc spaces are slightly inclined anterocaudal to posterocranial** in the cervical spine. Screws must remain in the vertebral body.

## Use Step-by-Step

### Option A: Variable-Angle Cancellous Screw (Drill Technique)

**A1 - Drill pilot hole:**
1. Select a drill bit and screw of appropriate length.
2. Insert the Drill Guide into the desired hole to give the correct variable-angle screw trajectory.
3. Insert the Drill Bit into the Drill Guide and drill to the desired depth (drill stops when the stop contacts the top of the Drill Guide).
4. Use intraoperative imaging to check the drilling operation.
5. Remove drill guide and bit.

**A2 - Measure screw length (optional):**
6. Use the Screw Length Indicator (387.292) to measure depth if needed.

**A3 - Tap (optional for dense bone):**
7. For dense bone, tap using the Tap for Cancellous Bone Screws (311.402 for 4.0mm, 311.404 for 4.5mm).

**A4 - Insert screw:**
8. Load a variable-angle cancellous screw onto the Screwdriver for Insertion (324.105, self-holding).
9. Advance the screw until the head is fully seated in the plate and the plate is lagged to the bone.
10. Confirm screw position with intraoperative imaging.

**A5 - Repeat and complete:**
11. Repeat for all remaining screw holes.

### Option B: Variable-Angle Cancellous Screw (No Drilling)

**B1 - Create pilot hole:**
1. Use the screw itself as a self-drilling cancellous screw. No separate pilot hole needed for softer bone.

**B2-B5:** Same as A4-A5 above.

### Option C: Fixed-Angle Self-Tapping Screw (Drill Technique)

**C1 - Drill pilot hole:**
1. Insert the Drill Guide (03.600.002) fully into the desired hole for the correct fixed-angle trajectory.
2. Insert the Drill Bit and drill to the desired depth.
3. Use intraoperative imaging to verify the drilling.
4. Remove drill guide and bit.

**C2 - Insert screw:**
5. Load a fixed-angle self-tapping screw onto the Screwdriver for Insertion.
6. Advance the screw until fully seated and the plate is lagged to bone.
7. Verify screw position with imaging.

**C3 - Repeat.**

### Option D: Fixed-Angle Self-Tapping Screw (No Drilling)

**D1 - Create pilot hole:**
1. The self-tapping screw can be inserted directly without a pilot hole in softer bone.

**D2-D3:** Same insertion and verification as above.

### For All Options - Tapping Dense Bone
- Dense bone may be pre-tapped using the Tap for 4.0mm or 4.5mm cancellous screws before screw insertion.

### Implant Removal

**Step 1 - Clean screw head:**
1. If access to the screw head is blocked by tissue, use the Cleaning Instrument for Screw Head (324.071). Insert into the screw head and twist back and forth until material is removed.

**Step 2 - Remove screw:**
2. Use the Screwdriver for Extraction (352.311). Insert the driver shaft into the screw head recess. Tighten the knob on the handle to thread the inner shaft tip into the screw's mating thread. Advance the sleeve downward to contact the plate surface by turning clockwise.
3. **Do not rotate the sleeve after it has contacted the plate surface.** While holding the sleeve, turn the handle counterclockwise to extract the screw.

**Step 3 - Remove plate:**
4. After all screws are removed, remove the plate.

**Precautions for removal:**
- After the second screw insertion trial, the plate needs to be replaced.
- The extraction screw driver should only be used for screw removal; use for insertion may lead to driver and/or implant breakage.
- If the inner shaft knob is not fully tightened to the handle, breakage of the driver may occur.

## Pearls / Troubleshooting

- **The 4.5mm screw is an emergency screw** for when the 4.0mm has stripped the bone and a larger thread is needed. Keep them available as a rescue option.
- **Screws should remain in the vertebral body.** The intervertebral discs in the neck region are slightly inclined anterocaudal to posterocranial. Ensure enough space between the intact adjacent discs and the screws.
- **Intraoperative imaging is essential** to verify screw position. Use fluoroscopy after each screw or after all screws.
- **For long spans or poor bone quality**, consider screws longer than 16mm and/or posterior fixation for inherently unstable constructs.
- **The extraction screwdriver (352.311) is specifically for removal.** Do not use it for insertion. It has a different mechanism (threaded inner shaft) that can break if used for screw driving.
- **The Drill Guide gives the correct trajectory** for fixed-angle screws. For variable-angle screws, the Drill and Screw Guide (03.613.001) provides the appropriate trajectory range.
- **Dense bone should be tapped** before screw insertion to avoid screw fracture or poor seating.
- **Plate should not overhang adjacent disc spaces** to minimize risk of adjacent-level ossification and dysphagia.
