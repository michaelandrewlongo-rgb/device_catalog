# Prestige LP Cervical Disc (Medtronic)

**Manufacturer:** Medtronic Spine
**Category:** cervical-disc

---

## What It Is

The Prestige LP is a metal-on-metal artificial cervical disc designed to replace a degenerated cervical disc while preserving segmental motion. The device consists of two titanium ceramic composite (TCC) endplates with a ball-and-trough articulation. The superior component contains the trough and the inferior component contains the ball. "LP" stands for low profile, referring to the reduced anterior profile compared to the earlier Prestige ST design. The low-profile anterior flange allows placement with minimal anterior overhang, reducing the risk of dysphagia. The device is implanted via a standard anterior cervical approach after discectomy, occupying the disc space and articulating to maintain flexion-extension, lateral bending, and axial rotation. Each endplate has rails on the bone-contacting surface that press-fit into prepared vertebral body channels to achieve primary fixation; bone ongrowth provides long-term fixation. No screws or supplemental plate required. FDA PMA approved for single-level use (2007) and two adjacent-level use (2016) at C3-C7.

## Sizing/Specs

### Endplate Footprints
- Small, Medium, and Large footprint options
- Footprint selected intraoperatively using trial implants to match vertebral body width and depth

### Heights
- Multiple height options to restore appropriate disc space height
- Height determined intraoperatively with trials after discectomy and endplate preparation

### Materials
- **Endplates:** Titanium ceramic composite (TCC) -- a proprietary titanium alloy with ceramic surface treatment
- **Articulation:** Metal-on-metal ball-and-trough
- **Rail fixation:** Integrated rails on endplate bone-contacting surfaces (no screws)

### Motion
- Allows flexion-extension, lateral bending, and axial rotation
- Semi-constrained design: the ball-and-trough articulation permits controlled motion in all planes

### MRI Compatibility
- MRI conditional at 3.0 Tesla or less under specified conditions
- Produces localized artifact on MRI (metal-on-metal device); CT provides better bony detail at the index level

### Radiopacity
- Fully radiopaque (titanium alloy); visible on plain radiographs and fluoroscopy
- AP and lateral radiographs used to assess implant position and motion on flexion-extension views

## Indications

- Reconstruction of the cervical disc at a single level or two contiguous levels from C3-C7
- Skeletally mature patients with symptomatic cervical disc disease (intractable radiculopathy and/or myelopathy)
- Disc disease confirmed by imaging (CT, MRI, or myelography) showing disc herniation or osteophyte formation producing symptomatic nerve root or spinal cord compression
- Patients who have failed at least 6 weeks of conservative (non-operative) treatment or who have progressive neurological deterioration

## Contraindications

- Active systemic or local infection at the operative site
- Osteoporosis or osteopenia sufficient to compromise implant fixation
- Known allergy or sensitivity to titanium, ceramic, or any implant material
- Prior fusion at the target level
- Significant cervical facet joint disease or degeneration at the target level
- Cervical instability: translation greater than 3mm or angulation greater than 11 degrees on dynamic radiographs at the target level
- Severe spondylosis at the target level (bridging osteophytes, complete disc height loss, absence of motion)
- More than two levels of symptomatic cervical disc disease requiring surgical treatment
- Skeletal immaturity
- Active malignancy or systemic disease that would compromise implant survival

## Compatible With

- **Approach:** Standard Smith-Robinson anterior cervical approach (no specialized retractor system required beyond typical anterior cervical instruments)
- **Discectomy instruments:** Standard anterior cervical discectomy instruments (curettes, rongeurs, burr)
- **Endplate preparation:** Prestige LP dedicated instrument set includes cutting guides and chisels for rail channel preparation
- **Trials:** Prestige LP trial implants for sizing (footprint and height)
- **Imaging:** Intraoperative AP and lateral fluoroscopy for positioning; postoperative flexion-extension radiographs for motion assessment
- **No supplemental fixation required:** The device achieves primary fixation via press-fit rails; no anterior plate, screws, or posterior fixation needed
- **Adjacent-level compatibility:** Can be implanted at two contiguous levels (e.g., C4-5 and C5-6) per the two-level PMA approval

## Use Notes

- The device is a motion-preserving alternative to ACDF. The primary theoretical advantage is maintenance of segmental motion, which may reduce the incidence of symptomatic adjacent segment disease compared to fusion.
- Proper endplate preparation is critical. The vertebral body endplate must be prepared with rail channels using the dedicated cutting guides and chisels. The rails provide rotational and translational stability without screws.
- Do not use in patients with significant facet arthropathy at the index level. The facet joints must be functional to share load with the artificial disc.
- Patient selection matters. Ideal candidates have single- or two-level soft disc herniations with radiculopathy, intact posterior elements, no significant kyphosis, and no instability.
- Metal-on-metal bearing produces minimal particulate debris. Long-term data from the IDE trial and post-approval studies extend beyond 10 years with maintained motion and low reoperation rates.
- Serum metal ion monitoring is not routinely recommended but has been studied. Titanium and vanadium levels remain within normal ranges in published cohorts.
- The low-profile anterior flange reduces but does not eliminate the risk of dysphagia. The device still has more anterior profile than a zero-profile interbody cage.
- Postoperative motion can be assessed on flexion-extension lateral radiographs. A functional range of motion greater than 2 degrees at the index level indicates a mobile segment (not heterotopic ossification-locked).
- Heterotopic ossification (HO) can occur and may limit or eliminate motion over time. Published rates of high-grade HO (Grade 3-4) vary from 5-25% depending on follow-up duration and grading system.
- Two-level arthroplasty with the Prestige LP was shown non-inferior to two-level ACDF in the FDA IDE trial, leading to the 2016 PMA supplement approval.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **vs. Mobi-C (Zimmer Biomet):** Mobi-C uses a cobalt-chromium-on-UHMWPE bearing (metal-on-poly) with a mobile-bearing design (free-floating polyethylene core between two endplates). Prestige LP is metal-on-metal with a fixed ball-and-trough. Mobi-C is also FDA-approved for one- and two-level use. Mobi-C has keeled endplates for fixation; Prestige LP uses rail channels. The mobile bearing in Mobi-C theoretically allows more physiologic coupled motion but introduces a polyethylene wear surface. Prestige LP avoids polyethylene but produces metal wear debris.
- **vs. ProDisc-C Vivo (DePuy Synthes):** ProDisc-C Vivo uses a cobalt-chromium-on-UHMWPE bearing with a ball-and-socket design (fixed bearing). It has a keel and screw fixation to the vertebral bodies. Prestige LP relies on press-fit rails without screws. ProDisc-C Vivo is FDA-approved for single-level use only. The ball-and-socket in ProDisc-C creates a fixed center of rotation; the ball-and-trough in Prestige LP allows a variable center of rotation.
- **vs. M6-C (Orthofix):** M6-C is a viscoelastic artificial disc with a polyethylene-sheathed artificial nucleus and woven fiber annulus, designed to mimic the biomechanics of a natural disc including shock absorption and coupled motion. It uses no metal-on-metal articulation. M6-C has a compressible core that allows axial compression; Prestige LP does not. M6-C is FDA-approved for single-level use. M6-C has a lower MRI artifact profile.
- **vs. Secure-C (Globus Medical):** Secure-C is a cobalt-chromium metal-on-metal disc with a ball-and-socket articulation and self-deploying keels for fixation. FDA-approved for single-level use. Both devices are metal-on-metal, but the articulation geometry differs (ball-and-socket vs. ball-and-trough). Secure-C uses keels rather than rails for primary fixation.
- **vs. ACDF (fusion):** ACDF eliminates motion at the treated level and provides immediate rigid fixation. Prestige LP preserves motion, potentially protecting adjacent segments from accelerated degeneration. ACDF is a well-established procedure with broader indications (trauma, tumor, multilevel disease, instability). Arthroplasty requires intact posterior elements and functional facet joints. ACDF has no bearing-surface wear concerns.
