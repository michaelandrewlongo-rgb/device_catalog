# CoRoent System -- NuVasive

**Manufacturer:** NuVasive (now part of Globus Medical)
**Category:** Interbody Cage (Lateral/XLIF, Anterior, Posterior)

## What It Is

A family of interbody fusion cages designed for multiple surgical approaches to lumbar interbody fusion. The CoRoent system includes several cage variants:

- **CoRoent XL:** A large-footprint PEEK-OPTIMA lateral interbody cage designed specifically for the XLIF (extreme lateral interbody fusion) transpsoas approach. Radiolucent with tantalum markers for visualization.
- **CoRoent XL-T:** A 3D-printed porous titanium version of the lateral cage designed for osseointegration via the XLIF approach.
- **CoRoent STALIF (standalone anterior lumbar interbody fusion):** An anterior lumbar interbody cage with integrated fixation, designed for standalone use without supplemental posterior fixation in select cases.

The CoRoent platform was developed alongside NuVasive's XLIF technique and is one of the most widely used lateral interbody cage systems. The lateral cages feature large footprints that span the dense apophyseal ring of the vertebral endplates, providing broad load distribution and coronal plane correction.

## Sizing / Specs

- **Material (CoRoent XL):** PEEK-OPTIMA (radiolucent); tantalum markers for fluoroscopic visualization
- **Material (CoRoent XL-T):** 3D-printed porous titanium alloy for osseointegration
- **Material (CoRoent STALIF):** PEEK-OPTIMA with integrated titanium fixation
- **Footprint (lateral cages):** Large rectangular footprint designed to span the vertebral endplate from apophyseal ring to apophyseal ring in the coronal plane
- **Heights:** Wide range of height options (typically 8mm to 18mm+); refer to surgical technique guide for complete sizing matrix
- **Lengths:** Multiple length options for AP endplate coverage
- **Widths:** Multiple width options for coronal endplate coverage (lateral cages are significantly wider than TLIF/PLIF cages)
- **Lordotic angles:** 0-degree, 6-degree, and 10-degree options available (size-dependent)
- **Graft window:** Open central chamber for bone graft packing
- **Approach (XL/XL-T):** Lateral transpsoas (XLIF)
- **Approach (STALIF):** Anterior (ALIF)
- **Levels:** L1-L5 (lateral); L4-S1 (anterior, STALIF); L5-S1 typically requires anterior or posterior approach due to iliac crest obstruction of lateral corridor

## Indications

- Degenerative disc disease (DDD) requiring lumbar interbody fusion
- Lumbar spondylolisthesis (up to Grade 2 for lateral approach in select cases)
- Degenerative scoliosis requiring coronal and/or sagittal correction
- Adjacent segment disease
- Pseudarthrosis or failed prior fusion
- Skeletally mature patients who have failed conservative treatment
- Lateral cages (XL/XL-T): lateral transpsoas approach, L1-L5 (L4-5 may require anterior-to-psoas approach depending on anatomy)
- STALIF: standalone anterior approach at L4-S1 in select cases with intact posterior elements

Lateral cages may be used without supplemental posterior fixation in select stable cases (e.g., stable degenerative disc disease, Grade 1 spondylolisthesis with intact posterior tension band). Most cases with instability, deformity correction, or multilevel fusion require supplemental posterior fixation.

## Contraindications

- Active infection at the operative site (local or systemic)
- Severe osteoporosis or osteopenia that would preclude adequate endplate support
- Known allergy or sensitivity to implant materials (PEEK, titanium)
- Inadequate bone stock, bone quality, or anatomy to support the implant
- Conditions that preclude the potential benefit of spinal fusion
- Retroperitoneal pathology or anatomy precluding safe lateral or anterior access (lateral approach)
- Prior retroperitoneal surgery with extensive scarring (relative, lateral approach)
- Spondylolisthesis grade 3 or higher
- Vascular anatomy precluding safe anterior access (STALIF)
- L5-S1 via lateral approach (iliac crest obstruction in most patients)

## Compatible With

- **Lateral retractor system:** NuVasive MaXcess integrated split-blade retractor system with real-time EMG neuromonitoring (XLIF approach)
- **Neuromonitoring:** NuVasive NVM5 or NVISION neuromonitoring platform with directional EMG for safe transpsoas corridor navigation
- **Pedicle screw systems:** NuVasive Reline posterior fixation system; Globus Medical posterior fixation systems; compatible with standard 5.5mm rod systems when supplemental fixation is required
- **Lateral plate:** NuVasive lateral plate fixation for additional stability without posterior fixation in select cases
- **Bone graft:** Autograft, allograft, bone graft substitutes (e.g., NuVasive Attrax putty, demineralized bone matrix) packed through the open graft chamber
- **Navigation:** Compatible with NuVasive Pulse navigation platform; tantalum markers (PEEK versions) or titanium body (XL-T) visible on fluoroscopy and CT-based navigation

## Use Notes

- The XLIF approach is a lateral transpsoas corridor. The patient is positioned in a lateral decubitus position. Real-time directional EMG neuromonitoring is essential to avoid lumbar plexus injury during psoas traversal.
- The large lateral cage footprint sits on the dense apophyseal ring bilaterally, which provides structural support and reduces subsidence risk compared to smaller TLIF/PLIF cages that load the weaker central endplate.
- Lateral cages provide powerful coronal plane correction for degenerative scoliosis. Lordotic cage options (6-degree, 10-degree) contribute to sagittal correction, but standalone lateral cages are limited in their ability to restore lumbar lordosis compared to ALIF or pedicle subtraction osteotomy.
- The PEEK construction (CoRoent XL) is radiolucent, allowing straightforward fusion assessment on plain lateral radiographs. The 3D-printed titanium version (XL-T) prioritizes osseointegration but limits radiographic fusion assessment on plain films.
- Standalone lateral fusion (without posterior fixation) is appropriate in select cases: stable single-level degenerative disc disease with intact posterior elements, minimal instability. Most surgeons supplement with posterior pedicle screws for spondylolisthesis, multilevel constructs, or deformity correction.
- L4-5 lateral access may be challenging due to the iliac crest and the position of the lumbar plexus. An anterior-to-psoas (ATP/oblique lateral, OLIF) approach may be needed at this level.
- L5-S1 cannot be accessed laterally in most patients due to the iliac crest. Use an anterior (ALIF) or posterior (TLIF/PLIF) approach at L5-S1.
- Endplate preparation is critical. Use trial instruments and rasps to prepare the endplates while preserving the bony endplate integrity. Endplate violation is the primary risk factor for cage subsidence.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **vs TLIF cages (Globus RISE, Medtronic Elevate, Stryker Tritanium TL, DePuy CONCORDE LIFT):** CoRoent lateral cages have a fundamentally different approach corridor (lateral transpsoas vs posterior). Lateral cages provide a much larger footprint spanning the apophyseal ring, offer powerful coronal correction, and avoid posterior muscle dissection. TLIF cages are posterior, smaller footprint, and do not require psoas traversal or neuromonitoring.
- **vs Medtronic Clydesdale lateral cage:** Both are lateral PEEK cages for XLIF/DLIF. Clydesdale has a similar large footprint. CoRoent XL-T (3D-printed titanium) offers an osseointegration option that Clydesdale PEEK does not. Platform integration differs: CoRoent integrates with NuVasive MaXcess retractor and NVM5 neuromonitoring; Clydesdale integrates with Medtronic systems.
- **PEEK (CoRoent XL) vs titanium (CoRoent XL-T):** PEEK is radiolucent for easier fusion assessment on plain films. Titanium XL-T provides porous osseointegration surface for bone ingrowth. Titanium has higher modulus than PEEK; the porous structure reduces effective modulus. Clinical preference varies by surgeon.
- **Standalone lateral vs supplemental posterior fixation:** CoRoent lateral cages can be used standalone in select cases, avoiding a second incision for posterior fixation. This is a potential advantage over TLIF cages, which always require posterior screws. However, standalone use is limited to stable pathology and single-level disease in most practices.
- **vs expandable lateral cages:** CoRoent XL and XL-T are static cages requiring disc space preparation and trialing to the target height. Some newer lateral cages (e.g., Globus ELSA) offer in situ expansion. Static cages are mechanically simpler with fewer failure modes.
