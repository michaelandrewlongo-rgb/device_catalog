# Tritanium TL Posterior Lumbar Cage -- Stryker

**Manufacturer:** Stryker
**Category:** Interbody Cage (TLIF)

## What It Is

A 3D-printed titanium interbody fusion cage designed for transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF). The Tritanium TL uses Stryker's proprietary Tritanium porous titanium technology, featuring a randomized porous structure engineered to mimic the porosity and modulus of cancellous bone, promoting osseointegration and biological fixation. The cage has a banana/kidney-shaped footprint designed for unilateral insertion through Kambin's triangle. It features an open central graft window for bone packing, toothed superior and inferior surfaces for endplate engagement, and radiopaque markers for fluoroscopic visualization. The Tritanium TL is a static (non-expandable) cage that requires supplemental posterior pedicle screw fixation.

## Sizing / Specs

- **Material:** 3D-printed porous titanium alloy (Tritanium technology)
- **Footprint:** Banana/kidney-shaped for unilateral TLIF insertion
- **Heights:** Multiple options; refer to surgical technique guide for complete sizing matrix
- **Lengths:** Multiple options to match vertebral endplate coverage requirements
- **Lordotic angles:** Multiple lordotic angle options to address sagittal alignment
- **Graft window:** Open central window for autograft, allograft, or bone graft substitute packing
- **Surface:** Randomized porous titanium structure on endplate-contacting surfaces; designed to mimic cancellous bone architecture
- **Radiopaque markers:** Tantalum markers for AP and lateral fluoroscopic visualization
- **Approach:** Designed for TLIF (transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion), unilateral posterior insertion
- **Levels:** L2-S1

## Indications

- Degenerative disc disease (DDD) at one or two contiguous levels from L2 to S1
- Lumbar spondylolisthesis (up to Grade 1)
- Lumbar spinal stenosis with instability requiring fusion
- Skeletally mature patients who have failed conservative treatment
- Must be used with supplemental posterior fixation (pedicle screws and rods) and bone graft

## Contraindications

- Active infection at the operative site (local or systemic)
- Severe osteoporosis or osteopenia that would preclude adequate endplate support
- Known allergy or sensitivity to titanium
- Inadequate bone stock, bone quality, or anatomy to support the implant
- Conditions that preclude the potential benefit of spinal fusion
- Spondylolisthesis grade 3 or higher (relative)
- Morbid obesity or conditions preventing adequate surgical exposure
- Patient non-compliance with postoperative restrictions

## Compatible With

- **Pedicle screw systems:** Stryker Xia, Serrato, ES2 posterior fixation systems; compatible with standard 5.5mm rod systems from other manufacturers
- **Retractor systems:** Compatible with standard MIS tubular retractor systems for MIS TLIF approach
- **Bone graft:** Autograft (local laminectomy bone, iliac crest), allograft, bone graft substitutes, and biologics packed through the open graft window
- **Navigation:** Stryker NAV3i platform with fluoroscopy or CT-based navigation; tantalum markers visible on standard fluoroscopy
- **Same surface technology as:** Stryker Tritanium C (cervical cage), Tritanium PL (PLIF cage) -- same 3D-printed porous titanium platform across the Tritanium product family

## Use Notes

- The Tritanium TL is a static cage. Unlike expandable TLIF cages (e.g., Globus RISE, Medtronic Elevate), the disc space must be distracted to the target height before cage insertion. Sequential trialing is required.
- The kidney-shaped footprint allows insertion through a unilateral transforaminal corridor. The curved geometry maximizes endplate coverage once rotated into final position.
- The 3D-printed porous titanium surface is the primary differentiator. The randomized porosity is designed to allow bone ingrowth through the structure rather than relying solely on ongrowth, potentially improving fusion rates compared to smooth PEEK or machined titanium.
- Pack bone graft into the central graft window before insertion. Additional graft can be placed in the disc space anteriorly before cage insertion.
- Confirm cage position on AP and lateral fluoroscopy before completing posterior fixation. Tantalum markers delineate the cage boundaries.
- The titanium construction means the cage is not radiolucent. Postoperative CT is more reliable than plain radiographs for assessing fusion through and around the implant. MRI is possible but will have local artifact.
- Standard TLIF approach: prone positioning, facetectomy, discectomy, endplate preparation, sequential trialing, cage insertion, posterior fixation.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **vs PEEK cages (e.g., NuVasive CoRoent, generic PEEK TLIF cages):** Tritanium TL's 3D-printed porous titanium is designed for osseointegration (bone ingrowth into the porous structure). PEEK is radiolucent, which simplifies fusion assessment on plain radiographs, but PEEK does not support bone ingrowth. Titanium modulus is higher than PEEK; the porous structure is intended to reduce effective modulus closer to bone.
- **vs expandable TLIF cages (Globus RISE, Medtronic Elevate, DePuy CONCORDE LIFT):** Tritanium TL is static and requires pre-distraction and trialing. Expandable cages insert at a reduced profile and expand in situ, reducing nerve root retraction. However, expandable cages have moving parts (more mechanical complexity), typically lack the porous osseointegration surface, and cost more.
- **vs Medtronic Elevate:** Elevate is expandable with PEEK/titanium hybrid construction. Tritanium TL is static, all-titanium, with a porous osseointegration surface. Different philosophies: Elevate optimizes insertion mechanics; Tritanium TL optimizes biological fixation.
- **vs Globus RISE:** RISE is expandable, all-titanium, with toothed endplates but not a 3D-printed porous structure. RISE allows in situ height adjustment; Tritanium TL requires accurate pre-sizing.
- **vs lateral cages (NuVasive CoRoent XL):** Tritanium TL is a posterior TLIF cage with a smaller footprint. Lateral cages have much larger footprints and are inserted via transpsoas approach. Lateral approach avoids posterior dissection but has different complication profiles (psoas injury, lumbar plexus).
