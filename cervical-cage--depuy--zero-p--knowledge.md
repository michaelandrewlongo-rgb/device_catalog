# Zero-P Anterior Cervical Fusion Device (DePuy Synthes)

## What It Is

The Zero-P is a zero-profile anterior cervical interbody fusion device that combines a PEEK-OPTIMA spacer with an integrated titanium alloy anterior fixation plate. Unlike traditional ACDF constructs (separate cage + anterior plate), the Zero-P sits entirely within the disc space with no anterior plate profile. This eliminates plate-related dysphagia and adjacent-segment ossification risks. The device uses four 3.0mm titanium cervical spine locking screws that angle 40 degrees caudally/cranially and 2.5 degrees medially/laterally, locking into the integrated plate with a StarDrive T8 interface. The screws lock at 1.2 Nm using a torque-limiting attachment.

## Indications

- Anterior cervical interbody fusion (ACDF)
- Single-level or multi-level cervical disc disease
- Cervical degenerative disc disease
- Cervical spondylosis
- Cervical radiculopathy/myelopathy
- Trauma
- Tumor
- Same indications as a standard ACDF with anterior plating, but without the plate

## Contraindications

- Active infection at the operative site
- Severe osteoporosis or inadequate bone stock (risk of screw pullout and subsidence)
- Known allergy to PEEK, titanium, or other implant materials
- Conditions precluding benefit of spinal implant surgery (e.g., irreversible neurological deficit not attributable to compression)
- Previously fused adjacent levels where the construct would span the fusion
- Skeletal immaturity
- Mental or neuromuscular disorders creating unacceptable risk of fixation failure or noncompliance

## Compatible With

- **Bone graft:** Autogenous cancellous bone, allograft bone, or bone graft substitutes packed into the PEEK spacer window
- **Imaging:** Standard AP and lateral fluoroscopy for intraoperative positioning; CT for postoperative fusion assessment (PEEK spacer is radiolucent, titanium markers visible)
- **Cervical retractor systems:** Standard anterior cervical retractor setups (e.g., Caspar-type distraction pins, self-retaining retractors)
- **Distraction systems:** Caspar distraction pins or vertebral body spreaders for disc space preparation
- **No anterior plate required:** The Zero-P integrates fixation into the spacer, eliminating the need for a separate anterior cervical plate
- **Dedicated instrument set:** Requires DePuy Synthes Zero-P instrument tray (Implant Holder, Drill Guides, Aiming Device, StarDrive screwdrivers, Torque Limiting Attachment 1.2 Nm)

## Use Notes

- The Zero-P sits entirely within the disc space with no anterior profile. This is its primary clinical advantage: reduced plate-related dysphagia compared to traditional cage-plus-plate constructs.
- Screw trajectory is fixed at 40 degrees caudal/cranial by the integrated plate geometry. This is not adjustable. Proper implant positioning is critical to ensure screws remain within the vertebral body.
- All four screws must be placed before final tightening to avoid shifting the implant during sequential tightening.
- The 1.2 Nm torque-limiting attachment must be used for all screw tightening. Failure to use it risks screwdriver breakage.
- Available in convex, lordotic, and parallel endplate geometries. Select based on patient endplate anatomy and desired segmental lordosis.
- Graft packing before insertion is essential. Use the dedicated Packing Block and Cancellous Bone Impactor.
- The device is cleared for single-level and multi-level ACDF. However, most published data supports single-level and two-level use.
- Smokers should be counseled regarding increased nonunion risk.

## Key Differences vs Competitors

- **vs. Tritanium C (Stryker):** The Zero-P is a zero-profile integrated cage-plate (PEEK spacer + titanium screws, no anterior plate overhang). The Tritanium C is a standalone titanium cage requiring a separate anterior plate. Zero-P reduces dysphagia risk by eliminating plate profile. Tritanium C offers 3D-printed porous titanium for osseointegration but creates more artifact on CT/MRI.
- **vs. CAPSTONE (Medtronic):** The CAPSTONE is a standalone PEEK cage requiring supplemental anterior plating. Zero-P eliminates the plate entirely. CAPSTONE offers cleaner radiolucent imaging of the graft window; Zero-P has titanium plate/screw hardware that partially obscures the fusion zone on plain films but not on CT.
- **vs. COALITION (Globus):** The COALITION is a 3D-printed titanium cage with optional integrated fixation screws. Both devices aim to reduce or eliminate the anterior plate. Zero-P uses PEEK for the spacer body (radiolucent, less stiff); COALITION uses porous titanium (stiffer, promotes bone through-growth, but more imaging artifact).
- **vs. Traditional cage + plate:** Zero-P simplifies the construct to a single implant, reduces surgical steps (no plate bending/contouring), and lowers dysphagia rates. The tradeoff is fixed screw angulation (40 degrees) with no variable-angle option, limiting flexibility in challenging anatomy.

## Relevant Setup

### Pre-operative Planning
- Determine spacer shape (convex, lordotic, or parallel) based on endplate anatomy and desired sagittal alignment
- Select height based on disc space measurement
- Standard vs. Large footprint based on vertebral body width

### Disc Space Preparation
- Complete standard anterior cervical discectomy
- Prepare endplates (remove cartilaginous endplate, preserve subchondral bone)
- Size the disc space with trial spacers

### Graft Packing
- Pack the PEEK spacer with cancellous autograft or bone graft substitute using the Packing Block (03.617.984) before insertion
- Use the Cancellous Bone Impactor (03.617.970) to firmly pack graft

## Use Step-by-Step

### Discectomy and Sizing
1. Perform standard anterior cervical discectomy with endplate preparation.
2. Select the trial spacer (parallel, lordotic, or convex; standard or large) and insert to determine appropriate height. The trial should fit snugly with mild distraction.
3. Confirm sizing with lateral fluoroscopy.

### Implant Preparation
4. Select the definitive Zero-P implant matching the trial (shape, size, height).
5. Pack the spacer with bone graft using the Packing Block and Cancellous Bone Impactor.

### Implant Insertion
6. Grasp the packed implant with the Implant Holder (03.617.980).
7. Insert the implant into the disc space. The plate faces anteriorly and sits flush with or slightly recessed from the anterior vertebral body edge.
8. Impact gently using the Impactor (flat tip 03.617.981 or ball tip 03.617.982) if needed to fully seat.
9. Confirm position with AP and lateral fluoroscopy. The implant should be centered, properly seated in height, and the plate flush with the anterior vertebral body.

### Screw Fixation - Option A: Drill Guide and Freehand Screw
10. Attach the Drill Guide with handle (03.617.962) to the implant plate.
11. Select the appropriate drill bit (2.0mm, with 12/14/16mm stop corresponding to screw length).
12. Drill the pilot hole through the drill guide. The drill stops at the correct depth when the stop contacts the guide.
13. Verify drill position with fluoroscopy.
14. Remove drill guide and bit.
15. Assemble the screwdriver: attach Torque Limiting Attachment (1.2 Nm) to the StarDrive Screwdriver Shaft (T8), connect to the Handle with quick coupling.
16. Load the screw (12, 14, or 16mm) onto the self-retaining screwdriver. The Holding Sleeve may be used for additional screw retention.
17. Advance the screw until the head contacts the plate.
18. Verify screw position with imaging.
19. Repeat for all 4 screws. Tighten screws only after all are inserted.

### Screw Fixation - Option B: Drill Guide and Freehand Screw (simplified)
Same as Option A with minor instrument variations.

### Screw Fixation - Option C: Threaded Drill Guide and Freehand Screw
20. Insert the Threaded Drill Guide (03.617.968) into the screw hole at the appropriate angle. The tip fits into the screw hole to produce the correct 40-degree angle.
21. Drill with the 2.0mm drill bit through the threaded guide.
22. Remove drill bit and threaded guide.
23. Insert screw as above.
24. Drill bits are color-coded by ring corresponding to screw length. When the ring is flush with the drill guide top, the correct depth is reached.

### Screw Fixation - Option D: Awl and Freehand Screw
25. Use the 2.0mm Awl with sleeve (03.617.990) to create pilot holes. Insert at 40-degree angulation into the screw hole, push down while twisting.
26. Verify awl position with fluoroscopy.
27. Remove awl, maintaining plate alignment.
28. Insert screws as above.

### Screw Fixation - Option E: Angled Instruments
29. For difficult anatomy where straight instruments cannot reach, use the 2.0mm Angled Awl (03.617.993) with Slotted Mallet (03.820.113) to create pilot holes.
30. Use the Angled StarDrive Screwdriver (03.617.900) to insert and tighten screws.

### Final Tightening
31. After all 4 screws are placed, tighten each screw to the recommended **1.2 Nm** using the Torque Limiting Attachment.
32. If the torque-limiting attachment is not used, breakage of the driver may occur. Always use it.
33. Screws placed using the surgical technique may not always be flush with the plate but will be sufficiently locked when 1.2 Nm torque is achieved.

## Pearls / Troubleshooting

- **Always use the 1.2 Nm Torque Limiting Attachment.** Without it, screwdriver breakage may occur. The attachment prevents over-torquing.
- **Tighten screws only after ALL are inserted.** Individual screw tightening before all are placed can shift the implant position.
- **Lateral screws should always point medially** (2.5 degrees medial angulation). This is a consistent rule across all fixation options.
- **40-degree screw angulation is critical.** The plate guides enforce this angle when the drill guide or awl tip is seated in the screw hole. Freehand screw insertion without guide risks improper trajectory.
- **Zero-profile advantage:** The plate sits within the disc space, not on the anterior vertebral body surface. This eliminates plate overhang and reduces dysphagia risk compared to traditional anterior cervical plates.
- **Screws may not sit perfectly flush** with the plate surface. This is normal. The locking mechanism engages at 1.2 Nm regardless of screw-plate flush.
- **For particularly hard bone, drill rather than awl.** The awl technique works well in softer cancellous bone, but drilling is recommended to minimize implant movement in dense cortical bone.
- **Take care that the awl does not move the implant** relative to the vertebral body during pilot hole creation. Stabilize the implant manually or with the aiming device.
- **Conical Extraction Screw (03.617.971S) is the rescue for stripped screw recesses.** Pre-drill the screw recess with the 2.0mm drill bit, then insert the conical extraction screw counterclockwise to grasp and extract. Single-use only. Do not use with power tools or torque-limiting attachment.
- **For implant removal:** First loosen all screws before removing any. This keeps the implant secured during the removal process. Use distraction at the disc space to facilitate implant extraction. Use the Aiming Device (03.617.963) for implant extraction.
